LANCASHIRE COUNTY CRICKET CLUB LIMITED

Chairman's Statement

Year Ended 31 December 2024

As we reflect on our Financial Performance for 2024, | would like to take the opportunity to look back on some of the key areas which defined Lancashire
Cricket’s year across the organisation.

Specifically, | will reflect on the on-field performances of our men’s and women’s squads during the 2024 season, provide further insight into The Hundred
investment process, review the year for our Emirates Old Trafford venue - and finally | will take a look at our ongoing commercial focus across the Club.

| would like to take this opportunity to thank our growing Membership base for your ongoing support and commitment to the Club during 2024 - and as we look
ahead to the 2025 season.

Cricket Performance in 2024

As | have stated before, the 2024 season was disappointing across the board for men’s and women’s cricket at Lancashire. As a Board, we apologise to our
Members and supporters. Since the end of last season and throughout the winter months, everyone at the Club, especially our cricket departments, have
worked hard in order to understand the issues of last season and to ensure we are focusing on material improvements.

For our men’s squad, the clear focus is on an immediate return to Division One of the Rothesay County Championship, at the first time of asking. In T20 cricket,
qualification from the North Group of the Vitality Blast is always expected by the Club, with a clear aim to reach Finals Day. In the Metro Bank One Day Cup, a
young squad will be pushed to perform more successfully in 50-over cricket following a tough, but valuable, learning experience last summer.

In terms of investment around our men’s cricket department, we know that we need to spend as close to the salary cap as possible and we are looking to
increase men’s cricket spend, where possible. The winter has seen the addition of Michael Jones from Durham plus overseas signings of Marcus Harris,
Ashton Turner, Chris Green, and Anderson Phillip. We are also delighted to have James Anderson committed to a number of games across the Rothesay
County Championship and Vitality Blast, following his retirement from international cricket.

Further investments have been made into the cricket department as shown by our captain Keaton Jennings signing a landmark five-year contract to commit his
long-term future to the Club, with Matty Hurst, one of the breakthrough stars from last season, also signing a new and improved contract. Our women’s cricket
team now has 21 players in total, the joint-most contracted players in the country and the most we have ever had ourselves as a county.

That brings me on to last summer for Lancashire Thunder (now Lancashire Cricket Women). Expectations were high for the side in 2024, especially after
reaching T20 Finals Day in 2023. Sadly, we fell short in both major competitions and never quite mounted serious challenges to quality from either group stages
of the Rachael Heyhoe Flint Trophy (50-over) or the Charlotte Edwards Cup (T20).

Lancashire Women have now moved into the new Tier 1 structure for domestic women’s cricket in 2025, which brings them fully under the Lancashire Cricket
umbrella, and we are looking forward to seeing what the new season brings on the pitch for both of our Red Rose squads.

After investing into a young squad and bringing in quality additions during the winter, such as Eve Jones and Grace Potts from Central Sparks, we are now at
the stage where we need to see genuine progress from the side. We have a quality head coach in former Notts and England wicketkeeper Chris Read, and we
are expecting to challenge the best teams across both competitions in 2025.

The Hundred investment process into Manchester Originals

A key development over the last 12 months across English and Welsh cricket, has been the process of external investment into the eight teams in The
Hundred.

Following a long and detailed process, Lancashire Cricket were delighted to see RP Sanjiv Goenka Group (RPSG) commit their investment to Manchester
Originals. This marked a momentous occasion for both Lancashire Cricket and Manchester Originals, as we entered into an exclusivity period with RPSG - who
were always one of our preferred bidders throughout the process.

For us, the focus had always been on securing an established cricket partner who can work with both Lancashire Cricket and Manchester Originals within a joint
venture framework that can benefit all parties. Our CEO Daniel Gidney and Manchester Originals Chair James Sheridan worked incredibly hard to meet with
Indian Premier League (IPL) franchises and others, to determine their suitability and interest before selecting our preferred bidders. | then met with them once
we knew that they were committed to the process.

We were delighted with the deal that was struck with RPSG. As a Board, we decided to sell 70% of Manchester Originals (21% of our own share, plus ECB
49%) to ensure that the finances of Lancashire Cricket are on a sound footing for future generations.

RPSG have strong IPL expertise, as owners of the Lucknow Super Giants franchise, while they have since successfully expanded into other cricket leagues
such as the SA20 with Durban Super Giants. Together, our ambition is for Manchester Originals to become the stand out team in The Hundred - in terms of our
on-field performance, attendance at Emirates Old Trafford and commercial success.

In terms of the benefits for Lancashire Cricket, this investment presents a hugely exciting opportunity for the Club. Conversations are ongoing within the Board,
while we have also held consultations with Members and staff, in order to understand how the funds generated should best be used for the benefit of Lancashire
Cricket and its Members for generations to come.

LANCASHIRE COUNTY CRICKET CLUB LIMITED

Finance Report

Year Ended 31 December 2024

The Club is delighted to report turnover of £34.1m (2023 £36.5m). Very strong revenue from non-cricket activities included three nights of concerts and the full
year impact of running the extended hotel of 250 rooms. Cricketing income was lower than the prior year which included the Ashes test.

The Club reports an operating profit after exceptional items of £4.7m and a surplus before taxation of £0.2m.

Income and expenditure account

Cricket

Income from cricket was impacted by the combination of rainy weather and a high volume of competing sporting events in the summer calendar, with
attendances generally lower than in the prior year across both international and domestic games.

In August for the Sri Lanka test over 55,000 fans were welcomed to EOT, but with capacity for more this was a disappointing result in terms of ticket sales. The
IT20 against Australia sold well but was unfortunately rained off without a ball bowled. Our cancellation and abandonment insurance covered the majority of
the revenue but any opportunity to generate further income was lost.

Domestic cricket revenue during the year was similar to 2023. Weather impacted key matches in the season with the Roses fixture cancelled and the women’s
and men’s Hundred games against Oval Invincibles also impacted.

Venue

Hotel and conference and events revenues continued to grow during the year, increasing by 19% on 2023. The hotel extension was opened in October 2023
and so this is the first report to reflect a full year of trading 250 bedrooms. For the first time, hotel revenue has exceeded that of international cricket.

In such a rain impacted year the value of having sustainable alternative income to protect the Club has been very clear.

Expenditure

Expenditure has decreased by £1.3m (4%) compared to 2023, primarily due to the reduction in the cost of hosting international cricket as 2023 included the
Ashes.

The widely reported high inflation levels of both labour and food costs has impacted costs across the Club. In September the Club took the step of partnering
with Sodexo Live!, a decision made to give certainty on the cost of food delivery at the venue, and also to elevate the offering for our conference and events
and hospitality guests.

The surplus has been impacted by an adverse fair value movement on the interest cap instrument of £0.1m.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Capital Investment

Following the major capital investment in the hotel extension completing in 2023, capital projects during the year were minimal.

Cash Flow

Net cash inflow from Operating Activities in the year was £4.2m.

During the year capital repayments commenced on the additional Metro Bank facility that was drawn in 2023 to partly fund the hotel extension.

Interest paid has increased on the prior year due to the increase in debt towards the end of 2023 and increase in base rate. The main loan facility with Metro
Bank has an interest rate cap in place which offers protection when the base rate is 3% or higher, but the additional £8m loan does not have this protection
against interest rate rises due to the prohibitive current cost of purchasing interest rate caps.

Borrowings

The loan balances less unamortised finance costs stand at £31.3m at the end of 2024. The £8m Metro Bank loan facility transferred from interest only
repayment to capital and interest repayment from October 2024, 18 months after first drawdown. Ensuring the debt is serviced and all covenant requirements
met has been a focus for the Club in this initial period of maximum debt levels. All repayment and covenant requirements for 2024 have been met.

Upon receipt of proceeds from the sale of the Hundred Teams the borrowing position of the Club will be significantly reduced in 2025.

Subsequent events

In February 2025, the Club and ECB entered into exclusive negotiations with RPSG who have had an offer accepted to acquire a 49% stake in the Manchester
Originals Limited team. As part of the arrangement, the Club will be gifted the remaining 51% in Manchester Originals Limited by ECB. As at the balance sheet
date and the date these financial statements were approved, the exclusivity period was ongoing and the transactions had not yet completed. The Club has
also agreed to sell 21% of its gifted 51% to RPSG and this transaction will complete at the same time.

LANCASHIRE COUNTY CRICKET CLUB LIMITED

Strategic Report

Year Ended 31 December 2024

This year’s Strategic Report shows evidence of Lancashire Cricket coming through a challenging year, on and off the field, while also embedding the long-term
financial sustainability strategy that the Club has previously embarked upon.

Doubling down on this 365-day vision with a fully diversified business model, with multiple anticyclical revenue streams, has undoubtedly put the Club under short:
term pressure in terms of cash flow management. Such management has required a delicate, dynamic plan to bridge the cost of the new hotel extension whilst
the hotel trade reaches maturity. It will, however, fund the Club’s continued trading and management of it's P&L for many years to come.

The report focuses on mitigation and management of these time-based challenges during 2024 and then looks forward through 2025 and beyond.

Year ended 31 December 2024

As a business, 2024 saw the Club deal with multiple challenges. As we grapple with the global franchise market, contracting players now has never been more
nuanced nor complex. The landscape has changed fundamentally over the past ten years. The gradual dilution of “Non Objection Certificates” — where,
historically, the Club as primary employer could block a player taking part in an overseas tournament for another team, if it was not in the best interests of the
team or the player, has created a difficult environment for recruitment, retention and maximising playing time of men’s players. The Men’s team were relegated
from the County Championship. In mitigation, we had an average of six players under 23 years old. Due to a combination of retirements, injuries, England
availability issues and some players choosing to play abroad rather than play red ball cricket, meant that a lack of experience was to the fore. A very
disappointing 50-over campaign and a T20 quarter final loss made 2024 a season for the Club to forget and move on from.

Lancashire Thunder (Women) had a season of mixed results on the field but consolidated, with the elevation to Tier 1 County status in 2025 and having the
largest contracted squad in the country. Winning the U18s double, and with the girl’s pathway making significant strides forward, has meant that competition for
places is now fierce and 2025 is very much seen as the year to push on.

Emirates Old Trafford continued its century old tradition of hosting international cricket with a Sri Lanka Test Match, although ticket sales were significantly down
on forecast. People come to cricket based on the opposition rather than the experience of the game, which we learnt this during the Sri Lanka Test. We will
strengthen our digital experience making it more exciting and compelling which will help persuade fans to attend regardless of the opposition.

For the first time, our hotel business turned over more than international cricket. Whilst on one hand this was disappointing, it proved the point that a diversified
business model with more non-cricket revenue than any other Club in the country is the cornerstone of mitigating against leaner years from an international
cricket perspective which also reduces reliance on the governing body. It was also disappointing to lose the Roses fixture and the IT20 vs Australia to rain, whilst
insurance mitigated against financial loss, it was heartbreaking for customers and staff alike.

The Hundred continued to grow a new and different audience for cricket and provided an investable platform for cricket in the UK to support the wider
capitalisation of the Game.

Our commercial partnership revenues continue to be just shy of £3 million with a stable of high-quality global brands.

The Conference and Events business stabilised and achieved its budgeted revenues. The Hilton Garden Inn continued to power the business forward breaking
more records, achieving over £8.5m turnover, along with really strong average daily rate, occupancy, and local market share.

At the same time, delivering a full year of trading for the new 100-bedroom hotel extension, integrating a completely different hotel revenue strategy and still
achieving just shy of 80% occupancy almost two years ahead of plan.

The first year of trading post practical completion of any major construction project was always anticipated to squeeze cashflows, as debt servicing and total debt
is at its highest prior to new trading and new revenues becoming established and mature.

In addition to this, our operating cost environment has hardened putting pressure on margins with pressure on labour cost and availability. With our scale now of
a mid-cap at over £35 million in revenues, agreeing new supplier partnerships with Sodexo Live!, Central Fusion and Clean Event will enable the Club to focus
its energies on growth in 2025 - with these large partners providing us with expertise and cost certainty to support margin stabilisation.

Notwithstanding these challenges and short-term focus on cash management, delivering a trading profit of £4.6 million is a record in a non-Ashes year,
incorporating a record CEH combined revenue in excess of £12.5 million.

2025 and beyond

At the time of writing, the ECB has completed its auction process to bring in external investment into The Hundred - via the sale of 49% of each of the eight
teams. With a target of £350 million Enterprise value for all eight teams, delivering just shy of a £1 billion valuation at £975 million was an excellent outcome for
the game as a whole. The Club also decided to sell 21% of its 51% shareholding, to take advantage of the higher than anticipated valuation, whilst developing a
joint venture agreement with minority protections within the shareholder’s agreement.

After spending five years developing relationships within India, the Board were delighted to partner with Kolkata based conglomerate RPSG with a valuation of

Remuneration — General

As the Club moved into a “business as usual” phase following completion of the hotel extension in 2023, a tougher trading environment and higher financing
costs presented challenging trading conditions for our businesses. While the inflationary pressures of the past couple of years appear to have calmed if not
quite abated, high interest rates, a moribund UK economy and higher living costs presented a difficult environment for our customers and our people.
Undertaking a salary review in this situation presents a difficult scenario. The Committee, alongside the Executive Team, focussed on striking a balance
between affordability, retention of key people and pragmatism in dealing with the Club’s annual pay review, which resulted in a standard pay rise of 2% across
the Club as a whole. Most people employed in the Club do not participate in bonus arrangements and have a fixed basic salary.

Executive Team

The Committee’s work in relation to the remuneration arrangements for the Executive Team is focused on two key areas:

: Basic salaries: ensure that the Club is able to reward and retain the best people in these crucial roles, taking into account both market forces and the
individual contributions of the people concerned to the growth and performance of the Club.

: Bonuses: the roles undertaken by the Executive Team are critical to the operation of the Club and it is appropriate that the team is incentivised and
rewarded via bonuses. Clear and measurable targets are set for each member of the team, against which performance and resultant bonus entitlement is
assessed. Bonus metrics are bespoke to each member of the Executive Team, albeit typically there is a common element linked to profit performance for the
Club as a whole. The amount of bonus linked to profit performance varies significantly depending on role (i.e. CEO and CFO have a larger element of their
bonus linked to financial performance than, say, the Director of Cricket Performance).

For 2024, appropriate basic salary increases were awarded to Executive Team members reflecting their individual contributions to the business and the
significant responsibilities that they carry. Bonus metrics were linked, in the main, to growth of our partnerships and conferencing/events/hotel business
streams, the delivery of our overall budgeted financial performance, the development at Farrington and the performance/development of our elite men’s and
women’s teams. Bonus payments were awarded to the Executive Team which, at an individual level, amounted to between 8% and 24% of their respective
basic salaries.

2 Accounting policies
a) Accounting convention
The principal accounting policies are summarised below. They have all been applied consistently throughout the year and the preceding year.
The financial statements have been prepared under the historical cost convention, modified to include certain items at fair value, and in accordance with Financial Reporting
Standard 102 (FRS 102) issued by the Financial Reporting Council.
Notwithstanding the net current liabilities position at 31 December 2024 of £3,953,569 (2023: net liabilities £3,392,792), the financial statements have been prepared on a
going concern basis.
As set out in the Board’s Responsibilities Statement on page 13, in preparing these financial statements the Board is required to prepare the financial statements on the
going concern basis unless it is inappropriate to presume that the Club will continue in operation. The Club currently meets its day to day working capital requirements with,
when required, the help of an overdraft facility which will be in place until at least the end of March 2026.
The Board has reviewed in detail the Club's cash flow projections, and its banking facilities and has considered the Club's ability to discharge its liabilities as they fall due
and meet its financial covenants. In doing so, they have a reasonable expectation that the Club has adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the
foreseeable future. The Board therefore continues to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the annual financial statements.
b) Fixed assets
i) Fixed assets are stated at cost less depreciation.
ii) The improvement element of expenditure on major ground refurbishment work is capitalised.
iii) Finance costs which are directly attributable to the construction of tangible fixed assets are capitalised as part of the cost of those assets. The commencement of
capitalisation begins when both finance costs and expenditure for the asset are being incurred and activities that are necessary to get the asset ready for use are in progress.
Capitalisation ceases when substantially all the activities that are necessary to get the asset ready for use are complete.
iv) Fixed assets are reviewed for impairment only if there is an indication that impairment has occurred. Impairment is measured by comparing the carrying value of the
fixed asset or income-generating unit with its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the higher of the amounts that can be obtained from selling the fixed asset or
income-generating unit, net realisable value, or using the fixed asset or income-generating unit, value in use.
c) Depreciation
Depreciation is provided evenly on tangible fixed assets at rates calculated to write off the cost, less estimated residual value, of each asset over its expected useful life as
follows:
Stands and buildings: various periods from 5 years to 50 years
Equipment, furniture & fittings: various periods from 3 years to 25 years
Motor vehicles: over 5 years
Land is not depreciated
d) Income
All income, including subscriptions, sponsorships and amounts received from the England and Wales Cricket Board (ECB), is accounted for on an accruals basis except for
donations which are accounted for in the year of receipt. Income represents amounts receivable from the Club's principal activities.
The Club's principal activities are comprised of the following:
The staging of domestic and international cricket matches and other large events leading to amounts received from gate receipts, ticket sales, advertising,
sponsorship and hospitality.
The provision of catering, hotel, retail and car parking services.
Income received in respect of future periods is treated as deferred income
e) Exceptional administrative expenses
One-off, non-recurring, material items are split out on the face of the income and expenditure account
f) Stocks
Stocks are stated at the lower of cost or net realisable value.
g) Pensions
Contributions payable to the defined contribution pension schemes are charged to the income and expenditure account in the year to which they relate. The total cost
charged to income of £645,021 (2023: £595,229) represents contributions payable to these schemes. As at 31 December 2024 contributions of £112,679 (2023: £159,398)
due in respect of the current year had not been paid over to the schemes.
h) Grants
Grants are accounted for under the accruals model as permitted by FRS 102. Grants relating to expenditure on tangible fixed assets are credited to the Statement of
Comprehensive Income at the same rate as the depreciation on the assets to which the grant relates. The deferred element of grants is included in creditors as deferred
income. Grants of a revenue nature are recognised in the statement of Consolidated Comprehensive Income in the same period as the related expenditure.
i) Taxation
Current tax, including UK corporation tax, is provided at amounts expected to be paid (or recovered) using the rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively
enacted by the balance sheet date.
Deferred tax is recognised on all timing differences where the transactions or events that result in an obligation to pay more tax in the future or a right to pay less tax in the
future have occurred at the balance sheet date. Timing differences are differences between the Club's relievable tax losses and its results as stated in the financial
statements that arise from the inclusion of gains and losses in tax assessments in periods different from those in which they are recognised in the financial statements.
Deferred tax assets are recognised when it is more likely than not that they will be recovered. Deferred tax is measured using rates of tax that have been enacted or
substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.
j) Leased assets
Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to the income and expenditure account on a straight line basis over the term of the lease.
k) Finance costs
Finance costs of debt are recognised in the income and expenditure account over the term of such instrument at a constant rate on the carrying amount.
|) Interest Income
Interest income is recognised in the income and expenditure account using the effective interest method. (The treatment of the loss on the interest cap is included in the
financial instruments policy).
