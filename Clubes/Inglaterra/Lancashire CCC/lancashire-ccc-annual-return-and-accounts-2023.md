LANCASHIRE COUNTY CRICKET CLUB LIMITED
Chairman's Statement
Year Ended 31 December 2023
As we approach the start of the 2024 campaign, everyone involved at Lancashire Cricket is very excited about what's to come over the course of this season,
both on and off the field. | am just back from spending time with our squads in India and there is a lot of excitement in the camp. As always, it’s going to be
another busy 12 months, but one which we are all looking forward to.
As we look forward to the season, I'd like to take this opportunity to thank our 7,000 Members who have already signed up for 2024. Back in 2021, it was made
clear to us that Members wanted the Club to grow its membership, and this is exactly what we’ve done. The team have worked hard to engage our existing
Members more effectively, but also to make the categories simple and flexible, and importantly more appealing to new Members. I'd especially like to thank
the Members’ Representative Group for all their continued hard work — acting as the link between the Members and the Club.
Cricketing Excellence
As | have stated before, bringing trophies back to Emirates Old Trafford is our number one priority. As a Club, given the investment that has gone in, we
haven't won enough silverware in recent years.
Our men's squad had a very promising year in 2022, after being runners up in every competition, and it was disappointing not to build on that last year. With
the appointment of a new Head Coach, two overseas additions of Nathan Lyon and Tom Bruce, and a trimmed and motivated squad, we lock forward ta
seeing the men's squad make significant strides forward in 2024.
Our Thunder squad continues to make great progress and it was pleasing to see the team reach their first Finals Day in the Charlotte Edwards Cup. We know
that throughout the season — and in both competitions — we still need to be more competitive, and the appointment of a new Head Coach again reaffirms this
commitment. With the investment and resources which are being put into the Thunder programme, we believe that we should be in a position to be winning
trophies and this is the aim.
We are really pleased to welcome both of these new Head Coaches to the Club. In Dale Benkenstein and Chris Read we have been lucky to secure two top
quality coaches with great experience and we are looking forward to working with both. They are aware of the ambitions of the Board and the Members, that
we want ta compete strongly and win trophies, whilst also focusing on the development of new, young talent coming through our system. This is very important
for the long-term health of Lancashire Cricket, and even more so with the rapid growth of franchise competitions around the world.
I am pleased to confirm to our members that Lancashire Cricket has submitted a bid to become a Tier One domestic women’s team. The submission follows
the England and Wales Cricket Board’s (ECB) major plans for the evolution of women’s cricket via the creation of a three-tiered domestic competition
structure, starting from 2025. Given the level of investment and work that has gone into the Thunder programme over the last four years — and our ambitions
for the future - we are confident that we will become one of the eight Tier One teams, which are due to be announced by the ECB in April.
World Class Facilities
Last year saw the completion of the 15-year, circa £75 million redevelopment of Emirates Old Trafford after the opening of the 100-bed hotel extension. It is
another high-quality facility which will underpin the Club's long-term financial position. The hotel extension launched in October, and we've seen strong
occupancy rates in the initial first few months of business, which is pleasing to see.
From a cricket perspective, construction work started on-site at our second home in Farington, in December. This is an exciting and vital project for us - one
that we've been working on alongside Lancashire County Council, and we are looking forward to seeing the facility take shape over the next 12 months.
The aim of the venue is to establish a new, year-round facility, which would host a number of men's and women's competitive matches each season, as an
alternative to Emirates Old Trafford, and provide a new training base for Lancashire’s men's and women’s teams, from age-group to first team. I'd also like to
re-affirm our commitment to continuing to play at our current outgrounds as well - as we're aware they are popular with our Members and supporters.
It will be the first cricket facility of its kind in the UK, and it will support the next generation of young cricketers coming through our system. Our aim is to have
cricket take place at Farington by the end of next summer and we will keep Members updated on its progress throughout this year. We also want to make sure
Farington is a ground that Members and supporters can really enjoy watching their cricket and we will be creating a subgroup this year to make sure we
develop a cricket facility with a great atmosphere and environment.
Major high-profile events
2024 will be another very busy year at Emirates Old Trafford — as every year seems to be!
We look forward to hosting the reigning Champions Surrey in our opening County Championship game of the season, whilst Thunder start their campaign a
couple of weeks later. Our T20 Vitality Blast competition starts at the end of May, which includes three T20 double headers at Emirates Old Trafford this year.
Thunder will also play a number of standalone fixtures at HQ. The 50 over Metro Bank Cup and The Hundred will once again run alongside each other again
throughout July and August.
Emirates Old Trafford will host England vs Sri Lanka over the August Bank Holiday, before Australia arrives for an 1T20 in September. Meanwhile, the venue
will also host three concerts this year, which are all sold out. We have two nights of the Foo Fighters (13 and 15 June) before Green Day play a week later, on
Friday 21 June.
As Members are aware, hosting concerts — as well as our hotel and conference and events business — are vital to help the Club generate important revenues
to support a world class stadium such as Emirates Old Trafford. Revenues from cricket alone do not allow us to own and operate a major Test Match venue
and ultimately all our profits go back into supporting the Club’s cricket objectives.

LANCASHIRE COUNTY CRICKET CLUB LIMITED
Finance Report
Year Ended 31 December 2023
I am pleased to be writing my first report to you since taking over from Les Platts as Treasurer last year. | thank Les for his many years of service to
the Club and for his invaluable help during the handover period.
The Club is delighted to report record turnover of £36.5m (25% higher than 2022), following hosting of an Ashes Test Match in the summer along
with a one day international against New Zealand. Off the field we successfully held two nights of concerts and opened our new 100 bed hotel
extension on time. The Club reports an operating profit after exceptional items of £5.3m and a surplus before taxation of £1.3m. Exceptional items
of £0.4m have been split out on the Income and Expenditure Account as they relate to one off restructuring costs incurred during the year. The
surplus has been impacted by an adverse fair value movement on the interest cap instrument of £0.7m.
Income and expenditure account
International Matches
We hosted a very successful sell out Ashes Test during July with dry weather and full days of cricket on days one to three. Unfortunately, day four
was hampered by rain and with thirty overs played our rain delay insurance did not kick in. Day five was a complete wash out, resulting in full
refunds on all tickets sold. The financial impact of the rain was disappointing given such a strong start and after all the hard work involved to stage
the event. In September we staged an 1T20 against New Zealand which delivered strong financials outperforming previous IT20s and ODIs held at
Emirates Old Trafford.
Domestic Cricket
Capacity was increased compared to 2022 as construction works were completed, which resulted in an increase in domestic cricket revenues.
Lancashire Lightning reached the Quarter Finals of the T20 Blast, with revenues increasing by 24% on 2022. Unfortunately, the Roses fixture was
rained off, with the majority of revenue covered through the Club’s cancellation and abandonment insurance.
Attendance at The Hundred continued to grow with a total of 63,000 tickets issued across the four fixtures, a 14% increase on 2022, with the
Northern Superchargers fixture seeing a record attendance of 18,400 for The Hundred at Emirates Old Trafford.
Venue
Hotel and conference and events revenues continued to grow during the year, increasing by 21% on 2022. The hotel extension was opened at the
end of October and immediately saw higher than expected occupancy levels.
Expenditure
Expenditure has increased by £4.7m (18%) compared to 2022. Hosting international cricket increased both staff related costs and cricket costs (with
hospitality delivery included within cricket costs). Average staff numbers have increased from 465 to 510 to support the increased revenue. We
continued to see the impact of inflation on labour and food costs. Utility contracts expired during the year and the current economic conditions also
impacted our ability to renew at the levels previously contracted.
Exceptional items of £0.4m are shown separately on the face of the Income and Expenditure Account. These are one-off, non-recurring expenditure
and relate to the cost of a staffing restructure during the year.
Balance sheet and cash flow
Capital Investment
The vast majority of the £13m capital investment in the year was the construction of the hotel extension, including new stand and restaurant area.
Other capital projects were minimal.
Cash Flow
Prior year cashflow reflected record advance sales for the Ashes towards the end of 2022, so 2023 sees a drop on net cash inflow of £2.1m. A
further £12.5m was spent on capital investment, with most of this being on the completion of the hotel development. This was funded by a new £8m
loan with Metro Bank and £0.5m from the ECB. We thank them both for their support with this project. The remaining £4m was funded through the
Club’s working capital.
Interest paid has increased on the prior year due to the increase in debt and increase in base rate. The main loan facility with Metro Bank has an
interest rate cap in place which offers protection when the bank base rate is 3% or higher. Due to the current high cost of purchasing interest rate
caps, the additional £8m facility does nat have the same protection. We continue to regularly monitor this position.
Borrowings
Borrowings stand at £32.2m at the end of 2023. The new £8m loan facility transfers to a capital and interest repayment 18 months after first
drawdown. Management of our debt continues to be the most significant challenge for the Club. However, we expect to meet both repayment and
covenant requirements.
Outlook
The Club are working to a reduced operating profit model for the next 12 months, acknowledging that income from international cricket in 2024 will
be lower than in 2023 given the impact of The Ashes. Partly offsetting that, we expect hotel revenues to exceed £8m as a result of having an
additional 100 rooms and there are also three concerts in 2024.
The Board has reviewed the Club's detailed cashflow projections based on these forecasts and its banking facilities and has considered the Club’s
ability to discharge its liabilities as they fall due. The Board has a reasonable expectation that the Club has adequate resources to continue in
operational existence for the foreseeable future. The Board therefore continues to adopt the going concern basis of accounting in preparing the
annual financial statements.
Vic Stewart

Strategic Report
Year Ended 31 December 2023
This year's Strategic Report comes at the end of a momentous year for the Club.
Firstly, it reports on how the Club powered through the year in 2023 notwithstanding the extraordinary nature of the year incorporating considerable construction
and development, major match delivery, managing both men's and women's squads as well as growing core trading. Secondly, the risks and uncertainties section
focuses oan 2024 and how the Club is looking to drive forward at pace across all areas this year and next.
As a business, 2023 saw trading continue to grow during an unprecedented level of operational activity on site. Emirates Old Trafford continued its century old
tradition of hosting international cricket with an Ashes Test Match and an IT20 against New Zealand. Our Red Rose Together campaign was best highlighted by
our domestic men’s and women’s cricket teams with Lancashire in the County Championship, Metro Bank One-Day Cup and Vitality Blast where we remained
very difficult to beat at home, but also struggled to find wins in the County Championship at Emirates Old Trafford. In the Vitality Blast, Lancashire Lightning
remain unbeaten at home in the group stages for over three years, despite losing a tight quarter final against an outstanding Surrey team.
Lancashire Thunder, our nascent professional women’s team also had a breakthrough season with a very young team reaching the Charlotte Edwards Cup T20
Finals day for the first time in their history. As a Club, gender equity is very important to us. As a Board and Executive team we are proud to have the most gender
diverse leadership in cricket. There is currently less central funding for women’s sport and the Club has worked hard to generate additional commercial revenues
from Hilton and Sportsbreaks to directly fund the accelerated growth of our Thunder set up. This is alongside additional direct Club subsidy and equal access to
facilities at Emirates Old Trafford, including being the only ground with a permanent men's and women’s home changing rooms. At the time of writing, the Club are
actively bidding to become a Tier 1 women’s professional team to further build on the platform already made.
In addition, the third season of the Hundred saw record attendance levels, attracting and growing a younger, more diverse audience to Emirates Old Trafford. The
Club also hosted two Arctic Monkeys concerts welcoming over 100,000 music fans to the venue, and really establishing Destination Emirates Old Trafford.
The Conference and Events business returned to our record revenues last seen pre-pandemic in 2018.
The Hilton Garden Inn continued to power the business forward breaking more records, achieving over £6.5m turnover, and beating budgeted KPls across
average daily rate, occupancy, and local market share.
A operating profit (before exceptional items) of £5.7 million is a record in an Ashes year (excluding an ICC Cricket World Cup) incorporating a record Ashes
revenue in isolation of over £11 million evidencing the Club's continued momentum and upward trading trajectory. All of this across a business operating within a
highly competitive leisure industry, a cost of living crisis and unprecedented levels of inflation and yet still our customers, clients, fans and Members came out in
force ta spend money with us.
At the same time, delivering a £20.3m capital project {including site clearance, demolition, construction and professional fees} on time and on budget the 100-
bedroom hotel extension, new restaurant, terraces, bar, stand and shell for a future museum space.
Platform for future accelerated growth
At the end of 2012, the Club recorded revenues of over £9.7m, this came via catering and the England and Wales Cricket Board through a combination of
broadcast revenues and international cricket. In 2023, the Club generated a turnover of just shy of £36.5m.
In 2024, the Club will turn over more revenue from its hotel than international cricket for the first time in its history and unlike any other cricket club in the world.
This underpins the Club’s diversification strategy to ensure the financial health of the cricket club in the long term.
In addition to this, the Club generated record sponsorship revenues in 2023. In 2023, Lancs TV media platform achieved exponential growth which is widely
regarded as the best of its kind in the domestic game with a nine-camera set up. During 2023, this new media platform grew from 7.8 million views to over 16
million views of its live streaming, with just under 40% of viewers being based in India. Lancashire remains the only First-Class county to stream live on Jio
Cinema and FanCode in India, There were 40 million television views of Lancashire Cricket and Emirates Old Trafford {including internationals) with just under £25
million worth of media value delivered across all Club partners in 2023. Hilton have also increased their already significant sponsorship support to the Club for
2024 specifically focusing on supporting Lancashire Thunder's continued growth and development.
The exciting second ground project at Farington has begun construction in earnest and is on target for a late 2025 completion. This cements a strategic
partnership with Lancashire County Council, including a joint commitment to work across schools and communities County wide to drive physical activity and
cricket participation.
The Club has also just undertaken the third joint men's and women’s overseas pre-season tour to Dubai and Bangalore. Lancashire Cricket remains the only club
to do this. This year, both squads travelled together to Dubai and stayed in the fantastic Conrad and trained at the Emirates Sevens Ground. Both teams then
enjoyed an extensive ten-day training camp in Bangalore in India. The teams both played T20s against strong, local opposition, whilst Thunder also played two 50
over fixtures. Meanwhile, Lancashire’s Men became the first ever First Class County to play a three-day red ball match against an Indian Ranji Trophy state team
in Karnataka.
2024 will see Sri Lanka visiting for a Test Match against England Men and an IT20 between World Champions England and Australia. In addition, there will be the
County Championship, Vitality Blast, the Metro Bank One-Day Cup competition, the Rachel Heyhoe Flint Trophy and the Charlotte Edwards Cup matches and also
the fourth year of The Hundred that has done so much to put elite women’s cricket on the map over the last three years.
There will be three concert nights in June 2024 as we welcome the Foo Fighters for two nights and one night with Green Day at Emirates Old Trafford.
The Club has built momentum during 2023 of its Equality, Diversity and Inclusion (ED!) strategy with a targeted focus in 2024 on driving a new inclusive
recruitment strategy, a welcoming and inclusive venues delivery plan, greater focus on staff inclusivity and also a drive to increase the number of female elite
coaches in cricket. EDI| remains front and centre of each Board and Executive meeting. Manchester is famous for celebrating diversity and we at the Club are
passionate about embedding difference and diversity within our core value of welcoming, across the whole organisation. It is vital that Lancashire Cricket
represents the communities that it serves.
Risks and Uncertainties
The Club is accelerating its growth strategy. As a community and benefit society, Lancashire Cricket does not have the ability to generate external investment or
bring on equity partners, and as such funding for growth will always be a challenge. The Club's debt position at practical completion of the new development has
peaked at £32 million.
