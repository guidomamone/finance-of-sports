THE NORTHAMPTON TOWN FOOTBALL CLUB LIMITED
STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.
Fair review of the business
The significant performance indicators for the club for the current and previous year are shown below: Review of the year The men’s first team finished the 2024/25 season in 19th position in League One, confirming League One status for a third consecutive season. The women’s first team, which for the 2024/25 season was operated through Northampton Town FC Community Trust, with the assistance of the club including an increased number of matches being played at Sixfields, finished the 2024/25 season 2nd in the FA National League Division One Midlands. On 15th March 2025 the East Stand (The LCS Stand) was opened for the very first time. This represented a significant milestone for the club following years of challenges and delays for matters that were out of the control of the club. The opening of the LCS Stand delivered 16 executive boxes, a pitch facing hospitality suite and boardroom for the first time in the club’s history, with additional seats taking the Sixfieldss capacity to 8,203. An important part of the stand development was the opening of the accessible seating platform. Additional to these facilities were a new concourse, reception and additional parking including disabled parking spaces. The LCS Stand subsequently won the Land & Development Award, at the 2025 Northamptonshire Business Excellence Awards, and was noted for enhancing supporter experience, providing high-quality facilities incorporating sustainable elements and for the use of local contractors during construction. The challenges associated with football financing and in particular inflation in the player wages market accelerated dramatically in 2024/25. The EFL operates Salary Cost Management Protocol (SCMP) which governs player spending. However, there is no limit on what a club may spend on player wages due to the allowance under the rules of equity injections, and no restriction on the levels of losses.. The average player spending in League One increased significantly, and despite the club increasing its player budget by over 20% compared to the previous season, the club remained in the bottom quartile of playing budgets in League One. These increasing player costs, along with significant other increases in costs, resulted in costs growing greater than the increase in turnover and the club reported a loss of £2,995,582 for the year, compared to a loss of £2,030,615 in the previous year. The directors have continued to fund the club and maintain the highest playing budget that they are able to, while trying to keep the reliance on owner funding and losses to a level that does not risk the short, medium or long term future of the club.

Development and performance
The directors’ ambition continues to be for the club and the game to be more sustainable, but in the current environment and with the financial pressures facing the game financial sustainability is not considered possible at this point. However, the directors are encouraged by the formation of the Independent Financial Regulator (IFR) and believe that a new distribution model alongside effective changes to spending cost controls will be necessary to help to achieve sustainability in the future. The club is engaging with the IFR, with the State Of The Game Report that the IFR is currently working on, ahead of publication in 2026, seen as a key milestone ahead of action to address the challenges facing the game and all clubs. TurnoverAn increase in turnover of 11.9% was achieved compared to the previous season. This represented a record turnover for the club. Football related turnover increased by 8.3%. The opening of the LCS Stand contributed to a 37.5% increase in commercial turnover, however being opened in the latter part of the season, meant that income in relation to the LCS Stand was only included in the financial statements for a small portion of the year. It was the first year of a new domestic broadcast deal for EFL clubs, increasing central funding received from the EFL, but having a small impact on attendances for matches available on TV, being all midweek and bank holiday matches, as well as those played at 12.30 on a Saturday. CostsCost of sales increased by 18.8% compared to the previous season. This included the increase in the playing budget of over 20%. As well as the significant increase in playing costs, non-playing wages increased due to the opening of the LCS Stand and increases in the National Minimum Wage and the rules governing the payment of employers National Insurance. These increases had a direct impact on wage costs, especially those associated to match day provision, but also indirectly for brought in agency staff and other services, which all increased significantly as a result of changes to National Minimum Wage and employers National Insurance. Administration costs increased by 11% with that increase almost solely due to the opening of the LCS Stand increasing certain administration and facility costs, including power, water, cleaning and insurance.

| 1.2 | Going concern |  |
| The financial statements have been prepared on the going concern basis, which assumes that the company will continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. The Northampton Town Football Club Limited (NTFC) currently has no bank lending facilities in place and operates entirely from its bank current account to meet its commitments as they fall due, receiving further funding from the shareholders periodically to assist with working capital and continued investment in NTFC's infrastructure. The financial results are dependent on a number of factors both in and out of NTFC's control. In assessing the appropriateness of the going concern assumption, NTFC have produced forecasts that extend to the end of the 2026/27 football season, taking into account several potential scenarios to reflect the inherent uncertainty of changes to NTFC's league position. Within the scenarios forecasted, NTFC acknowledges that further financial resources will be required from the owners, and although such funding is not contracted at this time, the directors believe that sufficient funds will be made available as required. Based on the forecasts prepared, and considering the reasonably forseeable scenarios expected in relation to the ongoing football season and expectation for the subsequent season, together with the availability of further funding from the owners as required, the directors are satisfied that NTFC has sufficient resources to continue to meet its obligations as they fall due. Accordingly, the directors have concluded that it is appropriate for the financial statements to be prepared on the going concern basis. Additionally, the company has liabilities due to Belle De Jour Limited and Sole Intention Limited, which the owners have stated they will not look to call upon for 12 months from signing the accounts as the ultimate owners of both of these entities. |  |  |

At each reporting period end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. Recognised impairment losses are reversed if, and only if, the reasons for the impairment loss have ceased to apply. Where an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but so that the increased carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined had no impairment loss been recognised for the asset (or cash-generating unit) in prior years. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the reversal of the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation increase.
