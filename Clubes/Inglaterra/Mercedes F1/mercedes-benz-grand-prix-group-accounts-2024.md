Principal Activities and Business review

The principal activity of the Group is to operate and manage the Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS F1 Team, which is
~ an entrant in the FIA Formula One World Championship. This activity includes the design, development,
manufacture, testing and racing of Formula One cars.

In 2024, the team finished P4 overall in the Constructors’ Championship. Notwithstanding this result, the team
made notable year-on-year progress in key performance metrics including overall reliability, points scored, and
claiming four Grand Prix wins, five further podiums and four pole positions. 2024 was a close-fought season in
which the top four teams won races; George Russell finished P6 in the drivers' championship with team-mate
Lewis Hamilton, who completed his 13th and final season with the team in Abu Dhabi, in P7.

The close competition on track led to a more even distribution of television coverage. Our share of coverage
grew to 15.2% for 2024, reflecting improved on-track competitiveness and higher number of podium finishes. In
line with this increased coverage share, the cumulative Advertising Value Equivalent (AVE) grew for commercial
partners and shareholders, reaching $5.7 billion (+8%).

In terms of key business performance metrics, turnover grew by 16% to £636.0 million (2023: £546.5 million).
This resulted primarily from higher commercial revenues from sponsorship and licensing, which accounted for
over 60% of income in 2024. This strong commercial performance coupled with close control of the cost base,
resulted in an annual EBIT of £160.6 million (2023: £113.8 million) and the team was confirmed to be in full
compliance with the 2023 Financial Regulations, following the FIA audit process. The loss for the year made by
Mercedes-Benz Grand Prix Estates Limited is reflected in the standalone statutory accounts and was caused by
one-off expenditure relating to the development of the site.

Alongside racing activities, the Applied Science division continues to deliver bespoke engineering capability and
services to a wide range of technology projects. The America’s Cup 37 campaign with INEOS Britannia in
Barcelona saw the team become the first British challenger to reach the America's Cup Match final since 1964.
The team secured 2026 technology supply agreements with Williams F1 and Alpine Racing during the year.

The Group remains committed to leading the way in building a more sustainable and inclusive sport for the
future. Further discussion can be found in the Group non-financial and sustainability information statement and in
the Directors’ Report.

Looking to the future, the Group announced the decision to promote Kimi Antonelli to the team from the 2025
season.

During the year the redevelopment of the Brackley campus began with major infrastructure works, prior to the
planned construction of two new buildings in 2025 which will help to optimise operational efficiency. Likewise, in
December 2024, the Group acquired Mercedes-Benz Motorsport Ltd from Mercedes-Benz UK Holdings Ltd to
enable optimisation of the Group structure.

The Directors look forward to competing in the new season and a delivering a more performant and competitive
championship campaign in the final year of the current regulations.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The management of the business and the execution of the Group’s strategy are subject to several risks, which
are reviewed by the board and appropriate processes put in place to monitor and mitigate them. The key
business risks are as follows:

Competition

The Group operates in a highly competitive environment. This results in pressure to achieve not only the Group’s
targets for sporting performance but also revenue generation from sponsorship and the Commercial Rights
Holder. This risk is mitigated by the recruitment and retention of highly talented and experienced personnel,
maintaining design, development, production and race circuit facilities at the highest level within Formula One,
securing the services of the best available race drivers and strong technical relationships with Mercedes-Benz,
Petronas and other partners.

Interest and liquidity risk

The Group maintains a strong cash generation capability and the Group's liquidity is monitored closely via weekly
cashflow projections and longer term cashflow planning to ensure that the Group can meet liabilities as they fall
due. Shareholders are updated monthly on the current liquidity of the business and any deviations to forecast.
During the year, the Group also extended access to a long-term Revolving Credit Facility, in order to cover this
risk.

Credit risk

The Group's exposure to credit risk is primarily through its sponsorship contracts and it has implemented policies
that require appropriate credit checks on potential sponsors during the negotiation of new contracts. Any specific
risks are brought to the attention of management and are reassessed regularly.

Foreign exchange risk
The Group is exposed to movements in foreign exchange rates because of revenue receipts and operating costs
in currencies other than sterling. These risks are monitored closely and reported on monthly.

Reputational Risk

Protecting the Group's brand and reputation remains a risk, recently the process has been reviewed with regard
to new sponsors and partnerships to ensure the highest levels of reputation are maintained.

Financial Key Performance Indicators
The primary objective of the Group is to perform on track, with the aim of winning World Championships and to
be financially successful in parallel. The financial performance of the Group is continuously monitored, and the

most key performance indicators are Revenue, EBIT and Free Cashflow.

The most dominant metric is EBIT which was £160.6m for the year ended 2024 (2023: £113.8m)

Identification, assessment and management of climate-related risks and opportunities

The Sustainability team manages day-to-day environmental risks in-line with the standards set out in the Group's
1$014001:2015 certified Environmental Management System. The Sustainability team assesses the Group's
environmental aspects and impacts based on compliance obligations as well as relevant risks and opportunities.
Key risks identified are communicated to the relevant department contacts, the EWG and MCM sponsors for
action.

In 2023, climate-related transition risks were assessed qualitatively by the EWG based on stakeholder
consultation and planned Group operations. External consultants were also engaged to assess current and
future climate-related physical risks.

In 2024, the business undertook its first Double Materiality Assessment (DMA) to identify material impacts, risks
and opportunities, which includes climate related risks. The scope for the DMA was set to align with the EFRAG
guidelines.

Several material impacts were’ identified for the business across the Environmental, Social and Governance
pillars. Ongoing tracking and management of the environmental related outputs from the DMA is carried out by
the Environmental Working Group.

Description of how processes for identifying, assessing and managing climate related risks are
integrated into the overall risk management process of the group in line with our Risk Management
Policy

On an annual basis, the impacts, risks and opportunities (IROs) identified in the DMA will be reviewed internally
by each Working Group to ensure that they remain relevant, capture internal and external factors which may
affect these and to identify any new IROs. These would be assessed against the DMA thresholds to ascertain
whether they are a material IRO.

Strategy

Environmentai risks and opportunities, including those which are climate-related, have been assessed over the
following horizons: short 1-2 years (to end 2025); medium 3-7 years (2026-2030); and long >7 years (2031-
2040). These timeframes have been chosen to align with the Group’s sustainability targets, financial planning
and sporting regulations framework. The size and prioritisation of climate related risks identified in the DMA
process has been reviewed by the EWG in 2024.

Climate-related risks and opportunities are classified as either physical or transition risks, and have been
grouped into ESG matters. These risks and opportunities have been scored based on two criteria:

e Magnitude: the potential magnitude of financial effects in the short, medium and long-term.
e Likelihood: the likelihood of occurrence of the risk or opportunity during a defined time horizon.

Physical Risks:

No material climate-related physical risks or opportunities have been identified.

Borderline risks identified include the increased likelihood of extreme weather events causing local flooding or
drought or the cancellation of Grand Prix events. The Group expects this to have an impact at Grand Prix events
over all time horizons assessed and are liaising with Formula One to support calendar reviews.

Transition Risks and Opportunities

Material climate-related transition risks and opportunities have been identified.

Metrics and targets

continual improvement of processes and procedures to reduce environmental impact.

The targets also indirectly support the management of the transition risks identified by the business through
delivering greater transparency on emissions reduction activities.

Environmental policy and mandatory greenhouse gas emissions reporting

Targets

The Group continues to make progress towards its 2030 carbon emissions targets. For Scopes 1 & 2, the Group
is targeting a 100% reduction of emissions, with any residual emissions to be compensated using carbon
removals in line with the Oxford Offsetting Principles. We are also targeting a 75% reduction in Scope 3

emissions within Race Team Control (RTC) and compensation of the residual 25% using carbon removals.

These targets align to the Mercedes-Benz AG Ambition 2039 for carbon neutral vehicle production and Formula
One's Factory to Flag emissions reporting.

Details of how these targets have been set are outlined in the Group Non-Financial and Sustainability
Information Statement.

In 2024, the Group became a signatory of the Climate Pledge and is setting a 1.5-degree-aligned target to cover
all Scope 3 emissions categories and support its ambition to meet Net Zero by 2040.

To deliver the RTC target, the Group has set out a reduction-first approach, with carbon avoidance certificates
and removals being used to compensate residual emissions. The Group will continue to assess market trends
and best practice when implementing reduction, removal and avoidance measures.

Energy targets and efficiency improvements are driven through the implementation of the 1S014001:2015
certified Environmental Management System (EMS).

Emissions
In 2024, the Group implemented a range of projects to reduce Scope 1 & 2 emissions.
Across the Brackley campus we continued to reduce energy emissions through the purchase of electricity

supplies backed by Renewable Energy Guarantees of Origin (REGOs) and gas supplies backed by Renewable
Gas Guarantees of Origin (RGGOs) which support the production of renewable energy.

To deliver energy efficiencies, the team has focused on facility upgrades and process improvements. These
include: updating the cooling programmes in the data centre for more efficient cooling; installing heat capture
systems within manufacturing areas, using waste heat to support hot water demands across the facility. This has
reduced cooling demand within manufacturing areas and reduced reliance on energy for water heating.

Since 2023, 550 individual energy sub-meters have been installed. In 2024 an updated energy dashboard was
rolled out to key departments, using information from these sub-meters, to improve energy efficiency. The Group
will continue to use this data to identify savings. All of the work completed has resulted in a total energy reduction
reduction per head of 18% between 2022 and 2024.

Gas supplies continue to be removed across our manufacturing, office and simulator facilities, reducing gas
consumption by over 179,500 kWh within 2024 when compared to 2023. The phase-out of gas across Brackley
will continue in 2025

To reduce overall fuel emissions, the business has transitioned most of its pool car vehicles towards Battery
Electric Vehicles (BEV) alternatives and included HVO100 within onsite equipment. This saved 95 tCO2e. of
Scope 1 fuel emissions compared to 2023. The remaining equipment will be assessed to ensure further
reductions can be made when market offerings allow.

Improvements have also been made with increased usage of HVO100 biodiesel in the UK and European legs of
the racing season. This included 100% coverage of HVO100 in the generators used across the garage and
motorhome, avoiding 61 tCO2e when compared to the emissions of fossil-based diesel fuel.

Separately, there was an increase in fugitive emissions compared to 2023. As a result, cumulative Scope 1
emissions in 2024 increased compared to 2023, notwithstanding activities to reduce energy consumption and
fuel emissions in other areas of Scope 1. A single system fault resulted in a substantial fugitive gas (F-Gas) leak
which drove the majority of this increase. Work is ongoing to reduce the risk of a repeat event in future.

Methodology

Scope 1 & 2 consumption data has been calculated in accordance with the GHG Protocol’s methodology for
operational control location and market-based approaches. In addition to reporting stationary combustion in line
with the GHG protocol categories, the use of an alternative reporting method for Scope 1 allows the presentation
of both a market and location-based footprint from the site’s gas supply, backed by RGGOs. The conversion
calculations from kWh to tCO2e were completed using internal consumption data from invoices and meter
readings. These conversions were completed using the 2024 UK Government GHG Conversion Factors for
Company Reporting with the appropriate gross calorific value (CV) conversion value for the reporting year
(01/01/2024 ~ 31/12/2024).

The intensity ratio has been calculated using the average number of employees within 2024. The final value was
calculated by dividing the total tCO2e by the average employee numbers in 2024.

Basis for opinion

We conducted our audit in accordance with International Standards on Auditing (UK) (“ISAs (UK)’) and

Going concern

liquidate the Group or the Company or to cease their operations, and as they have concluded that the Group and
the Company's financial position means that this is realistic. They have also concluded that there are no material
uncertainties that could have cast significant doubt over their ability to continue as a going concern for at least a
year from the date of approval of the financial statements (“the going concern period”).

In our evaluation of the directors’ conclusions, we considered the inherent risks to the Group’s business model
and analysed how those risks might affect the Group and Company’s financial resources or ability to continue
operations over the going concern period.

Our conclusions based on this work:

Company's ability to continue as a going concern for the going concern period.

Going concern

The financial statements have been prepared on a going concern basis which the directors consider
to be appropriate for the following reasons.

The principal activity of the Group and the Company is the design, development, manufacture, testing
and racing of Formula One cars. The Group and Company's cash flows are therefore dependent on
the continuation of those operations.

The directors have prepared cashflow forecasts and performed a going concern assessment which
indicates that, in both the base and reasonably possible downside scenario, which excludes revenue
agreements not yet signed, the Group and Company will have sufficient funds to meet their liabilities
as they fall due during the 12 month period ending 31 March 2026, the going concern assessment
period. In both the base and reasonably possible downside scenario, the revolving credit facility
disclosed in Note 20 is forecast to remain undrawn.

Consequently, the directors are confident that the Group and Company will have sufficient funds to
continue to meet their liabilities as they fall due for at least 12 months from the date of approval of the
financial statements and therefore have prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis.

Revenue

Revenue is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the
Group and the revenue can be reliably measured. Revenue is measured as the fair value of the
consideration received or receivable, excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales
taxes.

The Company principally earns revenue through sponsorship income, prize money, sale of goods
and rendering of services. Revenue in relation to sponsorship deals where there are a number of
deliverables, is recognised by attributing the revenue to each deliverable by considering their
individual standalone value and the overall value of contractual sponsorship income for a specific
sponsor. The revenue is then recognised once we are satisfied the relevant recognition criteria has
been met.

Where sponsorship deals are satisfied in payment other than cash, revenue is recognised on the fair
value of the goods or services received. Prize money is received in relation to performance in the FIA
Formula One World Championships and is based on reliable estimates of the expected prize money
available.

If no reliable measurement can be made for any revenue estimates, the revenue is not recognised.

1.12 intangible assets

Goodwill

Goodwill represents the difference between amounts paid on the cost of a business combination and
the acquirer's interest in the fair value of the Group's share of its identifiable assets and liabilities of
the acquiree at the date of acquisition. Subsequent to initial recognition, goodwill is measured at cost
less accumulated amortisation and accumulated impairment losses. Goodwill is amortised on a
straight-line basis to the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income over its useful economic
life.

Other intangible assets
Intangible assets are initially recognised at cost. After recognition, under the cost model, intangible
assets are measured at cost less any accumulated amortisation and any accumulated impairment

losses.

All intangible assets are considered to have a finite useful life. If a reliable estimate of the useful life
cannot be made, the useful life shall not exceed ten years.

The historical cost of intangible assets such as software licenses includes expenditure that is directly

1.14 Impairment of fixed assets

Assets that are subject to depreciation or amortisation are assessed at each balance sheet date to
determine whether there is any indication that the assets are impaired. Where there is any indication
that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset (or cash-generating unit to which the
asset has been allocated) is tested for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised for the amount
by which the asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the
higher of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs to sell and value in use. For the purposes of
assessing impairment, assets are grouped at the lowest levels for which there are separately
identifiable cash flows (CGUs). Non-financial assets that have been previously impaired are reviewed
at each balance sheet date to assess whether there is any indication that the impairment losses
recognised in prior periods may no longer exist or may have decreased.
