GROUP STRATEGIC REPORT FOR THE YEAR ENDED 31 DECEMBER 2025

Principal activities and business review

The principal activity of the Group is to operate and manage a Formula One racing team, the Mercedes-AMG
PETRONAS F1 Team, which is an entrant in the FIA Formula One World Championship. This activity includes
the design, development, manufacture, testing and racing of Formula One cars. The Group's success is
measured by performance in the Championship and by reference to the financial parameters defined by its
Shareholders.

In 2025, the team finished in second place in the Constructors' Championship with George Russell and Kimi
Antonelli finishing in fourth and seventh places respectively in the Drivers’ Championship. The team achieved
two race wins with Russell in Canada and Singapore, and a further 12 podium finishes over the course of the
season, including a maiden podium for Antonelli in Canada and a season-best finish of second place in Brazil.
The team’s F1 Academy driver, Doriane Pin, secured the title at the end of her second and final season in
the series, having finished in second place in 2024.

Television broadcast coverage over the 2025 season was influenced by the success of championship winners
McLaren who took 24% of the overall coverage. This contributed to our coverage decreasing from 15.5% in
2024 to 13.2% in 2025. In line with this reduced coverage share, the TV Advertising Value Equivalent (AVE)
decreased slightly for commercial partners and Shareholders, achieving $5.66 billion (-2% on 2024).

Formula One saw a record race attendance of 6.75 million in 2025 (+4% versus 2024). The sport had a global
fanbase of 831 million people as of the end of 2025, of which nearly 350 million are female, and is growing
most strongly with young female fans.

The growing appeal of the sport is reflected in the team's continued development of our Club hospitality
offering in Miami, Silverstone, Las Vegas and Abu Dhabi, an additional 4 million social media followers gained
during 2025 and a team partner roster of 24 premium global brands, including new partnerships with adidas,
Meta Al and Nasdaq in 2025. The Mercedes-AMG World of Performance is currently under development in
Qiddiya, a future race destination near Riyadh in Saudi Arabia.

In terms of key business performance metrics, turnover grew by £1.3 million to £633.4 million (2024: £632.1
million). This resulted primarily from higher commercial revenues from sponsorship and licensing, which
accounted for over £415 million of income in 2025. This strong commercial performance coupled with close
control of the cost base, resulted in an annual profit after taxation of £125.9 million (2024: £116.9 million).
During the year, the Group delivered £125 million (2024: £100 million) in dividends to the Shareholders from
distributable funds. The team was confirmed to be in full compliance with the 2024 Financial Regulations,
following the FIA audit process.

2025 saw the team refocus its Applied Science division following the decision by Aston Martin Racing to
move to Honda powertrain supply and by INEOS to withdraw their intention to challenge for the 38" America’s
Cup, which brought the team’s involvement to a conclusion. In respect of the supply of assemblies and
services to F1 customers, the team continues to deliver technology supply agreements to Williams F1 and to
Alpine Racing fram 2026. ,

We are committed to leading the way in building a more sustainable and inclusive sport for the future and
continue to make progress towards our 2030 carbon emissions reduction targets. We have pioneered the
use of sustainable fuels in our aviation and road logistics, achieving 99% coverage of HVO100 for our
European logistics, and transporting our race cars by electric trucks for the first time to the British and Outch
Grands Prix.

We continue to improve the energy efficiency of operations at our factory with 2025 seeing significant
investment into an on-site solar canopy and the ongoing transition to the use of electric vehicles in our fleet.
We refocused our carbon credit strategy to prioritise removals over offsets to compensate for residual
emissions.

Our partnerships with the Mulberry Schools Trust, Star Academy, the Association for Black and Minority
Ethnic Engineers and the Sutton Trust continue to grow. We hosted 35 students for work experience and
over 800 students were engaged through careers events, school visits and STEM activities. Scholarships at
Imperial College London and through Zero Gravity have provided support and access to STEM-related higher
education for under-represented students.

Looking ahead, the Group set important foundations in the latter months of 2025.

In October, we announced that our driver pairing of Russell and Antonelli would continue into the 2026
season, providing continuity into the most significant reset of Technical Regulations for over a decade.

Russell and Antonelli are both graduates of the team’s junior programme, having joined in 2017 and 2019
respectively. Multiple Grand Prix winner Russell, 28, competes in his eighth season in F1 and his fifth with
the team. Antonelli, 19, is in his second year, building on a strong rookie season in which he became the
third youngest podium finisher in the sport's history.

In November, George Kurtz, CEO and Founder of CrowdStrike, became a co-owner of the team through a
minority acquisition in Toto Wolffs ownership entity Motorsports Invest. George also became Technology
Advisor and joined the team’s Strategic Steering Committee.

In March 2025, Formula One and all 11 teams confirmed the signing of the commercial elements of the 2026
Concorde Agreement and later in December, Formula One, the FIA and all 11 teams finalised the
Governance Agreement, with the combined effect securing the future of the sport through to 2030.

The redevelopment of the team’s Brackley site with two new buildings will be completed in the second half
of 2026, helping to optimise operational efficiency and maximise employee well-being.

The Group will continue its principal activity as a Formula One racing team and its principal mission to win
on track. The Directors look forward to competing in the next season of Formula One in a new era of Technical
Regulations and delivering a competitive championship campaign in 2026.

Principal risks and uncertainties

The management of the business and the execution of the Group's strategy are subject to several risks,
which are reviewed by the Board and appropriate processes put in place to monitor and mitigate them. The
key business risks are as follows:

Competition

The Group operates in a highly competitive environment. This results in pressure to achieve not only the
Group's targets for sporting performance but also revenue generation from sponsorship and the Commercial
Rights Holder. This risk is mitigated by the recruitment and retention of highly talented and experienced
personnel, maintaining design, development, production and race circuit facilities at the highest level within
Formula One, securing the services of the best available race drivers and strong technical relationships with
Mercedes-Benz, PETRONAS and other partners.

Interest and liquidity risk

The Group maintains a strong cash generation capability and the group liquidity is monitored closely via
weekly cashflow projections and longer term cashflow planning to ensure that the Group can meet liabilities
as they fall due. Shareholders are updated monthly on the current liquidity of the business and any deviations
to forecast. During the year, the Group also increased, but did not use, its long-term Revolving Credit Facility,
to cover this risk.

Credit risk

The Group’s exposure to credit risk is primarily through its sponsorship contracts and it has implemented
policies that require appropriate credit changes on potential sponsors during the negotiation of new contracts.
Any specific risks are brought to the attention of management and are reassessed regularly.

Foreign exchange risk

The Group is exposed to movements in foreign exchange rates because of revenue receipts and operating
costs in currencies other than sterling. These risks are monitored closely and reported on monthly.

Reputational risk

Protecting the Group’s brand and reputation remains a risk; recently the process has been reviewed with
regard to new Sponsors and partnerships to ensure the highest levels of reputation are maintained.

Geopolitical risk

The Group's operations and financial performance are inherently exposed to geopolitical developments, as
the Formula One calendar and related activities are global in nature. Events such as political instability, armed
conflict, terrorism, civil unrest, or the imposition of sanctions may result in the disruption, postponement, or
cancellation of Grands Prix, including the inability to transport cars, equipment, and personnel, or to fulfil
commercial and sponsorship obligations.

Recent developments in the Middle East, including the cancellation of the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian Grands
Prix, highlight the potential for sudden and prolonged disruption. Such events may lead to reduced
Championship participation, loss or deferral of revenue, increased logistical costs, and potential impairment
of contractual arrangements with'sponsors, promoters, and suppliers. While the Group continues to monitor
geopolitical developments and seeks to mitigate impacts through contingency planning and flexible
operational arrangements, there can be no assurance that such risks can be fully mitigated.

Financial key performance indicators

The primary objective of the Group is to perform on track, with the aim of winning World Championships and
to be financially successful in parallel. The financial performance of the Group is continuously monitored, and
the most key performance indicators are revenue, operating profit, and free cashflow.

The most dominant metric is operating profit which was £166.7m for the year ended 2025 (2024: £156.2m).
The 2024 figure has been restated following the Group's transition from FRS 102 to UK-adopted IFRSs from
2025.

Future outlook

The future outlook for the Group is strong thanks to the robust health of Formula One and the Group's portfolio
of world-class partners. To date in the first quarter of 2026, the team has announced new partnerships with
Microsoft, Nu and Zuma.

The technical project for 2026 represents the biggest set of simultaneous rule changes in the sport's history,
with brand-new chassis, power unit and fuel regulations. This project is the focus of close collaboration
between the Group, Mercedes-AMG High Performance Powertrains and our Title and Technical Partner
PETRONAS, with the shared goal to deliver the most performant project in the sport's new era.

As a high-technology business, the Group can adapt competitively and commercially to changes in its
operating environment, and will continue to invest in capability, culture and mindset to ensure medium and
long-term success.

The Group would like to thank its Shareholders, Mercedes-Benz, Motorsports Invest, INEOS, our Title and
Technical Partner PETRONAS and all our team partners for their continued steadfast support.

Identification, assessment, and management of climate-related risks and opportunities

The sustainability team manages day-to-day environmental risks in-line with the standards set out in the
Group’s 1S014001:2015 certified Environmental Management System (EMS). The sustainability team
assesses the Group’s environmental aspects and impacts based on compliance obligations as well as
relevant risks and opportunities. The sustainability team score risks and opportunities and track
environmental observations and actions as part of the EMS.

Any risk identified with a score above the significance threshold is managed and summarised by the
Sustainability team into relevant updates throughout the year within EMS updates, department meetings or
Environmental Working Group (EWG) meetings. This process is also followed for repeat observations and
actions.

In 2024 the Group completed a Double Materiality Assessment (DMA), set to align with the EFRAG
guidelines, to identify impacts, risks and opportunities (IROs). With the support of a third party, potential
sustainability matters were identified by reviewing Annex | AR 16 of ESRS 1. This identification was
additionally informed by peer analysis, industry standards analysis, an ESG industry materiality map, and a
company review.

The businesses held a review in 2025 to confirm there were no material changes across the Environmental,
Social, or Governance pillars. It was determined that the 2024 DMA remained appropriate for activities in
calendar year 2025, and that the material impacts identified for the business across the Environmental, Social
and Governance pillars remained unchanged. It was agreed that the Working Groups and Committee would
continue to monitor our activities against these throughout the year.

There is ongoing tracking of the outputs from the DMA, as described below.

The size and prioritisation of climate related risks have been reviewed by the EWG in 2025.

Climate related risks and opportunities are classified as either physical or transition risks and have been

e Likelihood: the likelihood of occurrence of the risk or opportunity during a defined time horizon.

of occurrence. Semi-quantitative thresholds for the severity criteria and likelinood were set and reviewed by
a specialist third party and experts across the business.

Environmental policy and mandatory greenhouse gas emissions reporting
Targets

Mercedes-Benz Grand Prix Limited continues to make progress towards its 2030 carbon emissions reduction
targets. For Scopes 1 & 2, the Group is targeting a 100% reduction of emissions, with any residual emissions
to be compensated using carbon removals in line with the Oxford Offsetting Principles. The Group is also
targeting a 75% reduction in Scope 3 emissions within Race Team Control (RTC) and will compensate for
the residual 25% using carbon removals. These targets align to Formula One’s Factory to Flag emissions
reporting.

The Group is a signatory of the Climate Pledge and is targeting Net Zero across all Scopes 1, 2 and 3 by
2040. The pathway to meet this target has been 1.5-degree-aligned.

Additional details, including reporting boundaries and how these targets have been set, are outlined in the
Group Non-Financial and Sustainability Information Statement.

To deliver these targets, the Group has set out a reduction-first approach. From 2025 the Group has shifted
its carbon credit strategy in line with the Oxford Offsetting Principles. The Group is now prioritising the
procurement of carbon removals over carbon offsets to compensate for residual emissions. The Group will
scale the retirement of removal credits from 2025 to meet its 2030 RTC target.

Energy targets and efficiency improvements are driven through the implementation of the 1$014001:2015
certified Environmental Management System (EMS).

Emissions

To reduce Scope 1 & 2 emissions associated with infrastructure across the Group's Brackley site, the Group
focuses on activities including the removal of gas infrastructure, the increase of renewable energy generation,
and the reduction of overall electricity consumption.

2025 saw significant investment into an on-site solar canopy, which is forecast to generate 1.1 GWh of
renewable electricity per year. This in turn avoids the production of 195 tCO2e of location-based emissions
annually.

Furthermore, the Group continues to procure Renewable Energy Guarantees of Origin (REGO) and
Renewable Gas Guarantees of Origin (RGGO) certificates to cover all electricity and gas consumption on its
Brackley site, and these certificates also support the production of renewable energy across the wider market.

In 2025, the use of energy sub-meters has improved the tracking of electricity consumption and identification
of reduction opportunities across the Group's Brackley site. This programme has been trialled across several
departments, with a focus on promoting behavioural change and identifying process improvements. In
addition, there has been a reduction in equipment utilisation providing further energy reductions. The
combination of these activities has generated a 1% reduction in overall electricity usage compared to 2024.

The Group continued to focus on gas removal across its Brackley site in 2025. For example, heating
equipment in manufacturing areas was upgraded from gas to electricity, delivering a 19,469 kWh reduction
in overall gas usage compared to 2024. Further reductions in gas consumption will continue through 2026.

in addition to infrastructure upgrades, the Group continued to expand the use of battery electric vehicles
(BEVs) to reduce emissions within the mobile combustion emission category. Our pool car fleet is
transitioning to efectric, with five BEVs used across the full calendar year, following introduction part way
through 2024. This has in turn contributed to a 12 tCO2e emissions reduction in 2025 compared to 2024.

Emissions associated with trackside operations reduced in 2025 thanks to the centralised energy solution
provided by Formula 1 across the European season. This solution is powered by a combination of HVO100,
grid-purchased energy and solar power. The adoption of this solution, following a trial in 2024, has resulted
in a 2.6 tCO2e emissions reduction.

the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of the underlying asset. If this is the case, then the lease is a finance
lease; if not, then it is an operating lease. As part of this assessment, the Group considers certain indicators such
as whether the lease is for the major part of the economic life of the asset.

The Group recognises lease payments received under operating leases as income on a straight-line basis over the
lease term as part of ‘other revenue’.

The Group acts as lessor for components provided to customer teams. The components are provided for one race
season only and therefore the Group considers that these agreements constitute operating leases, not finance
leases.

1.18 Non-current assets held for sale

A non-current asset is classified as held for sale if its carrying amount will be recovered principally through sale
rather than through continuing use, it is available for immediate sale and sale is highly probable within one year.

On initial classification as held for sale, non-current assets and disposal groups are measured at the lower of
previous carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell with any adjustments taken to profit or loss. Intangible
assets and property, plant and equipment once classified as held for sale or distribution are not amortised or
depreciated.

This treatment may be a departure from the requirements of the Companies Act concerning depreciation of property,
plant and equipment. However, these property, plant and equipment are not held for consumption but for sale and the
Directors consider that systematic annual depreciation would be inappropriate. The accounting policy adopted is
therefore necessary for the accounts to give a true and fair view. The effect of this departure is to increase the
Company's profit for the financial year and tangible fixed assets by Enil (2024: Enil). .

25 Accounting estimates and judgements
Race car development

The race car development balance includes internal labour costs for time spent developing the following year’s racing
car. These costs are recognised within Inventary, under IAS 2, consistent with the recognition of the racing car and its
components as inventory.

Management assessed the possible treatment under IAS 38 (Intangible Assets), IAS 16 (Property, Plant and
Equipment) and IAS 2 (Inventory). Management concluded that IAS 2 represents the most appropriate treatment. The
interna! labour costs do not meet the recognition criteria of IAS 38, as the design activities do not result in an identifiable,
separable intangible asset capable of generating future economic benefits independently of the related Inventory, and
there is no separately identifiable tangible asset to which the internal labour costs are directly attributable for recognition
under IAS 16.

IAS 2 requires Inventories to be measured at the cost of purchase, cost of conversion and other costs incurred in
bringing Inventories to their present location and condition. Management has judged that the costs of designing racing
car components are an integral part of the production process and are necessary to bring those components to their
present condition. Accordingly, internal labour costs directly attributable to the design of components held in Inventory
are included within race car development.

Internal labour costs relating to research activities, proof of concept work, or components no longer held in Inventory
are expensed as incurred. Management applies judgement in determining the point at which design activities become
directly attributable to the production of Inventory and maintains processes and controls to ensure that only eligible
costs are capitalised.
