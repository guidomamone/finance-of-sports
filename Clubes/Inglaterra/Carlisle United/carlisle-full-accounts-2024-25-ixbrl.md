FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.
| The Company’s Business and Strategy |
| --- |

A full independent audit including this annual Strategic Report remains an important part of the Club’s engagement with supporters.
The principal activity of Carlisle United Association Football Club (1921) Limited (“the Company” or “ the Club”) remains unchanged. The Company undertakes all day-to-day operational trading activities of ‘Carlisle United’, holds the EFL ‘Golden Share’ and is the only subsidiary of CUFC Holdings Limited (‘Holdings’). Holdings is an intermediate company which does not trade. 1921’s ultimate parent company is Castle Sports Group Limited (‘CSG’) which took over control of the Company and Holdings in November 2023.
| Principal Activities and Business Review |
| --- |

The 2024/25 season was the Club’s 120th season as a professional football club and saw the Club back in League Two following relegation after one season in League One.
The Club began the season with Paul Simpson as First Team Manager, but suffered three defeats in the first four league matches. Paul Simpson left the Club on 31 August 2024. Mike Williamson was appointed First Team Head Coach on 19 September 2024. The following 22 games resulted in four wins, six draws and twelve defeats, leading to the dismissal of Mike Williamson on 3 February 2025. Mark Hughes was appointed Head Coach on 6 February 2025. The Club was relegated to the National League on 26 April 2025 following defeat at Cheltenham Town.
Despite a difficult season on the pitch, the Club continued to strengthen a number of its core revenue streams and make progress off the pitch. This was particularly evident through continued development in commercial activity, hospitality, facilities and supporter engagement.
In May 2024, the Club began a £6 million renovation project on the top three floors of the East Stand, creating new offices and classrooms on the first floor, four new hospitality lounges on the second floor and eight executive boxes on the third floor. This increased hospitality capacity by over 200 and all the areas were immediately popular with fans and businesses. This work has given the Club modern facilities which are contributing to increased commercial income both on match days and non-match days.
Commercial and retail income continued to perform well despite a difficult season, with new initiatives and packages launched across our commercial offering.
Home match attendances held well despite relegation from League One and disappointing performances in League Two. Attendances dropped by 7% from an average of 8,004 in 23/24 to an average of 7,424 in 24/25. Home fans reduced by only 4% from an average of 7,232 in 23/24 to 6,907 in 24/25.
Overall Club turnover reduced from £8.3m (23/24) to £6.6m (24/25), a reduction of £1.7m. Of this, £1.4m was attributable to reduced player trading income, which fell from £1.9m (23/24) to £0.5m (24/25). While overall turnover was lower, the Club continued to make progress in a number of its underlying revenue lines.
Within the first full season under the current ownership, the club saw a significant contribution towards the playing budget. Football expenditure increased materially as the Club sought to retain its League Two status. Total football expenditure increased from £3.8 million to £5.4 million in the profit and loss account, with additional investment in transfer fees of over £500,000.
Within football expenditure, player costs increased from £2.77 million in 2023/24 to £3.76 million in 2024/25, an increase of £1.0 million. These increased costs reflected a deliberate investment in football operations, although the outcome of the season fell short of expectations.

Strategy and Objectives
Following the end of the 2024-25 season, a full Club review was undertaken not just to identify what went wrong, but to define a clear and structured path forward, rooted in excellence, accountability and sustained improvement.
This review led to a restructure exercise across a number of Club departments with the aim of strengthening knowledge and expertise across the whole Club. From this the Club set out its Vision and Values.
The vision is simple –
“To compete at the highest level possible as a sustainable football club that inspires pride and belongs to everyone.”
This vision is shared across all the departments of the Football Club and the Community Sports Trust, and from it we measure our success against our values.
Integrity
The Club is driving high standards across all customer-facing departments; ensuring all interactions are positive and consistent. The Club has seen improvements in our media offerings and the delivery of information to fans now takes place across a variety of platforms. The Club remains an active participant in CUSG (Carlisle United Supporters Groups) meetings and ensures representation from the Club’s senior leadership.
Excellence
The Club has undertaken significant renovation projects to revitalise both unused and under-utilised areas of the stadium. The vision of the ownership has seen the creation of modern spaces with up-to-date facilities.
These projects include the East Stand Lounges and Executive Boxes, expansion of the East Stand offices, the Warwick Road End Fan Zone and toilets, the Neil Centre upgrade and the refitting of new home changing rooms and match official areas. These areas are a marked improvement on what was there before and now set the standard for future developments.
Ambition and Innovation
The Club is united in one sole objective, to be back in the Football League. Investment in stadium technology and commercial infrastructure supports this ambition. The installation of LED perimeter advertising and big screens around the stadium has also created greater visibility and impact for commercial partners.
The Club is working with partners to look at new technologies and the advances and effectiveness that can be delivered by embracing these new opportunities. We have seen this with our LED pitch perimeter boards which have provided new commercial opportunities, and our better CRM systems which enable us to engage better with fans. The Club is looking to further these developments with software enhancements to improve operational efficiencies.
Teamwork and Respect
The Club must work collaboratively with its wider community. It must listen and respond. Work is continually ongoing to improve this and ensure we use the methods supporters and partners value the most.
The Community Sports Trust has over 25,000 unique participants across education programmes, school delivery, soccer schools and more than 28 ongoing projects. This is work the Club is extremely proud of, both because of the difference this work makes to our community and the value it adds to the Club.
Our inclusion work continued and the Club was proud to have been awarded a Silver Award in the EFL Equality Code of Practice in January 2026.

Pride and Passion
The Club wants to create shared memories for its fan base that inspire lifelong loyalty. To achieve this, the Club needs to produce performances that excite and entertain fans and the wider audience.
Equality, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI)
In 2024/25 the Club established an Equality, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) Team to deliver initiatives that champion inclusivity, equality, and community wellbeing — both within the club and across the wider region. This Team of volunteers is coordinated through our Community Sports Trust and works with a wide range of charity and community partners to deliver meaningful projects. This work ensures all members of our community are represented and supported through the Club.
Strategic objectives
Five strategic objectives, consistent across the club, are in place to help us deliver the vision and values and ensure all departments are pulling together.
1.Deliver On-Field Success & Player Development
Focus on achieving promotion back to the EFL while strengthening the squad and developing academy players to progress into the first team.
2.Improve Fan & Community Engagement
Build stronger connections with supporters and the wider community through improved communication, events, and community programmes.
3.Commercial Growth & Revenue Expansion
Increase commercial income by growing ticketing, hospitality, sponsorships, retail activity, and digital engagement.
4.Develop Infrastructure & Financial Sustainability
Invest in facilities and operational improvements at Brunton Park while maintaining financial discipline to support long-term sustainability.
5.Embed the Culture to Deliver Our Vision
Promote the Club’s vision and values across all departments, fostering collaboration, accountability, and a high-performance culture.
| Principal Risks and Uncertainties |
| --- |

Ownership Risk
The Company is 93.2% owned by CUFC Holdings, which is in turn owned 90% by CSG. CSG provides capital to fund the Club’s needs, either in terms of long-term capital investment or subsidy for football spending to increase the ability of the club to compete on the pitch or emergency reserve funding.
Financial Risks
The key risk of the Club is the inability to meet its financial obligations. With the current ownership and funding structure, this risk is low at this time.
The Company operates in a challenging business environment where income streams can be unpredictable while costs are fixed. In addition, the Club faces new risks with increasing operating costs and the levels of discretionary spend available to its supporters and business partners.
