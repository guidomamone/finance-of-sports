Strategic Report

Chairman’s Statement

it is my privilege to present my first Chairman’s Statement following my appointment earlier this year. Taking
on this role at Leicester Tigers is both an honour and a responsibility. This Club has a proud history stretching
back nearly 150 years, and it is my commitment, alongside the Board and Executive, to ensure we build on that
heritage while positioning Leicester Tigers for a sustainable and successful future.

Thanks to Peter Tom CBE

At the outset, | must pay tribute to my predecessor, Peter Tom CBE, who has served Leicester Tigers with
distinction for decades, most recently as Chairman. His leadership guided the Club through periods of success on
the field, as well as significant financial and structural challenges off it.

Peter’s contribution to Leicester Tigers cannot be overstated. From overseeing Premiership titles to navigating
the challenges of professionalism in rugby, he has been a constant presence, providing stability and vision. On
behalf of the Board, staff, players and supporters, | extend my deepest thanks to Peter for his extraordinary
service so far. His legacy will endure as a cornerstone of the Club’s modern history. | am delighted that Peter will
remain as a Director of the Club and fully deserves his appointment to President of the Club.

Rugby Performance

The 2024/25 season marked a significant step forward for the team on the field. Under the leadership of Head
Coach Michael Cheika and his dedicated coaching team - including Peter Hewat (attack), Matt Everard/ David
Kidwell (defence), Dan Palmer (scrum), Brett Deacon (forwards), and Matt Parr (athletic performance) - the
squad rediscovered its competitive edge and identity.

After finishing 8th in the Premiership in 2023/24, the team responded superbly, securing 2nd place in the regular
season and progressing through to the Premiership Final at Twickenham. The semi-final victory over Sale Sharks
at Mattioli Woods Welford Road, sealed by a thrilling long-range try from Izaia Perese, was a reminder of the
attacking spark and resilience that define this Club. Although we were narrowly beaten by Bath in the Final, the
performance across the season demonstrated that Leicester Tigers are once again among the leading forces in
the Premiership.

We must also acknowledge the immense contribution of two club stalwarts, Ben Youngs and Dan Cole, who
retired at the conclusion of the season. Both players embodied the values of Leicester Tigers, serving the Club
and country with distinction. Their leadership, professionalism and connection with supporters leave a lasting
legacy. At the same time, it has been exciting to see the emergence of younger players such as Emeka ilione,
who scored in the Final, and other Academy graduates continuing to make their mark at the highest level. The
blend of experienced internationals, new recruits, and homegrown talent provides a strong platform for the
future. The 2024/25 season also represented an important step in the growth and development of our Leicester
Tigers Women’s team, competing in the Premiership Women’s Rugby competition.

In only their second season at this level, the squad continued to demonstrate resilience, ambition and clear
progress. Under the guidance of the coaching staff, the team recorded several notable victories and showed
consistent improvement against established opposition. Equally important has been the growth in matchday
support, with attendances rising steadily and the women’s team firmly embraced by the Tigers Family. The
pathway for women and girls in rugby across the region continues to expand, with increased participation in
community programmes and academy structures. Our commitment remains to provide opportunities at every
stage of the player journey, from grassroots through to the elite game, ensuring that Leicester Tigers Women
become a consistent force in the Premiership and a source of pride for our supporters.

Coaching Transition

Michael Cheika made a considerable impact in his single season at the helm. His energy, tactical clarity and
ability to galvanise the squad were instrumental in our resurgence. While we respect his decision to step down
for family reasons, we are grateful for his contribution in restoring momentum and confidence.

Looking ahead, we are delighted to welcome Geoff Parling as our new Head Coach. Geoff knows this Club
intimately, having represented Leicester Tigers with distinction as a player, and he brings extensive coaching
experience from the international stage. His appointment provides continuity of culture, combined with fresh
ideas and a long-term commitment to the development of our squad and playing philosophy.

Financial & Commercial Performance

The financial year has seen Leicester Tigers build resilience and strength, supported by outstanding backing from
our supporters and partners. Season ticket sales remained among the highest in the Premiership, while matchday
attendances averaged well above league standards, reinforcing our position as one of the best-supported rugby
clubs in Europe.

We also secured several new commercial partnerships and extended existing agreements, which underlines

the appeal of the Leicester Tigers brand and the confidence that corporate partners place in our future. These
partnerships, combined with strong retail and hospitality performance, have contributed positively to the Club’s
revenues.

At the same time, we remain focused on financial discipline. The Board continues to monitor costs carefully to
ensure we balance investment in rugby performance and facilities with the need to operate sustainably. The
progress of 2024/25 demonstrates that strong financial management and sporting ambition can work hand in
hand.

Community & Pathway Development

Leicester Tigers are more than a professional rugby club - we are at the heart of the community. During the
year, our Foundation and community teams continued their outstanding work across the region, delivering
programmes that engage young people, promote inclusivity, and use rugby as a vehicle for education and
wellbeing.

The Academy system remains central to our strategy. Developing homegrown players is not only essential for

Emeka Ilione is a testament to the effectiveness of our pathway and the commitment of our Academy coaches.
Continued investment in this area will ensure a pipeline of talent for the senior squad in the years ahead.

With the appointment of Geoff Parling as Head Coach, a strong commercial base, and an engaged supporter
community, the Club is well placed to build on the successes of 2024/25.

Rugby Income

During the season, we welcomed over 200,000 supporters to Mattioli Woods Welford Road, with the Gallagher
Premiership games leading the way with attendance numbers. In a nail biting and close season at the top of the table,
our final scheduled home game of the season against Newcastle achieved a sell out attendance, with the performance
on the pitch not disappointing our loyal fans as we secured the home play off against Sale Sharks. This game, not only
delivering success on the pitch and a place in the final at Twickenham, also delivered commercial benefit with a sell out
attendance in the stands and corporate hospitality.

Our loyal Season Ticket Holders, a key part of the clubs foundation, totalled 11,852 (2024: 12,054) generating income
of £3.3m (2024: £3.0m). Whilst the number of season tickets fell by 202, this was lower than the previous years decline
of 586, and we achieved a renewal rate of 96%. Our Season Ticket Holders are incredibly valuable to us and we are
continually reviewing our proposition and game attendance to ensure that we offer excellent value for money for all.

During the year we saw an increase in match by match ticket revenues for league games of £0.4m, predominantly driven
by the home play off against Sale Sharks, an incremental game to the season ticket package. For non-league games,

our revenue remained broadly flat year on year at £0.6m. Total match by match tickets were £3.2m (2024: £2.7m).
Revenues from our women’s games generated less than £0.1m, all generated from match by match attendance, bringing
total ticket sales to £6.6m (2024: £5.8m).

PRL / RFU / PWR Income

The combined distributions from PRL, the RFU and PWR increased by £0.3m from the previous year and whilst RFU

negotiated commercial agreements over which we have no control. With the 6 year Broadcasting deal in place between
TNT and PRL, and the 20 year PGP agreement from the RFU, we do however have certainty of income from both parties
in the medium to long term future.

Costs

Cost pressures outside of our control were faced during the year, notably the business rates for the stadium and
Oval Park, which combined added an additional £0.3m of cost, and is the key driver of administration expenses
increasing to £2.9m (2024: £2.5m).

Underlying staff costs of £15.1m were an increase of £0.8m from the previous year, accounting for annual pay
increases and the impact of the national insurance changes, with the increase heavily weighted to player spend
as we bolstered the squad and welcomed new players to the team. The Women’s team also saw increased
investment albeit the cost base was managed around the reduced length of the Women’s programme from 12
months to 8 months and is the key driver of the average playing and coaching heads decreasing from 106 to 85.
The Board and Senior Leadership Team regularly review the fixed cost base and some minor structural changes
were made during the year across the admin and support stuff, incurring exceptional costs of £0.1m (FY24:
£0.5m), with the average number of heads reducing from 109 to 100.

Financial position

The value of the balance sheet reduced by £2.7m by the end of the year, with net assets decreasing from £52.6m
to £49.9m. The Club however continues to maintain a healthy balance sheet, and continues to have the support
of several key Shareholders, most notably our Chairman, Tom Scott who released additional investment into the
club during the year.

Loans and overdrafts total £17.7m (2024: £17.2m). The loan with HSBC transitioned to interest only payments
for a two year period in December 2023, providing the Club with the much needed support and for which the
Board are very grateful. The loan, overdraft facility, and associated interest have been and continue to be fully
repaid in accordance with agreed terms, with £0.5m of interest being paid in the year. Following a re-profile
request, the loan with DCMS was extended and also transitioned to interest only payments during the year,
providing much welcomed support. Payments made during the year consisted of capital of £185k and interest of
£143k.

Environmental Stewardship

At Leicester Tigers, our commitment to environmental sustainability remains a core pillar of our operational
ethos. We recognise our responsibility not only to our shareholders and fans but also to the environment. Despite
ongoing economic challenges and financial pressures within the rugby industry, we have continued to make
significant strides in our sustainability journey over the past year.

Our club acknowledges the crucial role that businesses play in safeguarding the environment for future
generations. With this in mind, we have persistently pursued sustainable practices that contribute positively to
the world around us. Our achievements this year underscore our dedication to environmental stewardship, even
amidst external constraints.

Building on our previous successes, we have achieved a further 13.7% reduction in energy consumption at our
facilities. This impressive feat was realised through the installation of energy-efficient LED lighting and the
implementation of new control systems for heating and cooling. These measures have significantly enhanced our
energy efficiency, reducing our environmental impact.

Moreover, we are collaborating with Premiership Rugby to utilise a carbon accounting software platform. This
tool will allow us to accurately capture emissions in line with the Greenhouse Gas Protocol, establishing a
comprehensive carbon baseline. This initiative is a crucial step in our ongoing efforts to measure, manage, and
mitigate our carbon footprint.

Our catering partner, Levy, has taken bold steps to lead in sustainable food practices. Recognising that the global
food system contributes significantly to greenhouse gas emissions, they have implemented their ‘recipe for
change’ initiative, which includes:

« Sourcing locally and seasonally to reduce food miles

* Creating balanced plate menus that emphasise plant-based options

« Rebalancing protein sources to favour more sustainable choices

« Offsetting residual emissions to minimise environmental impact

Levy’s “Events for Change” initiative allows event clients to choose sustainably conscious menus, with the
carbon footprint of each dish clearly communicated. This transparency empowers clients to make informed
decisions about the environmental impact of their food choices. Additionally, all desserts served are now plant-
based, leading to a significant reduction in CO2 emissions per dessert from 1.3kg to 0.2kg. Furthermore, the
introduction of seaweed-based, fully recyclable, and home compostable food packaging solutions highlights our
commitment to innovative sustainability.

We have initiated meaningful discussions with club partners on sustainability issues and are planning for
matchday sustainability activations next season.

Risk & uncertainty
The principal risks and uncertainties of the business and the steps taken to mitigate these risks are set out below:

Income generation - the ability to generate income dictates the level of investment the board can make in
facitities as well as the on and off field personnel required to deliver the objectives of the club. A sustained fall
in incame leads to a reduction in the levels of financial resource available for re-investment. The overriding
driver of income for the club is performance on the pitch. The board aims to ensure success on the pitch by
recruiting world class coaching, medical personnel and players as well as providing the best possible training
environment. Off the pitch the board seeks to mitigate the risk to income by entering into long term agreements
with sponsors and other commercial partners. The club’s ticket pricing strategy is competitive and aims to strike
a fair balance between the highest possible attendances and financial return. Ticket prices are reviewed annually
in accordance with market conditions. Broadcasting and certain other revenues are derived from contracts which
are currently centrally negotiated by PRL, and the club does not have any influence, alone, on the outcome of
the various contract negotiations. However, the club, through its membership of various PRL committees fosters a
. strong and open relationship with the PRL executive team and, where possible, both parties work collaboratively
to grow central revenues which are ultimately invested back into the clubs.

above the depreciation charged in the period since the last valuation. Should these trends reverse, or continue
to increase above depreciation charged, then the valuation going forward could be materially different, even
when evaluated under the same basis.

Financial risk management and impairment of financial assets
The group is exposed to risks arising from the use of financial instruments. The following describes the group’s
' objectives, policies and processes for managing those risks and the methods used to measure them.

The principal financial instruments used by the group, from which financial instruments risk arises, are trade
receivables, cash and cash equivalents and other receivables and financial liabilities.

The group is exposed through its operations to the following financial instruments risks: credit risk, liquidity
risk and interest rate risk. The policy for managing these risks is set by the board. The overall objective of
the board is to set policies that seek to reduce the risk as far as possible without unduly affecting the group’s
competitiveness and flexibility. The policy for each of the above risks is described in more detail below.

Credit risk

Credit risk arises from the group’s trade receivables. [t is the risk that the counterparty fails to discharge
their obligation in respect of the instrument. The group is mainly exposed to credit risk from credit sales. It
is the group’s policy to collect all trade receivables prior to the delivery of services. Where credit is extended
beyond the point of delivery, the credit risk of new customers is assessed before entering into contracts. Such
ratings are then factored into the credit assessment process and the appropriate credit term applied for each
customer. The group does not enter into derivatives to manage credit risk.

All cash is held with reputable banks.

Other than the cash held by the group’s bank at 30 June 2025, there are no other significant concentrations of
credit risk within the group at the balance sheet date.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk arises from the group’s management of working capital and the finance charges on its
borrowings. It is the risk that the group will encounter difficulty in meeting financial obligations as they

fall due.

The liquidity of each group company is managed locally and monitored by the board at group level. The level
of the group’s facilities is approved periodically by the board and negotiated with the group’s bankers. At the
balance sheet date, cash flow projections were considered by the board and the group is forecast to have
sufficient funding facilities to meet the group’s obligations as they fall due, under all reasonably expected
circumstances. .
