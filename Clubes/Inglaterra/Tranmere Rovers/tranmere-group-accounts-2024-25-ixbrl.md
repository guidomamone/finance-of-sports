FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.
Fair review of the business
Season 24/25 started with much optimism as the Club had the vastly experienced Nigel Adkins as manager. He was backed by the strongest playing budget in recent years (over 3x the 2014 playing budget when the current ownership acquired the Club). The season started strongly as the team went unbeaten in the league in August, whilst also picking up wins in the opening Carabao Cup and Vertu Trophy fixtures. Despite the positive start, the next few months did not match expectations. By the end of September the team had dropped out of the top half of the league table and by the end of January it was in the bottom three.After a further poor run of form, the decision was made to part ways with the manager. Andy Crosby was appointed to take charge of first team affairs while the Club undertook the process of recruiting a new manager. His impact was immediate, with the team unbeaten in his first three matches and performances showing a marked improvement. This early progress resulted in him being appointed interim manager until the end of the season. The subsequent performances were such that if they were replicated across a full season it would have resulted in automatic promotion. In the event, the Club finished the season nine points clear of relegation. As a consequence Andy Crosby was appointed as manager on a permanent basis.Financial review Turnover fell slightly compared to prior year to circa £5.8 million (23/24: £6.0m) primarily due to the loss of Liverpool Women playing at Prenton Park. However, the Club experienced strong revenue growth in day-to-day operations across all of ticketing, commercial, catering, retail and education, despite on pitch performance being poor, which is a testament to the staff working hard behind the scenes and the loyalty of the fan base.The start of the new EFL TV deal was worth an additional £0.3m in 24/25, although the changes in regulations around the domestic streaming of matches resulted in a fall in streaming revenues of £0.1m. Despite the £0.2m fall in overall revenues, the decision was taken to increase the Club’s playing budget by £0.7m in order to improve the team's competitiveness in anticipation of an imminent sale of the Club. As a result the Group’s cash operating loss widened to £1.7m (24/25: £0.7m). Unfortunately, the 24/25 season demonstrated how an increased playing budget does not always lead to an improvement in on pitch performance and the revenues that would usually be associated with that. The Club therefore continues to identify new football performance agnostic opportunities for revenue growth, particularly in the commercial and international departments.Balance SheetThe Group has a net asset position of £14.7m (23/24: £16.7m). The reduction in net assets reflects the cash operating loss of circa £1.8m for the Club (£1.7m for the Group), non-cash items (largely depreciation) of circa £0.6m and improvements to working capital of circa £0.4m, offset by £0.3m of equity injections. Net current assets include deferred income in relation to season tickets of £0.6m as well as partnership and sponsorship deferred income of £0.2m for season 25/​26. The net assets as at the balance sheet date are net of a provision for deferred tax of £1.8m. The Club chooses not to reflect in the balance sheet contingent assets related to sell on clauses in respect of former players.Season ticketsThe total number of season tickets sold for season 2024/​25 was 3,704, a slight increase on the previous season’s 3,549. Approximately 41% of those purchasing season tickets took advantage of the Club's unique interest free funding option.The Club is hugely grateful for the strong and committed fan base who have continued to purchase season tickets, despite the tough economic climate and a disappointing season. CommunityServing the community has always been at the heart of Tranmere’s strategy. The Club remains an effective local delivery vehicle for community support, enabled by an excellent level of co-operation between the Club, Tranmere Rovers in the Community (the Club’s associated charitable foundation) and key partners and sponsors including Wirral Borough Council and Essar Oil UK Limited.

Fair review of the business - continued
The Club continues to put community work at the heart of what it does, both through its own efforts and working closely with Tranmere Rovers in the Community (TRIC). During the 2024/​25 season the Club and TRIC had over 34,000 engagements across its community activities and have now provided over 25,000 tickets to families who would otherwise not have the opportunity to attend a football match. The current economic climate means that deliveries of food hampers to those in need has continued throughout much of the year, and the Club’s Super White Christmas activities took place once again, to ensure that vulnerable groups such as the economically disadvantaged, people experiencing homelessness and those who are simply lonely were all included in a range of festive activities across the Club.The Club continues to work with many groups, including those living with dementia, those recovering from addiction and those with additional needs, as well as hundreds of young people and adults who benefit from a whole range of sporting and other activities designed to ensure their physical and mental wellbeing.Tranmere Rovers Women and Girls teams saw success in 2024/​25 at all levels, with silverware for the development team in the Liverpool Women’s & Youth League Division One and for the U16’s and U14’s in the County Cup. It is encouraging to see talented young players breaking into the first team through the player pathway.Future developments / post balance sheet date reviewThe principal aim of the directors remains to ensure that the Club is financially resilient and able to achieve on-field success without being reliant on unsustainable annual external investment. However, the continued material year-on-year increases in player wages required to remain competitive in League Two provides a challenging environment. As has been seen, the increase in the playing budget to the highest the Club has ever had in season 24/25 (and then higher again in 25/26) does not guarantee on-field success.To be competitive, the Club must continue to increase the capacity to generate income which is not impacted by on-field performance to the same extent as has previously been the traditional model for a football club. The Club has started to implement its plans to regenerate the recreation centre and Legends Lounges at Prenton Park with the benefit of the support of Wirral Borough Council. There are also significant opportunities in the international market for inbound programmes and there is scope to expand the range of sponsors, with “Club 1884” having been launched in June 2025 to better engage local businesses.Longer term projects include the expansion of the Campus and the potential for a new stadium as an integral part of the regeneration of the Wirral. As has been previously indicated, the current ownership recognises the need for further investment and has highlighted all of the above projects to prospective investors, reflecting the inherent potential of the football club.In Autumn 2025, an exclusivity agreement was reached with new investors with the intention that they should be in place ahead of the January transfer market. This exclusivity agreement was extended in January 2026. Whilst the transaction has taken longer than the desired timeline, at the date of this report, the investors have agreed the purchase of the Club. In the event that the current party is unable to complete a takeover, the Club will re-engage the numerous parties who have expressed a serious interest previously.Closing statementsEveryone at the Club shares the supporters’ frustration with how the 24/25 season panned out and are now focussed on achieving the best possible outcome for the 25/26 season whilst ensuring a swift and smooth transition to new custodians of the Club.The directors have carried out extensive reviews of a variety of scenarios and are satisfied that the Club has sufficient resources to meet all its contractual liabilities for the foreseeable future.The Chairman, Vice Chairman and the Board would like to take this opportunity to express their thanks to the fans, staff, Associate Directors, Tranmere Rovers in the Community, Tranmere Rovers Official Supporters Club, and the Club's corporate partners for their incredible support throughout a difficult period.
