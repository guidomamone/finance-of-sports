BROADCAST REVENUE

Total broadcast revenue has increased by £0.9m due to an uplift in the international merit prize money per place (merit award based on final league place finish) netted against slight overall reductions in both the UK and International TV revenue, which are distributed evenly between all the clubs.

50% of the domestic TV revenue is allocated to clubs based on the number of times they are selected for live domestic broadcast in the UK (facility fee) and their final Premier League position (merit payment). The Club was selected for live domestic broadcast on 22 occasions during the 2022/23 season (2021/22: 22) generating £19.2m in facility fees (2021/22: £19.2m). The Club's 17th-place finish generated £6.8m in UK merit payments, which was £1.7m lower than the 16th-place finish in 2021/22 generated (2021/22: £8.4m).

The remainder of the domestic TV revenue is shared equally between each club and £31.2m has been recognised in year ended 2023, which is a slight reduction with the 2021/22 season of £31.8m.

International TV revenue is recognised in two parts, with the largest portion being shared equally by all clubs representing £48.8m (£0.9m reduction on 2021/22: £49.7m). The remainder is distributed on a merit-based system according to the league position finish, with £5.7m recognised for the 17th-place finish (2021/22 season: £1.8m, 16th-place).

GATE RECEIPTS

Gate receipts revenue of £17.3m was generated from 19 Premier League matches and gate receipts from three away cup ties. The Club progressed to the third round of the EFL Cup and exited the FA Cup in the third round. The £15.6m in the 2021/22 season was generated from 19 Premier League matches, two home cup ties and four away cup ties. In the prior season, the Club progressed to the third round of the EFL Cup and achieved a quarter-final appearance in the FA Cup. The increase in gate revenue was due to uplift in ticket prices across Season Tickets.

The Club continues to benefit from unwavering support from its supporters. The Club is extremely grateful for the continued strong Season Ticket sales and renewal rate seen at the end of the year ended 30 June 2023. Despite the Club making available the maximum permissible number of Season Tickets for sale under Premier League rules, demand for Season Tickets has, once again, significantly outstripped the available supply and the Club continues to operate a Season Ticket waiting list for supporters wishing to purchase a seat in the future.

SPONSORSHIP AND COMMERCIAL ACTIVITIES

The Club's sponsorship, advertising and merchandising revenue totalled £19.2m, which was £15.8m lower than achieved in the 2021/22 season (£35.0m). The movement relates to the removal of USM as the Club's Training Ground Sponsor (USM revenue recognised in the prior period in full) netted against sponsorship uplifts on renewals and new sponsorship agreements (welcomed Stake, BOXT, Socios, Christopher Ward, Marc Darcy to the portfolio). The Club took the decision in March 2022 to suspend all commercial sponsorship arrangements with the Russian companies USM, Megafon and Yota.

The suspension of these commercial sponsorship arrangements remained in place throughout the remainder of the previous and this financial year.

The Club's other commercial revenue totalled £19.7m, which was an uplift of £4.4m on that achieved in the 2021/22 season (£15.3m). The uplifts relate to increases in Premier League central sponsorship revenues, increased income generated from Club tours (USA and Australia) and income received from UEFA for players participating in the 2022 World Cup.

OPERATING EXPENSES
(EXCLUDING PLAYER AND MANAGEMENT TRADING)

Operating expenses (excluding player and management trading) increased to £213.1m (2021/22: £205.5m), which includes exceptional costs of £3.2m (2021/22: £0.6m). The overall increase of £7.6m represents the increased costs associated with attending both pre and mid-season tours, adverse movements in foreign exchange rates, Premier League retention bonuses for the new coaching staff taken on in the year and increased new stadium operational expenditure.

The Club's total wage to turnover ratio has increased from 90% in 2021/22 to 92% in 2022/23. The Club looks to reduce the wage to turnover ratio going forward, whilst also ensuring that the First-Team squad remains as competitive as possible. As in previous years, the ongoing outsourcing of the Club's retail and catering operations, which reduces turnover (and costs) when comparing to other clubs who manage these functions in-house, also results in an artificially inflated wage to turnover ratio. The Club's total wage to turnover ratio would increase accordingly from 87% in 2021/22 to 89% in 2022/23 if retail and catering operations were not outsourced.

The Club has once again committed significant funds to the development of its new stadium, with the Club incurring capital cost of £210.9m (2021/22: £154.4m).

As a result of the above, the Club made an operating loss before player and management trading of £40.9m (2021/22: £24.5m), excluding player trading.

PLAYER AND MANAGEMENT TRADING

Under the FRS 102 accounting standard, the Club is required to capitalise the cost of acquiring a player's registration (transfer and agent fees) and then amortise it over the length of the player's contract, effectively reducing the balance sheet value of a player over that time. No increase in the valuation of a player is permitted until that player is sold and a revised value is crystallised in the profit and loss account through a one-off profit or loss on disposal.

The Club continued to commit significant investment into its playing squad during the 2022/23 season, spending £91.5m on additions, which represented an uplift of £36.5m (2021/22: £55m). This resulted in an amortisation charge of players' registrations of £77.6m during 2022/23 (2021/22: £68.3m).

The Club recognised an operating exceptional cost of £7.1m, which was incurred for amounts payable in relation to a change in coaching staff. The Club also recognised an exceptional impairment charge of £4.8m (2021/22: £nil) in relation to player registrations.

Profit on the disposal of player registrations was £47.5m, a decrease of £20.2m on the previous year. The prior period included the disposals of players such as Richarlison, Lucas Digne and Moise Kean compared to the disposals of Anthony Gordon, Nathan Broadhead, Ellis Simms and various contingent receipts in the current period. It should be noted that the balance sheet value of the Club's playing squad of £144.5m at 30 June 2023, as calculated under FRS 102, was significantly lower than the insured value of the squad at the same point in time.

After player and management trading, the Club generated a loss before interest and taxation of £82.9m (2022: net loss £35.6m).

After interest and taxation, the Club recorded a loss for the year of £89.1m (2021/22: loss of £38.3m).

### 20. PENSIONS

Certain staff of the Group are members of either the Football League Limited Players Retirement Income Scheme, a defined contribution scheme, or the Football League Limited Pension and Life Assurance Scheme ("FLLPLAS"; "the Scheme"), a defined benefit scheme. As one of a number of participating employers in the FLLPLAS, the Group is advised only of its share of the Scheme's deficit and recognises a liability in respect of this.

As a result, the contributions paid to the scheme reduce the provision. The Group is unable to identify its share of the underlying assets and liabilities of the Scheme on a consistent and reliable basis and therefore accounts for the Scheme as if it were a defined contribution scheme.

Contributions are also paid into individuals' private pension schemes. Total contributions across all schemes during the year amounted to £1,163,000 (2022: £873,000). The amount outstanding at year end was £146,000 (2022: £80,000).

### 21. POST BALANCE SHEET EVENTS

Since 30 June 2023, the Club has entered into transfer agreements to acquire the registration of Norberto Bercique Gomes Betuncal (from Udinese), Youssef Chermiti (from Sporting CP), Jack Harrison (loan from Leeds United), Arnaut Danjuma (loan from Villarreal) and Ashley Young (free agent). At the date of approval of the financial statements, closing of the transaction is expected to occur early in 2024 and remains subject to regulatory approval, including from the Premier League and the Football Association.

On 17 November 2023, an independent commission handed Everton Football Club an immediate 10-point deduction (in season 2023/24) for breaching the Premier League's Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR) for the 2018/19, 2019/20, 2020/21 and 2021/22 financial years. At the date of approval of the financial statements, Everton has since lodged an appeal against this penalty, and are currently awaiting the appeal process to begin. The Club is also aware that the Premier League will charge the Club with a PSR breach for 2020/21, 2021/22 and 2022/23 financial years.
