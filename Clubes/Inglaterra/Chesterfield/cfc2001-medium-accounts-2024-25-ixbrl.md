| CHAIRMAN'S REPORT |
| --- |
| FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025 |
| --- |
| This financial report represents Chesterfield FC's first full season back in the EFL after a six year absence and the first full year since the Kirk family took control of the club. Whilst reaching the play offs and achieving the highest average attendance in many years was a great effort and built on the solid foundations laid in the promotion season, the past year has been overshadowed by the death of my brother Phil. |
| --- |
| I would like to take this opportunity to remind all investors that without the ambition, direction and financial support provided by Phil and his wife Sharon the club would not be celebrating its second season in the Football League and indeed, it probably would not even be in existence. Phil loved the club and was so proud to become the Owner, it is a great shame that he never had the opportunity to see the future years of league football that his investment and passion will bring to our club. The support Phil, Sharon and the whole family has received from the club, the local community and the wider football world has been truly humbling and I would like, on behalf of all of us, to thank everyone for your love and best wishes during what has been a truly terrible period. |
| --- |
| This is my first year as Chairman of Chesterfield FC and both I and the club would like to place on record my thanks to Mike Goodwin who stepped down as chairman after five years but continues to serve on the board as Vice Chairman. The role Mike has played in stabilising the club during one of its most turbulent periods has been immense, I can only hope to come close to matching his calm stewardship of the club, he will be a very difficult act to follow. |
| --- |
| These accounts once again show a considerable operating loss yet a big improvement from last year. Undoubtedly the increased revenues from being members of the EFL have played a large part in the reduction but running an ambitious club in the current football economy is an expensive business. The Owners were comfortable with the level of investment required and continue to support Paul Cook and the football department as we develop and improve the squad. The club losses are underwritten by the continued capital injections by the Kirk family and we are more than comfortable with the debt reduction repayments on our outstanding external loans from the two Councils and Sport England. |
| --- |
| Further capital investment has resulted in an overhaul of the hospitality areas as we continue to modernise the stadium and its facilities. The three lounges are now state of the art and it is a strange but confident boast that we have the finest toilets in hospitality across the EFL. Our challenge will now come in promoting the venues for private hire and club run events whilst navigating the tricky economic conditions affecting the country as a whole. |
| --- |
| The next few months will see the club and football come under government regulation for the first time with the establishment and introduction of the Independent Football Regulator (IFR). Over the next three years, the IFR will fundamentally reshape how English football is governed, with club licensing at the centre of its approach. Established by the Football Governance Act 2025, the IFR's core mission is to strengthen financial sustainability, corporate governance and fan engagement across the top five tiers of men's football, replacing the previous system of self-regulation. |
| --- |
| From the 2026-27 season, the 116 clubs in the Premier League, EFL and National League will begin applying for provisional operating licences. This means that we will be submitting strategic business plans, financial forecasts and evidence of governance and supporter engagement. Clubs will have up to three years to transition to full licences, during which the IFR will supervise progress and may impose bespoke conditions on at-risk clubs to control costs, strengthen liquidity and boost long-term resilience. |
| --- |
| Alongside licensing, the Regulator will enforce enhanced Owners' and Directors' tests, monitor debt-funded takeovers more closely, and increase transparency about clubs' financial risk profiles. This regime aims to protect clubs' heritage, safeguard fans' interests, and prevent insolvencies that have historically destabilised the English game. The board welcomes this approach and hopes that it will lead to greater prudence amongst the clubs that we compete within the league. Chesterfield FC has already reached out to the Regulator offering to be a pilot club with a view to getting our governance, financial controls and processes in line with the regulators requirements at the earliest opportunity. One thing that we should all note is that this in no way guarantees more money from the Premier League to clubs at our level. |

| STRATEGIC REPORT |
| --- |
| FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025 |
| --- |
| The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025. |
| --- |
| The 2024/25 financial year saw Chesterfield FC return to the Football League for the first time in six seasons. While this was welcome and came with an increase in revenue, particularly from the football authorities, it did come with challenges for the business. |
| --- |
| We also found out about the health concerns of the majority shareholder, Phil Kirk, and tragically he died shortly after the end of the year. The reaction in the football and energy world, and particularly among Chesterfield supporters, showed the esteem in which he was held. |
| --- |
| REVIEW OF BUSINESS |
| --- |
| The team seemed to take the step-up in its stride and flirted with the automatic promotion spots before an injury crisis around Christmas threatened to derail any thought of promotion. Fourteen of the first-team squad were unavailable for the busy holiday period as the team struggled to fill a full quota of substitutes. One of the Academy players became the second ever youngest debutant in the club's history as a result. |
| --- |
| As players returned from injury, there was a strong finish to the season. With only one defeat in the last quarter of the term the team rallied from sixteenth to make the play-offs at the last match of the season. Unfortunately, the semi-final against Walsall was a step too far in the first season back. |
| --- |
| Attendances again continued to be remarkable. A decision was made to restrict visiting supporters to the north wing of the East Stand, this 1061 seats met EFL requirements but meant that home supporters exceeded eight thousand for eleven league games and the play-off match. Twice there were crowds of more than ten thousand with the average of almost 8500 being a 55-year record. Season ticket sales again slightly increased to record numbers for 2025/26 and while the average for crowds has dropped marginally it is still well over eight thousand. |
| --- |
| The injury issues last season meant that a real appraisal of the playing staff was difficult. Steps have been taken in the medical department to investigate patterns injuries and a more robust protocol for pre-signing medicals has been implemented. The latter resulted in the first two potential signings being cancelled. |
| --- |
| At the start of this season several signings were made of players who might have been expected to be competing at a higher level. It is clear that the status of Paul Cook helps in bringing talent to the club. The board has laid down a strategy to decrease the age of the squad and sign resaleable players who are developing. This summers recruits fit this strategy. |
| --- |
| The return to the Football League brought extra layers of governance and financial reporting. While the safeguarding protocols were established immediately those around Equality, Diversity and Inclusion are still being developed; there is no suggestion that there are any issues with this at the club. The stringent controls around the Salary Cost Management Protocol change from season to season and we will now also have to work with the new Independent Football Regulator (IFR). The IFR's core objectives will be to protect and promote the financial soundness of regulated football clubs, protect and promote financial resilience of English football and safeguard the heritage of English football. The club welcomes the introduction of this regulator. |
| --- |
| With Sharon Kirk becoming the effective owner of Chesterfield FC, the board has instigated a new strategic plan for the club. This particularly addresses the expected financial shortfalls that are bridged by the owner to ensure a financially viable model. |
| --- |
| Work continues to develop the training ground at Hasland; this is important for the squad and in recruiting new players. While the pitches are now first-rate, the other facilities need to be replaced with a new building. The club is committed to this development as part of Phil Kirk's legacy. |
| --- |
| The Academy continues to be a hybrid model. A category 4 youth team only status but supported by scores of other young players on education-based schemes. Despite having only year-one scholars the youth team finished seventh of twenty-five in the Football League Youth Alliance. The young footballers almost exclusively made up the members of the reserve team that held its own in the Central League. As well as one player signing on professional terms on his seventeenth birthday, another has featured in the first-team and several regularly train with the first-team squad. |

| PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES |
| --- |
| The death of Phil Kirk, as well as being a tragedy for his family and friends, had to be identified as a threat to the business he owned. Sharon Kirk has pledged to continue with her husbands vision to provide a legacy for Phil and has made commitments around the future of the club. |
| --- |
| With the country, and the wider world, still faced with economic uncertainty, there remains a threat to businesses in the leisure industry. While there have been concessions from the government around some of the hospitality sector, it is not clear whether these will benefit events at the stadium. The work to renovate the lounges that has been championed by Sharon Kirk has made the stadium the premier hospitality and business event venue in North Derbyshire. The feedback about the changes has been strong and it is hoped that demand will increase both for our own events and for externally booked functions. |
| --- |
| In football it is what happens on the field that generates most of the risks. This includes commercial activities and to a lesser extent non-football business. Everyone knows the rewards that promotion to the League meant a similar step-up in funding and quality of football can follow if the team is successful again. Football is also an industry where as well as budgets, cashflow is a significant factor. |
| --- |
| FINANCIAL KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS |
| --- |
| The major ongoing cost in football is the playing budget. Clearly there will always be a balance between ensuring that the team is competitive and able to challenge for promotion as well as being financially viable. The new strategy should help to firstly monitor this and move the business to a stronger position. |
| --- |
| In terms of judging performance, we expect to see the turn-over rise each year. However, any rise should be generated by increased income. We are looking at more business away from matchdays in the renovated lounges and greater commercial opportunities that the club's profile allows. The primary business is football so keeping our season ticket numbers around the record levels is important. Match-by-match sales have reduced a little, probably as the national economy suffers. Strategies are being established to engage more of the local community and to push the attraction of the club and local football to a wider geographical area. |
