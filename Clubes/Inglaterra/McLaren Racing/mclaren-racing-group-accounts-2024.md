Strategic report

Principal activities

The principal activity of McLaren Racing Limited (the “Company”) and its subsidiaries: (collectively the
“Group”) during the year was that of participating in a number of motor racing series throughout the world,
most notably in Formula One (“F1”). This includes the design, development, manufacture and racing of
’ Formula One cars. During the year the Group also participated in IndyCar, Extreme E, Formula E and
Esports.

Business review
Racing season and commercial review

2024 saw McLaren Racing Limited reclaim the Formula One Constructors’ Championship, marking a
triumphant return to the top after 26 years. The season’s success was a testament to the hard work of the
entire team, supported by our valued partners and passionate fans. It also demonstrated the team’s
remarkable progress in recent seasons, rising from 4th position in 2023 to 1st in 2024.

Success extended across other racing series as well. In IndyCar, Pato O’Ward secured three race wins
while in Formula E, Sam Bird delivered the team’s first-ever victory in Sao Paulo.

As a Group, we have seen revenue increase from £431,082K to £530,314K primarily as a result of increased
commercial activity after a strong season of on track performance.

The strong revenue growth has resulted in an operating profit of £57,601K (2023: £30,498K), whilst net
interest expense of £20,065K (2023: £18,907K) is broadly flat year on year resulting in a profit before tax of
£37,536K (2023: £11,591K).

The net profit figure translated to a consolidated cash and cash equivalents balance at year end of
£237,417K (2023: £138,942K) from increased cash inflow from operating activity, largely from increased
deferred income, offset against increased financing activities including £40,000K repayment of loan notes
in February 2024. , ,

The McLaren Racing Limited standalone financial statements showed revenue of £488,393K (2023:
£386,968K). This again is primarily due to the strength in sponsorship revenue. The strength in revenue
flowed through to an operating profit of £48,462K, (2023: profit of £24,892K).

In December 2024, the Group acquired the remaining 25% of McLaren Indy LLC to take 100% ownership.
investment in McLaren Racing Limited ,

Since December 2020, McLaren Racing Limited has had a long-term investment led by MSP Sports Capital
(“MSP”), who co-invested with its strategic partners UBS O’Connor, LLC. and The Najafi Companies, whilst
an additional tranche of funding was provided by UBS O’Connor LLC, the investment group Caspian and
Ares Capital (ASME Holdings |, LP).

The investment amount is in the form of convertible loan notes and warrants, valued at £179,000K at
December 2024 (2023: £194,700K). Under the arrangement, the Company has the option to draw down an
additional amount of £15,000K (2023: £15,000K) up to 31 December 2025. The warrants are convertible
at any time at the option of the warrant holders, for £0.01, into equity shares of the Company. The warrants
have given the investors voting and dividend rights on an ‘as-converted’ basis. As at 31 December 2024
the investors cumulatively held 29.25% (2023: 29.25%), in the Company and Group on a diluted basis.

Principal risks and uncertainties
Competitive & operational risks

As ever the key direct risks and uncertainties faced by the business are continuing to be competitive in all
operations, ability to gain and retain sponsorship, control of the cost base of developing the competitive cars
and staff recruitment and retention.

A Group wide risk register is maintained with direct input from across the business. This aims to highlight
relevant risks and provide focus for mitigations and contingency planning, with emerging issues being
escalated to the Board for discussion.

Financial risk management

The Group is primarily funded through prize money and its sponsorship agreements, and the risks around
these are determined largely by on track performance. The risks are managed by optimising results through
investing in both research and development of the car, the team that design, build & race the car and through
signing medium and long-term agreements with key partners and sponsors.

Cash flows are carefully budgeted and controlled by the Directors to ensure there is sufficient liquidity within
the business to operate through both the short and long-term.

The Group has foreign currency dealings in USD, Euro, SAR and multiple other currencies. Key transactions
are reviewed by the Board who agree policies and approaches to manage associated risks around
fluctuating FX rates for our prize fund and foreign denominated sponsorship income. The hedging policy
agreed by the Board is to give a level of certainty over the foreign exchange fluctuations. This is assessed
regularly by the Board, with hedging arrangements in place to mitigate against the biggest risks, whilst
balancing that the FX rates have been incredibly volatile in 2024 and therefore not always produced a
favourable result.

Environmental and social sustainability overview

Our approach to sustainability is shaped to deliver on the Group’s vision, mission, strategic goals and values.
Everything we do feeds into our vision to be the most pioneering and exhilarating racing team in the world.
Since 2022 we have focused our work on achieving our goals and our mission — to set the standard for high
performance in sport — in the context of sustainability.

Within the Group’s operational control, we are minimising our environmental impacts by investing in
innovation such as low-carbon fuels, circular materials and waste-reducing processes. There are also
elements which the Group cannot directly control, such as the increasing number of races in the F1 calendar
and the boundaries of the sports’ regulations. To manage these indirect impacts, we use our influence to
engage with championship organisers, regulators, race promoters and other stakeholders to raise.
awareness and collaborate towards building a more climate-resilient sport.

McLaren Racing Limited publishes an Annual Sustainability Report in the spring of each year, where it
reports on its sustainability strategy and progress in detail.

The Group and Company have a history of strong environmental commitments. McLaren was the first F1
team to be awarded the Carbon Trust Standard for carbon management in 2010 and has retained this (or
equivalent standards) since, most recently obtaining assurance against 15014064 for 2022 and 2023
calendar years. The team was also the first in F1 to be awarded the FIA Environmental Accreditation at the
highest available level (three-star) in 2013; the award is renewed every two years, most recently in 2024,
and is a requirement to compete in F1. The Group is a signatory to the UN Sports for Climate Action
framework since 2021, regularly attending climate working groups in the sports industry and publishing a
Climate Transition Action Plan in 2023 in support of this initiative. In 2024, McLaren Racing Limited became
the first F1 Team to have GHG emissions targets validated by the Science-Based Targets Initiative (SBTi),
in line with its ambitious goal of reaching net-zero emissions by 2040.

Sustainability governance

McLaren Racing Limited has sustainability governance structures in place at leadership, management and
operational levels of the business, up to and including the Board. The purpose. of these structures is to
maintain governance and oversight of sustainability projects and investments, assess and manage climate
and sustainability risks, maintain awareness among leadership, and facilitate embedding good sustainability
practice across the Company.

McLaren Racing Limited is also an active member of several external groups which govern or manage
sustainability within motorsport. The Group engages with these external bodies to influence sustainability
elements outside its direct control, and to champion sustainability progress within the motorsport industry.

The McLaren Racing Board provides strategic oversight and decision-making on sustainability including
priority setting, risk management and opportunity realisation. The Board meets quarterly, and sustainability
is a standing agenda item in Board meetings, where progress is reported on by the Company CEO.

The McLaren Racing Executive Team provides strategic leadership on sustainability and reports via the
CEO to the Company's Board of Directors. The Chief People & Sustainability Officer is a member of this
group.

The McLaren Racing Sustainability Steering Committee meets monthly and oversees and facilitates
delivery of the sustainability strategy and its integration into business activities, processes, decision-making
and management. This group governs sustainability risks, opportunities and strategic decisions at the
managerial level. It is attended by the Executive Team along with various Directors from across the
business, and is chaired by the Director of Sustainability. Progress against climate targets is measured and
reviewed annually in line with the Group’s external sustainability reporting cadence, and the Steering
Committee are kept regularly updated on targets ahead of Sustainability Report publication.

The McLaren Advisory Team (MAT) is a group of trusted global business leaders who represent a cross-
section of sectors. The MAT meets quarterly to provide McLaren Racing Limited and its corporate partners
with strategic insights and expertise, which extends to the topics of corporate sustainability.

Climate risk assessment
McLaren. Racing Limitéd’s portfolio of climate-related risks is broadly divided into two categories:

1. Transition risks, which represent changes in market, economic and policy contexts as a result of
climate change. These could include proliferation and tightening of regulations, technological
advancements, and changes to consumer and market preferences.

2. Physical risks, which represent damages, losses or operational impacts caused by extreme
weather events (such as heat, cold, flooding, drought, storms, wildfires, etc.), and which are likely
to worsen in frequency and severity with climate change. .

McLaren Group Limited maintains a risk register which covers climate change and sustainability topics within
its scope. This register is prepared, maintained and regularly updated by the Group Head of Risk, and
presented for approval to the Audit and Risk Committee, which meets quarterly.

Risks are assigned a likelihood rating based on frequency within a defined time frame, from highly unlikely
(50 year horizon) to almost certain (one year). Risks logged in the risk register which have no mitigating
controls or cations in place, or risks which remain major in spite of mitigations, are raised directly to the
Board as key climate-related transition risks. Individual climate-related risks are not currently assessed for
financial impact.

Risks are categorised by thematic area, with sustainability being one category of risk, spanning both social
and environmental thematic areas. As at December 2024, two risks were identified as climate-related key
risks in the transition risk category, which were raised to the Board. Physical risks are monitored and
mitigated where necessary, however currently no physical climate-related risks are categorised as key risks
in the risk register. Facilities risks at site level relating to the McLaren Campus and McLaren Technology
Centre, including risks relating specifically to climate resilience, property and business interruption, are
assessed by the Group through its risk and insurance processes. Currently no such risks are escalated to
the Board.

Limited. As of 2024 the Group incorporates ESG and Sustainability into its internal audit program. An internal

sustainability strategy and goals.

Physical climate risks

Whilst physical climate risks outside of the Group’s operational control are not currently noted as material
risks in the risk register, McLaren Racing Limited continues to monitor and mitigate risks on this topic.

The global nature of the World Championships we compete in, alongside the range of weather patterns and
available mitigation measures at different locations, exposes motorsport championships and race events,
and therefore the Group in its ability to participate in them, to a range of physical climate risks.

The makeup and order of race calendars is not directly within the Group’s control; this sits with the
championship organisers, whilst location-specific mitigation measures are managed by race promoters and
the tracks themselves. As such, the Group’s primary mitigation measure for this risk is engagement with
these stakeholders through the external governance structures mentioned in the prior section of this Report,
and through maintaining a strong network of peers across other racing teams and organisations.

‘ Environmental metrics and targets

The Group measures its annual greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in line with the GHG Protocol Corporate
Standard. This calculation covers Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions, including all relevant Scope 3 categories.

In April 2022, the Group publicly committed to setting near and long-term science-based targets in line with
keeping global average temperatures within 1.5 degrees above pre-industrial levels and having these
validated by the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi). These targets were approved by the SBTi in
February 2024, andthe full wording of these is elaborated below. For the purposes of our climate targets,
we define our near-term targets as 2030, and long-term as 2040 and beyond.

Our Near-Term Targets:
We commit to:

e reduce absolute scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions 80% by 2030 from a 2019 base yeart;
increase active annual sourcing of renewable electricity from 97.7% in 2022 to 100% by 2030; and
reduce absolute scope 3 GHG emissions from purchased goods and services, capital goods, °
upstream transportation and distribution, and business travel 50% by 2030 from a 2022 base year.

Our Long-Term Targets:
We commit to

e reduce absolute scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions 90% by 2040 from a 2019 base yeart; and
e reduce absolute scope 3 GHG emissions from purchased goods and services, capital goods,
upstream transport & distribution, and business travel 90% by 2040 from a 2022 base year.

+ The target boundary includes land-related emissions and removals from bioenergy feedstocks.

As of its 2023 Annual Sustainability Report, the Group began tracking a GHG intensity metric, expressed
as GHG emissions per race, broken down by racing series. The primarily intention of this is to have a
consistent measure of GHG emissions, which allows for business growth since the company set its baseline
figures. McLaren’s entry into new racing series, its expansion to two new sites (McLaren Racing Composites
and Bicester Heritage), and the growth of the Formula 1 event calendar have all resulted in increased
absolute emissions attributable to business growth.

These factors complicate like-for-like emission comparisons against the Group’s absolute emissions
baseline, whereas the per-race intensity metric provides a normalised view. Whilst it does not have a formal
target set against it, this intensity metric provides an alternate perspective on decarbonisation progress
alongside absolute emissions.

Progress against targets

McLaren Racing Limited’s initial emission reduction focus has been on energy efficiency at its UK
headquarters, switching to biofuels for road logistics, reducing freight weight, moving freight from air to sea
where possible, and engaging with key suppliers and corporate partners to realise opportunities to reduce
emissions from its supply chain, including through the purchase of Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF)
certificates.

The business plan considers the costs of achieving the Group’s targets in the short term, including budgeting
for critical decarbonisation initiatives in operations, logistics, business travel and supply chain. Investments
to date include purchasing of Sustainable Aviation Fue! credits (SAFc) and carbon removal credits to signal
demand to the market for low-carbon fuels, and deployment of HVO biofuel for the Group’s logistics
operations. The Group expects to sustain this investment in emission reduction year-on-year as it
progresses towards its near-term and net-zero climate targets. Unpredictability of prices for low-carbon
fuels, carbon removal and related credits present a risk to this strategy; the Group controls this risk by
securing supply of credits through multi-year partnerships. Whilst long-term forecasting of costs to our net-
zero target is more difficult, the Board are committed to the initiatives required to meet long-term targets.

Energy efficiency and emissions reductions

In December 2023 a 20,000L biofuel storage tank was installed on-site at the McLaren Technology Centre,
enabling all diesel-powered vehicles (primarily operated road freight trucks and vans, but also supplier
vehicles) leaving the site to be fuelled with hydrotreated vegetable oil (HVO). This has resulted in significant
reductions in GHG emissions from operated road logistics and transport, which the Company will measure
and report in its 2024 Sustainability Report.

Across its Woking sites, the Group procures 100% renewable electricity sourced from wind, solar and
hydroelectric generation. This electricity sourcing is evidenced by Renewable Energy Guarantees of Origin
(REGO) certificates, and effectively reduces the Company's Scope 2 emissions on a market basis.

Since a significant portion of the Company’s Scope 3 emissions result from aviation (spread across.transport
& distribution and business travel), in 2024 it has invested in Sustainable Aviation Fue! credits (SAFc) to
compensate these emissions. The objective of purchasing SAFc is to financially support the development
and production of SAF, send a demand signal to the aviation fuel market, and to reduce the overall GHG
intensity of global aviation activity. Further details will be disclosed in the 2024 Sustainability Report.

The Company’s focus for decarbonisation in 2025 will be establishing a supplier sustainability engagement
programme, since reducing supply chain emissions will be critical to meeting the Company’s near-term and

net-zero emissions targets.

Financial risk management objectives and policies

The Group’s and Company's activities expose it to a number of financial risks including foreign exchange
tisk, credit risk, and liquidity risk. Foreign exchange and liquidity risk are managed by the Group using
detailed cash flow forecasting to highlight and mitigate areas of exposure.

The hedging policy agreed by the Board is to give a level of certainty over the foreign exchange fluctuations.
This is assessed regularly by the Board, with hedging arrangements in place to mitigate against the biggest
risks, whilst balancing that the FX rates have been incredibly volatile in recent years and therefore not
always produced a favourable result.

At each balance sheet date non-financial assets not carried at fair value are assessed to determine whether

previously recognised revaluation. Thereafter any excess is recognised in the profit and loss account.

estimate of its recoverable amount, but only to the extent that the revised carrying amount does not exceed the

Financial assets and liabilities are offset and the net amounts presented in the financial statements when there

(v) | Hedging arrangements

The Group designates certain derivatives as hedging instruments in cash flow hedges to manage the cash flow
exposures of working capital. At the inception of the hedge relationship, the entity documents the economic
relationship between the hedging instrument and the hedged item, along with its risk management objectives
and clear identification of the risk in the hedged item that is being hedged by the hedging instrument.
Furthermore, at the inception of the hedge the Group determines, and documents causes for hedge
ineffectiveness.

For any forwards entered into, changes in the fair values of derivatives designated as cash flow hedges, and
which are effective, are recognised directly in equity. Any ineffectiveness in the hedging relationship (being the
excess of the cumulative change in fair value of the hedging instrument since inception of the hedge over the
cumulative change in the fair value of the hedged item since inception of the hedge) is recognised in the profit
and loss account.

For any options entered into, changes in the fair values of derivatives designated as cash flow hedges, and
which are effective, the intrinsic value of the option is recognised directly in equity. Any change in time value
since the inception of the hedge is recognised in profit or loss.

Hedge accounting is discontinued when the Group revokes the hedging relationship, the hedging instrument
expires or is sold, terminated, or exercised, or no longer qualifies for hedge accounting. Any gain or loss
accumulated in equity at that time is reclassified to profit or loss when the hedged item is recognised in profit or
loss. When forecast transactions are no longer expected to occur, any gain or loss that was recognised in other
comprehensive income is reclassified immediately to profit or loss.
