FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2022
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2022.
PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES
Based at the Twickenham Stoop stadium, the principal activity of the company (the Club) is the operation of Harlequin Football
Club (Harlequins) the professional rugby club. Our men's team participates in the Gallagher Premiership, the European Professional
Club Rugby tournament (EPCR) and the Premiership Rugby Cup. Our women's team participates in the Allianz Premier 15s.
In addition to running our elite rugby teams, Harlequins supports the Harlequins Foundation and significant activities in its local
communities.
THE YEAR IN REVIEW
Rugby
Both the men’s and women's teams entered the 2021/2022 season as champions of their respective leagues, determined to defend
their titles. Unfortunately, both team's title defences ended at the semi-final stage. Our opponents in both semi-finals were
Saracens who won both fixtures in compelling, hard-fought matches.
The men's 2022/23 season is approaching its half-way stage and Harlequins occupy a top four league position. The Women's
league season has had a delayed start due to the Women's Rugby World Cup, held in New Zealand, where Harlequins players
featured strongly in various nations' teams. Harlequins' England representatives did us proud with England losing only narrowly to
New Zealand in the tournament's final.
Operational update
The financial impact of Covid-19 on the Club will continue to be felt for some years to come as we manage increased debt levels
taken on during the pandemic. We remain committed to achieving financial sustainability.
Matchday attendances throughout the 2021/2022 season were exceptional with every men's Gallagher Premiership home game
selling out. This was followed by record demand for 2022/2023 Season Tickets allowing the Club to launch a new Ticket Priority
Pass that enables non-Members to secure seats before general sale. We continue to see strong matchday demand in the current
season and the sale of Big Game 14 tickets in on track to be our most successful event yet.
We made significant investment during the off-season which included the creation of a new expanded fan village area behind the
North Stand and a range of renovations making for a greatly improved match day experience. The works encompass improved
premium bars and food outlets, a seating area designed for live entertainment and a re-vamped club shop. The Jason Leonard
Gate benefits from a new covered entrance, additional Ticket Office window and state-of the-art turnstiles. The results of
post-match fan surveys tell us that these improvements are noticed and appreciated.
At the beginning of the 2022/2023 season, we changed our kit and retail partner to Castore which has provided a new and
refreshed look to our products. The Club also announced a new multi-year partnership with Fuller's London Pride as our new
official beer supplier. Fullers is a local, south-west London brewer with a strong heritage in rugby.
During the year, the Club renewed its partnerships with both DHL and Charles Stanley which shows our continued commercial
strength and security.
The Club acknowledges and values the support it receives from all of its sponsors and financial partners which is key to our
financial future and in our journey to financial sustainability.
Quins in the Community
Our community team is committed to develop the game and the reach of Harlequins through continued support across fifty
community rugby clubs as part of our affiliate club programme as well as engaging with schools and hosting a number of camps.
In May 2022, the Club launched a broad level of community and grassroots rugby engagement at the Big Summer Kick-Off (BSKO)
event, effectively a second Big Game for the club. A fun packed series of pre-match activities saw over one thousand participants,
a curtain raiser to our first ever BSKO at Twickenham Stadium.
Becoming Double Champions in 2021/2022 provided us with an excellent opportunity to increase engagement with our affiliate
clubs and our community team undertook an ambitious trophy tour across our hinterland.

Environmental, Social & Corporate Governance
The Club continues to make every effort to minimise waste and to recycle as much as possible. It aims to minimise the use of
energy resources and reviews and improves operational processes to have the least impact on the environment and implement
measures to reduce the Club's carbon footprint.
Ahead of the 2022/2023 season, the Club announced a new partnership with Lyfecyle to offer a sustainable solution to plastic
pollution when visiting the Stoop. Lyfecycle, offers an innovative solution to prevent plastic pollution through unique
self-destructing plastic technology, built into the new cups fans and visitors will use when attending events at the stadium.
The Club has in place policies to ensure that all employees are treated fairly and specifically to prohibit discrimination on the
grounds of age, disability, religion, sex, nationality and ethnic origin and to ensure health and wellbeing.
For a second year, the Club celebrated Black History Month to grow inclusion and diversity across our Club and the sport and
Harlequins are proud of the level of support and positive engagement from our supporters.
Harlequins is pioneering LGBTQ+ inclusion in rugby, having been the first ever professional rugby Club to host annual Pride
fixtures, as well as being the first rugby union club to have a dedicated LGBTQ+ Supporters' Association. Quins Pride do important
work ensuring that LGBTQ+ rugby fans of any team know that they are welcome and actively supported at Twickenham Stoop.
This year, Harlequins and The Harlequins Foundation also part-funded research into homophobia and sexism in women's rugby
with Monash University in Australia. This helped show areas of improvement for rugby when it comes to homophobia and
strengthened the resolve of all of us at Harlequins to do our part to improve LGBTQ+ participation.
The Club is aware there is still much more to be done to which we remain committed.
It is important to the Club that we nurture a supportive environment where employees are valued and respected. Throughout our
operations we encourage collaboration, teamwork and creativity both on and off the field and provide a range of opportunities for
learning and development.
The Club continues to support the mental and physical wellbeing of employees through a number of interventions including gym
access and a dedicated EAP offering confidential counselling and other support services. During the 2021/2022 season, the Club
successfully launched a new online mental wellbeing and resilience program for all employees including players and a dedicated
course for line managers.
The health and safety policy is reviewed annually by the Board and is updated to incorporate the changing circumstances of the
business environment.
Harlequins Foundation
The Foundation has continued to go from strength to strength. The relationship with the Club has been integral to what the
Foundation has been able to achieve.
The Foundation has continued to develop several key strategic partnerships with organisations to help support scaling up our reach
and continue to focus on mental health issues.
The Foundation uses its network and brand to inspire, educate and equip individuals and communities to change their lives and
fulfil their potential. It has recently launched a new and exciting mental health campaign, "Lend An Ear" which promotes better
listening skills to enable teammates, colleagues, friends and family to talk when times are tough.
The Harlequins Foundation also supports the work the Club is doing with regards to inclusivity, as well as running inclusive
programmes to improve engagement in rugby from those who may not have taken part otherwise. Whilst much of the work it does
is focused on the broader Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion landscape, they have hosted panels and seminars specifically focused
on LGBTQ+ inclusion.
Launched last year, 'The Jesters’ is Harlequins' wheelchair rugby team, created by the Harlequins Foundation which is a fully
inclusive team welcoming of new and veteran players alike. The Jesters provides a rare opportunity for contact sport that disabled
people often have no access to.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
Elite team performance
The key risks facing Harlequins are the results and performance of the elite men's team. Despite the team not retaining the
Premiership title in the 2021/2022 season, the Club has reported record season ticket sales, with home league games essentially
selling out.
The failure of Worcester and Wasps, two Gallagher Premiership clubs in the early part of the 2022/2023 season is a harsh reminder
of the significant challenges faced by all premiership clubs.
The Premiership is engaged in work with the remaining clubs, the Rugby Football Union and other stakeholders across the game.
The shared goal is to put in place stronger foundations that underpin the long-term prosperity for the sport in this country, creating
a system and a model where investors in clubs feel the confidence to take a long-term view on the asset growth of the club and
the sport.
The Club's Board and senior management team continue to focus their efforts on developing a commercial business to underpin
the strength of our rugby operations. We aim to develop a financially stable environment that helps de-risk the uncertainty of elite,
competitive sport. However, success on the pitch remains the greatest driver of long-term sustainability.
Failure to comply with competition regulations
The financial and reputational risk of material non-compliance with rugby competition rules and regulations could lead to a loss of
future revenue streams, and even relegation from the Gallagher Premiership, thereby threatening the Club’s sustainability.
Harlequins mitigates such risk through a series of robust internal controls to ensure that we comply with and meet our obligations
to disclose required information for each relevant competition, and in particular, the Gallagher Premiership salary cap.
Failure to renew key commercial contracts and/or a reduction in central funding
Reduced commercial revenues, either negotiated by ourselves or Premiership Rugby centrally, could have a material impact on the
Club’s long-term profitability. Harlequins continually invests in talented people, building dedicated sales and marketing teams to
develop a corporate pipeline and proactively manage existing contracts and relationships.
Funding
For the time being Harlequins remains reliant on the continuing ongoing financial support from its ultimate shareholders. It is
imperative that the Club continues its journey towards financial sustainability.
Cyber security breaches causing financial and/or reputational harm
Harlequins takes the threat of cyber security and its responsibility as a data controller seriously. We deploy the latest cyber
protection systems which are dynamically updated to counter known and potential threats.
Terrorism
Sport is no stranger to the threat from terrorism. Since the 1972 Olympics in Munich, high-profile sporting and other live events
have been targeted with varying degrees of impact and success. In recent years several incidents at high-profile sporting events
have been prevented, but we are aware that we must remain vigilant to possible terrorism risks.

CONCLUSION
We are committed to achieving playing success within a commercially sustainable business. The Club continues to manage costs
and identify and consummate revenue opportunities with the aim of delivering an improved and sustainable financial position.
2021/2022 has been a recovery year after the aftermath of Covid-19 which, with the support from the Club’s stakeholders, we
have come through and believe that we are stronger for it. Despite not retaining the title, we finished in the Top 4 for both our
men's and women's teams and remain committed to the style of rugby we are playing and by the opportunities which this presents
us both commercially and in our communities. We face challenging times but believe that we have an exciting future in the
2022/2023 season and beyond.

FINANCIAL INSTRUMENTS
Liquidity risk
The company is responsible for the management of liquidity risk, which is the risk that the financial obligations cannot be met as
they fall due. Working capital and finance charges on borrowings are managed through a combination of operating cash flows and
funding from the continued support of the principal shareholder of the immediate parent Harlequin FC Holdings Ltd, Blue Sky
Holdings Limited. At the balance sheet date, the cash flow forecasts are reviewed by the board and the group is forecast to have
sufficient funding facilities to meet the company's obligations as they fall due.
Interest rate risk
The company is exposed to fair value interest rate risk on its fixed rate borrowings and cash flow interest rate risk on floating rate
deposits, bank overdrafts and loans, due to changes in market interest rates.
Credit risk
Credit risk arises from the company's trade debtors, being the risk that the counterparty fails to discharge their obligation in
respect of the instrument. All customers who wish to trade on credit terms, allowing for the payment of debt after the delivery of
goods or services, are subject to credit verification procedures. Trade debtors are monitored on an ongoing basis and provision is
made for doubtful debts where necessary. Risk is mitigated through developing strong relationships with our customers.

Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance sheet
date.
Timing differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from those in
which they are recognised in the financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that have been
enacted or substantively enacted by the year end at that are expected to apply to the reversal of the timing difference. The
tax rate used for this purpose is 25% (2021: 25%).
Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they will be
recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.
No deferred tax asset has been recognised on tax losses carried forward because, at present, it is not certain there will be
future taxable profit from which reversal of underlying losses can be deducted. However tax losses carried forward have
been used to reduce the deferred tax liability on the chargeable gain on investments detailed below. This has been
restricted to the amount of losses expected to be available for use subject to the amount of group deduction allowance
available to the company.
