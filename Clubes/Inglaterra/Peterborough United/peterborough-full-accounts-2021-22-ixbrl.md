STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2022
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2022.
Summary of the businessThe Directors report a profit before tax for the year of £860,413 (2021: loss before tax of £1,087,988). Key Performance Indicators The following key performance indicators are used by the company to measure and evaluate its performance: The directors take a cautious view with respect to financial risk management with the company being well managed by its board who operate a strict and prudent fiscal policy in all aspects. Budgets and projections indicate that revenue generated from footballing activities being season ticket sales, gate receipts, FL Club distributions, competition prize money and TV & broadcasting revenue are insufficient to cover expected costs. The directors are continually mindful that financially the company is dependent and will continue to be dependent upon the support provided by its beneficial owners, Mr D MacAnthony, Dr J Neale and Mr R Thompson. Turnover is approximately 168.5% up on the previous year, cost of sales have increased by approximately 64%, leading to an increase in the gross profit by 232%. Overall Staff Costs are up by approximately 31.4% with this being a targeted area for savings on an ongoing basis. Our Average Attendance in the prior year had been affected due to the coronavirus pandemic. We continue to undertake Attendance Initiatives Projects to improve this position. Principal risks and uncertainties The nature of professional football presents many risks and uncertainties. The Board is aware of the risks that affect the company and they are regularly reviewed.Liquidity risk is the main financial risk faced by the company and this is addressed by the careful management of its working capital requirements. Uncertainties connected with the various income streams are acknowledged and assessed and future funding requirements are continually reviewed by the board. The board are aware of the significant uncertainties that potentially impact the company, further information on these uncertainties can be found in the going concern section within note 1.2 to the financial statements.Review of the businessFollowing automatic promotion to the Sky Bet Championship and the celebrations from that incredible day against Lincoln City had subsided, our immediate focus as a football club was to prepare for what was to come, both on and off the pitch. Competing in the Sky Bet Championship was always going to be a huge challenge, particularly for a club the size of ours. The league consists of clubs with far greater infrastructure, finance and resources and many had recently played in the Premier League. The company issued a supporters bond, The Peterborough United Bond was fully subscribed raising £2.5m and we thank everyone for their excitement and participation.There were some positive moments on the pitch during the season. We enjoyed some memorable victories over established Championship sides including Birmingham City and Blackburn Rovers and we did the double over Queens Park Rangers and who can forget the last minute winner against Derby County.

Review of the business (continued)However, as with any league, consistency is key and we struggled to find that throughout the campaign. Despite the best efforts from everyone at the Club and whilst managing the difficult long-term effects of Covid-19 on finances and resources, the Club was ultimately unsuccessful in its aim to maintain Championship status despite a spirited last few months under the management of Grant McCann, which culminated in a morale-boosting conclusion to the season with a 5-0 win over Blackpool.We did enjoy a positive run in the Emirates FA Cup, which culminated in a fiercely contested fifth round tie against Premier League giants Manchester City in front of a packed Weston Homes Stadium.We came away from that game with plenty of positives both on and off the pitch and our sponsors and partners were boosted by the international coverage that a tie like that can provide. Our Academy and investment in the development of young players continues to go from strength to strength. The season recorded over 90 senior appearances from Academy graduates for the second season running, coupled with outstanding academic results for our second year apprentices. Professional contracts were awarded to 10 youth team players and international honours for Ronnie Edwards and Janos Bodnar for England and Hungary respectively were reward for their continued progress. The Club also celebrated the successful application and audit process, which resulted in receiving a three-year Category 2 Licence for the Academy, the first time the Club had achieved this in its history. A fantastic achievement and deserved recognition for the hard work of Academy Manager Kieran Scarff and his team. As part of this successful application for the new status, the indoor dome at the training ground was completed, which not only serves our youngsters within the Academy, but also the wider community.The Posh Women also continued to flourish and 21/22 was another landmark season. The first team maintained their status in FAWNL Division One Midlands in their debut season and also reached round one of the Vitality FA Women’s Cup for the first time. The season ended with a showcase game against Hashtag United at the Weston Homes Stadium in front of a record attendance of 1,400. There is a clear plan in place from Women’s General Manager Bobby Copping to continue to grow this area of the football club.During the season, the Club said farewell to long serving and highly respected Chief Executive Officer Bob Symns who retired in the autumn. The owners and everyone connected with the Club would like to thank Bob for many years of dedicated service and wish him well in retirement. Bob has taken the role of Club Ambassador, attends games regularly and continues to be a source of support and advice for staff.Behind the scenes, we continue to grow and improve in all areas. We have worked hard on our new streaming service Posh+, providing exclusive content as well as live matches across the first team and the under 21s. The Club continues to be grateful and very fortunate to have a small but fantastic team of staff who work tirelessly all year round. The Board would like to place on record it’s gratitude to every member of staff for their hard work and commitment to making our Club the best it can be. Finally, we would like to thank our fantastic fans, sponsors and partners for their continued support.

Closing Summary It is fair to say that the 2022-2023 campaign was a rollercoaster ride, which culminated in a play-off semi-final defeat at Hillsborough against Sheffield Wednesday. However, the football club have reset, we are re-charged and together, with Darren Ferguson at the helm, we are excited about the future. The club received multiple bids during the January transfer window in 2023 for several players, which we rejected because the ownership felt that it would give the club a better opportunity to achieve promotion and but for a few seconds we may have had that chance at Wembley. Such are the highs and lows of football.At the time of writing this report we have successfully sold Jack Taylor and we wish him every success in his future career. As part of our reset, we expect a number of players to depart ahead of the next Sky Bet League One campaign with several transfer listed following the conclusion of the last term. Additionally, we are very excited about the ongoing recruitment campaign and believe 2023-24 campaign will be the start of another development “sprint” where we see significant talent playing for our beloved club.The new Sky Bet League One campaign gets underway in August and we will, as always, do our best to entertain our supporters on the pitch. Material uncertainty related to going concern
We draw your attention to note 1.2 in the financial statements which indicates a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt over the company’s ability to continue as a going concern. In forming our opinion on the financial statements, we have considered the adequacy of the disclosures made in note 1.2. The financial statements do not include the adjustments that would result if the company were unable to continue as a going concern. Explanation of material uncertainty At the balance sheet date the Company is in a net liability position. Day to day working capital requirements are met through existing cash and banking facilities, bank loans, investor financing and from the support of its beneficial owners by way of shareholder related loans. The Board have assessed the adequacy of the financial facilities available to the company, using management information, including a recent review of forecasts which extend beyond one year from the date of approval of these financial statements. The amount of funding required is uncertain and is dependent upon key assumptions around season ticket sales and matchday income, football performance, overhead costs, future player sales, contingent receipts and transfer fees payable and the company’s ability to secure future debt and/or equity finance. In addition to the above, additional funding is likely to be required to meet any payments agreed as part of the disputed related party loan, as explained in notes 1.2 and 24. The Board have obtained a letter of support from two of the ultimate beneficial owners, Darragh MacAnthony and Jason Neale, whereby they have provided confirmation of their intention to support the company and to provide additional loan facilities as required to meet any shortfalls that may arise from variances to the forecast cash flows. In addition, they have provided further confirmation of their intention to support the company by not demanding repayment of loans owing to related parties for the foreseeable future. The level and timing of the potential cash requirements have been considered by the board, however, they acknowledge that the letter of support is not legally binding and there is no contractual certainty that the shareholders will make available such funding, nor require repayment of their existing loans. As explained in note 1.2, this creates a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt over the company’s ability to continue as a going concern. Our opinion is not modified in respect of this matter.

| 1.8 | Financial instruments |  |

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12 ‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments. Financial instruments are recognised in the company's balance sheet when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument. Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.
Other financial assets
Other financial assets, including investments in equity instruments which are not subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures, are initially measured at fair value, which is normally the transaction price. Such assets are subsequently carried at fair value and the changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss, except that investments in equity instruments that are not publicly traded and whose fair values cannot be measured reliably are measured at cost less impairment.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting end date. Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.

Based on the current state of negotiations, the company has concluded that no further amounts are required to be provided in the financial statements at 30 June 2022.
