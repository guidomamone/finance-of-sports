STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2024
The Directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2024. Summary of the businessThe Directors report a loss before tax for the year of £2,317,552 (2023: loss before tax of £3,532,195). Key Performance Indicators The following key performance indicators are used by the Company to measure and evaluate its performance: The Directors take a cautious view with respect to financial risk management with the Company being well managed by its Board who operate a strict and prudent fiscal policy in all aspects. Budgets and projections indicate that revenue generated from footballing activities being season ticket sales, gate receipts, FL Club distributions, competition prize money and TV & broadcasting revenue are insufficient to cover expected costs. The Directors are continually mindful that financially the Company is dependent and will continue to be dependent upon the support provided by its beneficial owner, Mr D MacAnthony. Turnover is approximately 3.5% down on the previous year, cost of sales have decreased by approximately 5.8%, leading to a decrease in the gross profit by 2.1%. Overall Staff Costs are down by approximately 5.2% with this being a targeted area for savings on an ongoing basis. Principal risks and uncertainties The nature of professional football presents many risks and uncertainties. The Board is aware of the risks that affect the Company and they are regularly reviewed.Liquidity risk is the main financial risk faced by the Company and this is addressed by the careful management of its working capital requirements. Uncertainties connected with the various income streams are acknowledged and assessed and future funding requirements are continually reviewed by the Board. The Board are aware of the significant uncertainties that potentially impact the Company, further information on these uncertainties can be found in the going concern section within note 1.2 to the financial statements.Review of the businessThe 2023-2024 campaign was full of excitement and entertainment following the desolation in the manner of how the previous season concluded. During the summer, manager Darren Ferguson and his staff re-moulded the squad, bringing in younger talent to compliment an already talented group of players. That determination to re-shape the squad in that way was done with the belief that a no-fear attitude would see the club thrive on the pitch against the odds and so it proved.

Review of the business (continued)With the supporters firmly backing the squad and the management, everybody came together as one and there were some superb days, none more so than the Bristol Street Motors Trophy final at Wembley Stadium when over 20,000 fans made the journey to the capital to see Academy graduate Harrison Burrows net twice, including an unforgettable last-minute winner to overcome Wycombe Wanderers. After success in the competition back in 2014, it was great that ten years on, the team could once again provide memories that will last a lifetime for the staff, sponsors and supporters. In the FA Cup, after tense victories against Salford City and Doncaster Rovers, we welcomed Leeds United to the Weston Homes Stadium, but the Championship side were too strong on the day in front of a capacity crowd here in PE2. The team reached the play-offs for the second season running but were undone by Oxford United over two legs, who went on to achieve promotion at Wembley. We were obviously all disappointed not to get to the final, but overall, it has to be a successful campaign with silverware secured and plenty of thrills and spills on the pitch. The Academy continues to go from strength to strength with Dan Robinson appointed into the role of Academy Director following a successful spell at Burton Albion. While performances on the pitch didn’t yield consistent results in the league, there were still plenty of players that tasted first team experience across the competitions including a first start for Harley Mills and first appearances for Donay O’Brien-Brady and Will Van Lier with other Academy graduates on the bench. With the Academy now at Category Two status, the Under 21s faced regular tough opposition within their league structure, but under Ryan Semple, delivered some impressive results. The Peterborough United Women had a successful campaign in the 2023/2024 season, including a top three finish in the FA Women’s National League Division One Midlands, obtaining their highest ever points tally and players’ names appearing on the back of their shirts for the first time in the club’s history. They also reached round two of the Adobe Women’s FA Cup and reached the final of the County Cup. With Sam Winch now in position as General Manager there is a real desire to try and obtain promotion from the level they operate at.The success of all of Peterborough United’s football teams would not be possible without the hard work, commitment, and dedication of the team behind the scenes with the Commercial and Retail departments continue to excel with another superb return with their financial figures.Finally, as always, we would like to thank our superb fans, sponsors, and partners for their continued support.Going concern At the balance sheet date the Company is in a net liability position. The Company's business activities combined with factors likely to impact future development, performance and position have been considered by the Board and continues to be reviewed in line with forecasts which extend beyond a year from the date of approval of this report. The Board acknowledges their requirement to prepare the statutory financial statements on a going concern basis unless it is inappropriate to presume the Company will continue to be in business. Details of the Board's assessment of going concern is set out in note 1.2 of the financial statements. Closing SummaryWith the departures of Harrison Burrows, Josh Knight, Ronnie Edwards and Ephron Mason-Clark in the summer, a rebuild began. Our league results have been disappointing and at the time of writing, we sit in 15th position.However, fans were treated to an away trip at Everton in the 3rd round of the FA Cup in front of 40,000 fans at Goodison Park. The last chance for many to visit this iconic venue before the Toffees move to their new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock for the 25-26 season.In addition, we have just secured a return trip to Wembley as defending champions of the EFL Trophy - renamed the Vertu Trophy this season. A magnificent reward for the supporters, and likely to be a sell out as we face Birmingham City Football Club, who last won this trophy in 1995 when a certain Mr Barry Fry was Manager.

| 1.8 | Financial instruments |  |

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12 ‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments. Financial instruments are recognised in the company's balance sheet when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument. Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.
Other financial assets
Other financial assets, including investments in equity instruments which are not subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures, are initially measured at fair value, which is normally the transaction price. Such assets are subsequently carried at fair value and the changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss, except that investments in equity instruments that are not publicly traded and whose fair values cannot be measured reliably are measured at cost less impairment.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting end date. Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.

Other The company has financial commitments under contract for hardware support and maintenance of pitches at the main ground and academy.
