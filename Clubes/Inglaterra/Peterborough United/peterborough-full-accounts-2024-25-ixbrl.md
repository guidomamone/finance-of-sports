FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.
Review of the business
The 2024-2025 campaign might have been a challenge in the league, but it did provide another visit to Wembley Stadium, and another successful visit too, as goals from Hector Kyprianou and Harley Mills secured the Vertu Trophy against all the odds.Birmingham City were overwhelming favourites to lift the silverware and with the final taking place less than seven days after they secured the League One title, they were in celebratory mood in the capital. However, Darren Ferguson’s side put in a superb performance and thoroughly deserved the 2-0 victory – which meant we became the first side to retain the trophy and the only side to win five successive games at the national stadium in the world!The highlight of the league campaign was undoubtedly the 6-1 victory over local rivals Cambridge United in front of a raucous Weston Homes Stadium crowd. It was the second year in a row we had scored more than five against the U’s.We also claimed all three points in the return fixture at the Abbey Stadium, which was another memorable occasion for the supporters in attendance. There were first team debuts handed out to a number of players including Andre Changunda and Joe Andrews on the final day at Rotherham United. The Academy enjoyed a positive campaign with the Under 18s lifting the Professional Development League Cup after a 1-0 victory over favourites Sheffield United. With a host of Under 16s participating in the Under 18s and a number of Under 18s in the Under 21s group, it was a fine 12 months for development both under the management of Jimmy Unwin and Sam Gaughran.The Posh Women had another record-breaking campaign in the 2024/25 season, including a top three finish in the FA Women’s National League Division One Midlands and reaching their highest ever points tally. They also reached round two of the Adobe Women’s FA Cup after beating higher level opposition in round one. This was their final season playing their home fixtures at Abbey Lawn, the home of Bourne. With the recruitment continuing to be strong, there is a real sense that the 2025-2026 campaign can be their year.Once again, the success on the pitch has been supplemented by the tremendous work off it, with the commercial team continuing to break records in terms of revenue and the retail department going from strength to strength following the opening of the new Store. The Posh Store, which replaced the Posh Venue, is arguably one of the best retail outlets outside of the Premier League and has been well received by supporters and industry experts.Finally, we would like to thank our superb fans, sponsors, and partners for their continued support and look forward to an exciting season.At the time of writing (February 2026) it can be said that our season to date has been a tale of two halves. Although the season started full of optimism, results did not follow and after just 3 wins from the first thirteen games, Darren Ferguson departed the Club.Darren’s legacy is significant at the football Club, four promotions, three cup wins at Wembley, with an unprecedented two back to back, he is the greatest manager in Peterborough United’s history.Luke Williams was appointed Manager shortly after kicking off what has quickly become a turnaround campaign in the League. A memorable 3-0 win against high flying Stockport County highlighted the resurgence, and with four straight wins at one point impressing with attacking intent and defensive improvement.Whilst there will be no return trip to Wembley in April in the Vertu Trophy, there is still plenty of optimism of a good League campaign.

| 1.2 | Going concern |  |
| The Board of Directors are required to prepare the statutory financial statements on the going concern basis unless it is inappropriate to presume that the company will continue in business. The company has net liabilities of £13.5m (2024: £16.6m) and made a profit after tax in the current year of £2.9m (2024: loss of £2.3m) and has net current liabilities of £12.1m (2024: £13.6m). Day to day working capital requirements are met through existing cash and banking facilities, bank loans, investor financing and from the support of its beneficial owners by way of shareholder related loans.To enable the Board to assess the adequacy of the financial facilities available to the company, they have used management information, including a recent review of forecasts which extend beyond one year from the date of approval of these financial statements. The forecasts make key assumptions around season ticket sales and matchday income, football performance, overhead costs, future player sales, contingent receipts and transfer fees payable and the company's ability to secure future debt and/or equity finance, if required. Any reliance placed on the forecasts by the Board is dependent upon the above factors. At the time of preparation, the Board recognises that current projections indicate that the company will require additional funding within the next 12 months to meet its operational cash flow requirements and to facilitate loan repayments and cash requirements for other financing commitments. The assumptions made by the Board when determining the expected funding requirement do not include any contingent income relating to potential sell-on fees. The amount of additional funding required is uncertain and the Board acknowledges that the required amounts will depend upon significant uncertainties relating to:-the assumptions relating to the timing and amounts expected to be received in respect of player sales;-the ongoing support of the beneficial owner, Darragh MacAnthony. On the basis of the above, the Board acknowledges the existence of material uncertainties which may cause significant doubt over the company's ability to continue as a going concern. The Board have obtained a letter of support from the ultimate beneficial owner, Darragh MacAnthony, confirming the intention to support the company and to provide additional loan facilities as required to meet any shortfalls that may arise from variances to the forecast cash flows. In addition, further confirmations have been provided of the intention to support the company by not demanding repayment of the loans owing to Darragh MacAnthony for the foreseeable future. The level and timing of potential cash requirements have been considered by the Board, however, they acknowledge that the letter of support is not legally binding and there is no contractual certainty that the shareholder will make available such funding, nor require repayment of the existing loans. If the support of the shareholder was required and not forthcoming, the company may not be able to raise additional funding to provide sufficient cash to enable it to meet its liabilities as they fall due. This creates a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt over the company's ability to continue as a going concern. Nevertheless, on the basis of the information available to the Board at the current time, the Board believe that there is a reasonable expectation that the company will have adequate resources to continue in operational existence for the foreseeable future. | 1.8 | Financial instruments |  |

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12 ‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments. Financial instruments are recognised in the company's balance sheet when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument. Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.
Other financial assets
Other financial assets, including investments in equity instruments which are not subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures, are initially measured at fair value, which is normally the transaction price. Such assets are subsequently carried at fair value and the changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss, except that investments in equity instruments that are not publicly traded and whose fair values cannot be measured reliably are measured at cost less impairment.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting end date. Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.
