STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2023
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2023. Summary of the businessThe Directors report a loss before tax for the year of £3,532,195 (2022: profit before tax of £860,413). Key Performance Indicators The following key performance indicators are used by the company to measure and evaluate its performance: The directors take a cautious view with respect to financial risk management with the company being well managed by its board who operate a strict and prudent fiscal policy in all aspects. Budgets and projections indicate that revenue generated from footballing activities being season ticket sales, gate receipts, FL Club distributions, competition prize money and TV & broadcasting revenue are insufficient to cover expected costs. The directors are continually mindful that financially the company is dependent and will continue to be dependent upon the support provided by its beneficial owner, Mr D MacAnthony. Turnover is approximately 41.7% down on the previous year, cost of sales have decreased by approximately 3%, leading to a decrease in the gross profit by 53.3%. Overall Staff Costs are down by approximately 15.3% with this being a targeted area for savings on an ongoing basis. Principal risks and uncertainties The nature of professional football presents many risks and uncertainties. The Board is aware of the risks that affect the company and they are regularly reviewed.Liquidity risk is the main financial risk faced by the company and this is addressed by the careful management of its working capital requirements. Uncertainties connected with the various income streams are acknowledged and assessed and future funding requirements are continually reviewed by the board. The board are aware of the significant uncertainties that potentially impact the company, further information on these uncertainties can be found in the going concern section within note 1.2 to the financial statements.Review of the businessThe team adjusted to life back in League One with four out of five league wins and hopes were high for a quick return to the Championship. However, we were unable to maintain that run and four straight league defeats plus 2 in cup competitions dampened expectations. Consistency was difficult to find and with more disappointing results in December and over the Christmas period culminating in a 0-3 home defeat to Wycombe on New Year’s Day, the club parted company with club legend Grant McCann and his assistant Cliff Byrne.

Review of the business (continued)With the club in eighth position and five points off the playoffs, Darren Ferguson returned for his fourth spell agreeing to manage the team for the rest of the season. As Club Chairman and owner Darragh MacAnthony said ‘When you look for a manager in January, it is a very difficult time, an impossible time to get someone in, so if you were to write down what you would want in a manager, someone who identifies with how you work as a football club, someone who has had multiple promotions on their CV, someone with a style of football that gets people off their seats, where would you find a manager like that?' It just so happens we have one that lives nearby and has agreed to come and help us until the end of the season.Results improved and with 13 wins out of 19, what looked like an improbable play off place earlier in the season was sealed with a fantastic win on the last day of scheduled fixtures at Barnsley.Buoyed by a full house at the Weston Homes Stadium, supporters were treated to a spectacular first leg play off semi-final display against Sheffield Wednesday resulting in a 4-0 win. We all know what happened in the now infamous second leg at Hillsborough, and the utter despair of being a few seconds away from Wembley, only for the Owls to score in the 90th minute to take the tie to extra time. Sadly, with the tie all square at 5-5, our dreams of Wembley and promotion back to the Championship were shattered in the penalty shootout.The club continued to invest heavily in the Academy, and we completed our first full season as a Category 2 Academy. The U18’s had a great season finishing 3rd in the league and winning two cups, the Professional Development League Cup, and the David Joyce County Cup. Benji Arthur received an England call up and featured in the England U18s International tournament in Lisbon. Expectations remain high for a number of players in the Academy programme, and during the season there were over 100 first team league appearances for academy graduates.The Posh Women continue to thrive, and another successful season followed a move from the Idverde Training Ground to a new home, The Abbey Lawn, at Bourne Town FC which enabled them to attract many new supporters. A top four finish in the FA Women’s National League Division One, reaching the third round of the Vitality Women’s FA Cup and winning the Northants FA County Cup gave a record-breaking season on the pitch. The club successfully applied for and received a 3-year license from the FA to set up a girls Emerging Talent Centre (ETC) in September 2022. With four age groups – U10s, U12s, U14s and U16s this provides the opportunity for young girls in the community to play football, receive high quality coaching and a pathway into the senior ladies’ football teams.It would be remiss not to mention the fantastic team behind the scenes. The success of all of Peterborough United’s football teams would not be possible without their hard work, commitment, and dedication. Finally, as always, we would like to thank our superb fans, sponsors, and partners for their continued support.Going concern At the balance sheet date the Company is in a net liability position. The Company's business activities combined with factors likely to impact future development, performance and position have been considered by the Board and continues to be reviewed in line with forecasts which extend beyond a year from the date of approval of this report. The Board acknowledges their requirement to prepare the statutory financial statements on a going concern basis unless it is inappropriate to presume the company will continue to be in business. Details of the Board's assessment of going concern is set out in note 1.2 of the financial statements. Closing SummaryMany faces, who had served us well and delivered promotion in the COVID 20-21 season, departed over the summer and we wish them all well with their new clubs. We returned for the new season, refreshed with the youngest team in the league, and the rebuild began. Some scintillating performances have been delivered and at the time of writing we are sitting 5th in the table and have the Bristol Street Motors Cup Final at Wembley to look forward to. Our first visit to the national stadium in ten years.

| 1.8 | Financial instruments |  |

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12 ‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments. Financial instruments are recognised in the company's balance sheet when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument. Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.
Other financial assets
Other financial assets, including investments in equity instruments which are not subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures, are initially measured at fair value, which is normally the transaction price. Such assets are subsequently carried at fair value and the changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss, except that investments in equity instruments that are not publicly traded and whose fair values cannot be measured reliably are measured at cost less impairment.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting end date. Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.
