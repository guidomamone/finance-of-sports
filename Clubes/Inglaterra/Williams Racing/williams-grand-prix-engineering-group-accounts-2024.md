Williams Grand Prix Engineering Limited

Strategic Report.

The principal activity of the Group is to operate and manage a Formula One racing team, through the design and
manufacture of racing cars for participation in the FIA Formula One World Championship.

Formula One as a sport demonstrated continued growth during the 2024 season, racing across a record-breaking
24 races in the calendar. Formula One’s growing reach across North America and the ongoing commercial draw
from fans, spectators and partners alike delivered increased commercial rights revenue for all teams. The sport’s
continued growth is demonstrated by Nielsen data showing F1 is the most popular sporting series with over 750
million fans, and a 13% increase in total TV audience during 2024 to 1.48bn. The team finished in 9" place in the
2024 Constructors’ Championship with both Alex Albon and Franco Colapinto scoring top 10 finishes at races
during the year. However, on-track performance in 2024 was significantly hampered by an unusually high number
of crashes which restricted scoring opportunities and increased costs. Race results in 2024 did not reflect the
significant ongoing transformation of the team and continued investment in infrastructure, people and technology
which has been ongoing since 2020. The long-term objective of the team remains to return to the front of the grid
through continued investment in these areas to develop world-class technology and talent.

The Group’s suite of commercial partners also grew this year, with significant brands such as Komatsu, Mercado
Libre, Globant, Keeper Security, Vast Data and Zoox joining the growing commercial portfolio alongside Gulf
Oil, MyProtein, Stephens, Michelob ULTRA, Kraken, Duracell and Dorilton Ventures. Williams continues to
lead the grid with an innovative and creative offering to partners and fans alike, with pioneering fan activations
across the season that secured several major awards. Our Lap of Legends activation with Michelob ULTRA -
harnessing AI to create the world’s first virtual vs real F] race - won four awards at the Cannes Lions International
Festival of Creativity and was named Sports Technology of the Year at the SBJ Tech awards. Our city centre fan
zones presented by Kraken, which aim to democratise F1, were named Best Live Experience at The Race Media
Awards.

For the year ended 31 December 2024 revenue was £179.8m, (2023: £127.0m) and the loss after tax was £49.8m
(2023: £84.3m). Whilst the Group remains in a loss position, this is in line with expectations and the Group’s
strategy to continue investing in all areas of the business to drive both on-track and commercial performance in
pursuit of success in the medium and long-term. Revenue increased in 2024 as a result of higher commercial rights
revenue associated with finishing 7 in the 2023 Constructors’ Championship (2022: 10") and increased
sponsorship revenue as the team adds to its stable of high quality commercial partners.

The balance sheet remains strong with net assets of £130.1m as at 31 December 2024 (2023: £67.3m), providing
a sound financial base on which to continue the team’s long term strategy of returning to the front of the grid and
being financially sustainable.

The team is strengthened in 2025, with Alex Albon remaining with the team and being joined by multiple F1 race
winner Carlos Sainz. Both drivers signed multi-year agreements in 2024, after careful consideration and
understanding of our longer term strategy. In a further vote of confidence in the team’s ambition and momentum,
global collaboration software provider Atlassian (NASDAQ: TEAM) was announced as Official Title Partner,
Official Technology Partner and Official Collaboration Software partner in January 2025, a strategic partnership
that is also the biggest in Williams’ history. The Group has continued to invest and expand its Williams Racing
Driver Academy Programme, through which, Franco Colapinto became a 2024 driver for 9 races. The Academy
supports drivers on every rung of the motorsport ladder, helping them to develop the skills needed to become the
champions of the future. In addition, the Group continues to expand its STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering
and Mathematics) schools outreach programme, which brought 7,390 8- to 16-year-olds to our Grove HQ free of
charge to experience STEM careers through the lens of motorsport. Our belief and investment in future talent was
also evident in the launch of the Komatsu-Williams Engineering Academy, a global programme to discover and
develop the brightest and best young engineers and mentor them through their education, and in our Early Careers
programmes which now make up more than 10% of our employee headcount.

The business remains committed to transformation, led by anew and strengthened senior management team, based

on the team’s values of Innovation, Teamwork, Resilience, Excellence and Accountability coupled with further
investment in technology and people to develop a world leading team.

Section 172 Statement

The Directors act to promote the overall success of the Company and maintain high standards of business conduct
across all areas of the Company. Key stakeholders are identified as suppliers, customers, employees and
shareholders.

The Company strives for long, productive relationships with all stakeholders, conducting regular reviews across
all levels of the Company and proactively considering the long-term stakeholder impact in all principal decisions.

Principal decisions are defined as both those that are material to the Company, but also those that are material to
any of our key stakeholder groups. Where there are conflicting priorities from different stakeholders, the Directors
act for the overall best outcome of the Company.

The Company understands that attracting and retaining a diverse and talented range of employees and suppliers
is core to achieving the long-term objectives and success. The Company regularly communicates with its
employees through town hall presentations, race debriefs, internal emails and intranet communications whilst
proactively and regularly assessing engagement through various employee forums.

Supplier relationships are fostered and developed to reflect the innovative, fast pace and high-quality requirements
of the industry with regular communication and review, including collecting objective data and constructive
feedback to assess mutua! performance, to build strong, long lasting and productive relationships.

Identification and management of risks

The Board of Directors for Williams, which has overall responsibility for risk management and internal control,
identifies key risks and puts in place appropriate internal controls. The Board provides strategic direction on risk
related decision making to ensure that the appropriate policies are adopted within the Company.

Senior managers have responsibility for identifying and evaluating risks relating to their areas of responsibility.
They are also responsible for implementing and monitoring effective controls to manage these risks. Where
necessary they will bring in expertise from outside the Company to ensure matters are addressed appropriately.
The key risks to the Company continue to be as follows:

Foreign exchange risk

The Company is exposed to movements in foreign currency exchange risk due to revenues and costs in currencies
other than sterling. These risks are monitored and reported on a regular basis.

Liquidity risk
The Company’s liquidity is closely monitored with regular and robust reviews with forecast processes around
future cashflows, on both a short term (within three months) and long term (up to five years) basis.

Basis of consolidation

The consolidated financial statements include the financial statements of the Company and its subsidiary
undertakings made up to 31 December 2024. A subsidiary is an entity that is controlled by the parent. The
results of subsidiary undertakings are included in the consolidated profit and loss account from the date
that control commences until the date that control ceases. Control is established when the Company has
the power to govern the operating and financial policies of an entity so as to obtain benefits from its
activities. In assessing control, the Group takes into consideration potential voting rights that are currently
exercisable.

An associate is an entity in which the Group has significant influence, but not control, over the operating
and financial policies of the entity. Significant influence is presumed to exist when the investor holds
between 20% and 50% of the equity voting rights.

A joint venture is a contractual arrangement undertaking in which the Group exercises joint control over
the operating and financial policies of the entity. Where the joint venture is carried out through an entity,
it is treated as a jointly controlled entity. The Group’s share of the profits less losses of associates and of
jointly controlled entities is included in the consolidated profit and loss account and its interest in their net
assets is recorded on the balance sheet using the equity method. Transactions with an associate are
eliminated to the extent of the Group’s interest in the associate.

Where a Group company is party to a joint venture, the company accounts directly for its part of the income
and expenditure, assets, liabilities and cash flows. Such arrangements are reported in the consolidated
financial statements on the same basis.

Under Section 408 of the Companies Act 2006 the Company is exempt from the requirement to present its
own profit and loss account.

In the parent financial statements, investments in associates were carried at cost less impairment.

the period. The Company believes that the full RDEC claim included in the balance sheet will be settled
by HMRC. The increase in receivables of £21.5m receivable during the year reflects the current year

Significant accounting policies

Revenue

The Company principally earns revenue through sponsorship and commercial rights. Where sponsorship
arrangements provide the sponsor with multiple deliverables, contractual revenues are allocated to each
deliverable based on the relative fair value of the separate components. The majority of sponsorship revenue
is recognised evenly over the course of the year. Where sponsors make payment other than in cash, revenue
is recognised based on the fair value of the goods or services received or the fair value of the services
provided. Where these amounts cannot be reliably estimated, no revenue is recognised. Commercial rights
revenues in relation to performance in the Constructors’ Championship are based on performance in the
preceding season. This revenue is recognised across the period of the Constructors' Championship.

Other operating income

The Company generates other operating income predominantly by providing marketing services to fellow
subsidiary companies, and by research and development (see below). The income is recognised evenly
across the period to which the services relate. In addition, operating income includes income from use of, or
disposal of, heritage assets, as they represent activities normally considered to be operating as part of
management of a Formula One team developing and managing racing cars.

Research and development

The Company is heavily committed to research and development activities to maintain its position as a world
leader in motorsport. All expenditure on research and development is expensed as incurred. The Company
claims RDEC in respect of its research and development expenditure. The Company recognises the value
of the RDEC gross of tax as other operating income in the year in which the underlying research and
development expenditure was incurred, The net RDEC receivable is recognised within debtors.

Investments

Investments in subsidiary companies are held at cost less accumulated impairment losses. Investments in
associate companies are held at cost less accumulated impairment losses.

Heritage assets

The Company maintains a collection of historic Formula One cars and other vehicles of significance to
the Company. These assets are held on the balance sheet at valuation. The valuation has been performed by
a specialist looking at the cars individually, considering the value inherent in the provenance attached (e.g.
championship winning cars), as well as looking at sales values for similar vehicles where possible. Assets
are revalued every five years and gains and losses are recognised in other comprehensive income. Formula
One racing cars and other vehicles retained at the end of each year are initially recorded as heritage assets
with a value of £50,000 up until a revaluation takes place.

land. No depreciation is charged during the period of construction. Assets in the course of construction are
stated at cost and are not depreciated until they are available for use. Upon completion of construction,
such assets will be transferred to appropriate asset categories and depreciated accordingly.

Leasing and hire purchase commitments

Assets held under finance leases, which are leases where substantially all the risks and rewards of
ownership of the asset have passed to the Company, and hire purchase contracts are capitalised in the
balance sheet and depreciated over the shorter of the lease term and asset's useful lives. A corresponding
liability is recognised for the lower of the fair value of the leased asset and the present value of the minimum
lease payments in the balance sheet. Lease payments are apportioned between the reduction of the lease
liability and finance charges so as to achieve a constant rate of interest on the remaining balance of the
liability. Rentals payable under operating leases are recognised as an expense on a straight-line basis over
the lease term.

Financial assets (including trade and other debtors)

A financial asset not carried at fair value through profit or loss is assessed at each reporting date to
determine whether there is objective evidence that it is impaired. A financial asset is impaired if objective
evidence indicates that a loss event has occurred after the initial recognition of the asset, and that the loss
event had a negative effect on the estimated future cash flows of that asset that can be estimated reliably.

An impairment loss in respect of a financial asset measured at amortised cost is calculated as the difference
between its carrying amount and the present value of the estimated future cash flows discounted at the
asset’s original effective interest rate. For financial instruments measured at cost less impairment, an
impairment is calculated as the difference between its carrying amount and the best estimate of the amount
that the Company would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the reporting date. Interest on the
impaired asset continues to be recognised through the unwinding of the discount. Impairment losses are
recognised in profit or loss. When a subsequent event causes the amount of impairment loss to decrease,
the decrease in impairment loss is reversed through profit or loss.
