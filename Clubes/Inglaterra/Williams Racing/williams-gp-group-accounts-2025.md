Strategic Report
The principal activity of the Group is to operate and manage a Formula One racing team, through the design and
manufacture of racing cars for participation in the FIA Formula One World Championship.
Formula One as a sport demonstrated continued growth during the 2025 season, racing across 24 races in the
calendar and breaking new ground off track through the release of FI The Movie, the season-opening 75%
anniversary celebration at London's 02, and a host of new commercial agreements. The sport's continued growth
is demonstrated by Nielsen data showing F1 is the world's most popular annual sporting series with 827m fans —
a year-on-year increase of 12% — and a 6.8% increase in total TV audience during 2025 to 1.83bn. The ongoing
commercial draw from fans, spectators and partners alike delivered increased commercial rights revenue for all
teams. Williams enjoyed its most successful season since 2016, with a fifth-place finish in the Constructors'
Championship with 137 points, two podium finishes in Baku and Qatar, and Williams' first-ever Sprint podium
in Austin. Alex Albon finished 8th in the Drivers' Championship and Carlos Sainz finished 9", demonstrating the
significant progress being made by the team in line with the long-term plan to return to the front of the grid through
continued investment in infrastructure, people and technology.
The Group's suite of commercial partners also grew in 2025, most significantly through the announcement of a
Title Partner in Atlassian, the NASDAQ-listed leader in collaboration software. Super Group (through its Betway
and Jackpot City brands), NMC2, Airia, Brillio and Santander joined the growing commercial portfolio alongside
Komatsu, Kraken, Gulf Oil, MyProtein, VAST Data, Stephens, Duracell, Keeper Security, Zoox and Dorilton
Ventures. Driven by a dedication to bring fans closer than ever to the sport, Williams grew its global fanbase to
over 400m fans and was the fastest-growing F1 team on social media.
During the year, the Company transferred much of its trade and assets to a newly formed subsidiary, Williams
Grand Prix Engineering Services LLP, an entity in which the Company is a 99% member. This update to the
corporate structure makes the organisation fit for the future, having been relatively unchanged in the nearly 50
years of operation since formation. The changes are about future-proofing our business, enabling smarter ways of
working and positioning us to stay competitive at the highest level. The inclusion of a partner group will aid
retention of certain staff and recognises the importance of those people to the delivery of the business plan.
Member distributions have been declared to the 1% member of Williams Grand Prix Engineering Services LLP.
For the year ended 31 December 2025 revenue was £244.0m, (2024: £179.8m) and the loss after tax was £25.1m
(2024: £49.8m). Whilst the Group remains in a loss position, this is in line with expectations and the Group's
strategy to continue investing in all areas of the business to drive both on-track and commercial performance in
pursuit of success in the medium and long-term. Revenue increased in 2025 as a result of increased sponsorship
revenue as the team adds to its stable of high quality commercial partners, partially offset by lower commercial
rights revenue associated with finishing 9th in the 2024 Constructors' Championship (2023: 7")
The balance sheet remains strong with net assets of £98.6m as at 31 December 2025 (2024: £130.1m), providing
a sound financial base on which to continue the team's long-term strategy of returning to the front of the grid and
being financially sustainable.
In a further vote of confidence in the team's ambition and momentum, the team added several major new
commercial partners heading into the 2026 season: Barclays, Anthropic, BNY, Wilkinson Sword, Estrella Galicia,
Nuveen, New Era and Girard-Perregaux, The Group has continued to invest in the Williams F1 Team Driver
Academy Programme, which supports drivers on almost every rung of the motorsport ladder, helping them to
develop the skills needed to become the champions of the future. In addition, the Group continues to expand its
industry-leading STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics) schools outreach programme,
which brought more than 12,000 8- to 16-year-olds to our Grove HQ free of charge to experience STEM careers
through the lens of motorsport.
The business remains committed to returning Williams to the front of the grid, underpinned by the team's values
of Innovation, Teamwork, Resilience, Excellence and Accountability.

Identification and management of risks

The Board of Directors for Williams, which has overall responsibility for risk management and internal control,
identifies key risks and puts in place appropriate internal controls. The Board provides strategic direction on risk
related decision making to ensure that the appropriate policies are adopted within the Company.
Senior managers have responsibility for identifying and evaluating risks relating to their areas of responsibility.
They are also responsible for implementing and monitoring effective controls to manage these risks. Where
necessary they will bring in expertise from outside the Company to ensure matters are addressed appropriately.
The key risks to the Group continue to be as follows:
Foreign exchange risk
The Group is exposed to movements in foreign currency exchange risk due to revenues and costs in currencies
other than sterling. These risks are monitored and reported on a regular basis.
Liquidity risk
The Group's liquidity is closely monitored with regular and robust reviews with forecast processes around future
cashflows, on both a short term (within three months) and long term (up to five years) basis.

Credit risk
Exposure to credit risk is mitigated through assessing the credit quality of each commercial sponsor and other

Geopolitical risk
At the time of approval of these financial statements, ongoing geopolitical instability and conflict in the Middle
East presents a risk to the Company arising from disruption to the Formula One calendar, with two races scheduled

further race postponements, cancellations, or changes to event logistics.
Such disruptions may have an impact on the Company's revenue generation, cost base, and operational planning,
depending on the nature and duration of any effected events. Williams will continue to monitor the events and
closely work with the FIA to understand the impacts to the business. Mitigations to this risk include limiting
forward contractual obligations in respect of future events.
These risks will continue to be monitored by the Group in 2026 and beyond.

Intangible assets

Intangible assets comprise software which is initially recognised at cost. Amortisation is calculated so as

in the course of construction are stated at cost and are not depreciated until they are available for use. Upon

Stocks and work in progress

Stocks and work in progress are valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value, after due regard for
obsolete and slow moving stocks. Net realisable value is based on selling price less anticipated costs to
completion and selling costs. Cost includes all direct costs and other costs incurred in bringing inventories
to their present location and condition.

Leasing and hire purchase commitments

Assets held under finance leases, which are leases where substantially all the risks and rewards of
ownership of the asset have passed to the Company, and hire purchase contracts are capitalised in the
balance sheet and depreciated over the shorter of the lease term and asset's useful lives. A corresponding
liability is recognised for the lower of the fair value of the leased asset and the present value of the minimum
lease payments in the balance sheet. Lease payments are apportioned between the reduction of the lease
liability and finance charges so as to achieve a constant rate of interest on the remaining balance of the
liability. Rentals payable under operating leases are recognised as an expense on a straight-line basis over
the lease term.

Financial assets (including trade and other debtors)

A financial asset not carried at fair value through profit or loss is assessed at each reporting date to
determine whether there is objective evidence that it is impaired. A financial asset is impaired if objective
evidence indicates that a loss event has occurred after the initial recognition of the asset, and that the loss
event had a negative effect on the estimated future cash flows of that asset that can be estimated reliably.

An impairment loss in respect of a financial asset measured at amortised cost is calculated as the difference
between its carrying amount and the present value of the estimated future cash flows discounted at the
asset’s original effective interest rate. For financial instruments measured at cost less impairment, an
impairment is calculated as the difference between its carrying amount and the best estimate of the amount
that the Company would receive for the asset if it were to be sold at the reporting date. Interest on the
impaired asset continues to be recognised through the unwinding of the discount. Impairment losses are
recognised in profit or loss. When a subsequent event causes the amount of impairment loss to decrease,
the decrease in impairment loss is reversed through profit or loss.

Trade payables and receivables

Trade payables and trade receivables are not interest bearing and are payable or receivable within one year.
They are recorded at their nominal value less any allowance for estimated irrecoverable amounts. Any
losses arising from impairment are recognised in the income statement.

Bank borrowings and overdrafts

Interest bearing loans and overdrafts are initially measured at fair value (which is equal to cost at
inception), and are subsequently measured at amortised cost, using the effective interest rate method. Any
difference between the proceeds net of transaction costs and the amount due on settlement of borrowings
is recognised over the term of the borrowing.
