FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.
Review of the business
The directors consider the financial results for the year ended 30 June 2025 to be satisfactory. Season 2024-25 was the sixth season in the English Football League (‘EFL’) for Salford City Football Club (‘the club’) and the 11th full season that the club operated under the ownership of Project 92.. The operating loss prior to exceptional items of £4,734,124 was anticipated and represents an improvement on the previous year’s operating loss of £5,373,663. A record turnover of £4,386,721 was an increase of 15% on the previous year and included a new two-year commercial partnership with US-based drinks company Sazerac. Profit on sales of player registrations of £824,387 included a sell-on payment received from the sale of former player, Brandon Thomas-Asante, from West Bromwich Albion to Coventry City and the transfer of Callum Hendry to Milton Keynes Dons. This highlights the financial benefits derived from developing young players as part of a successful player trading plan. On 31 July 2024, L H Chan resigned as a director of the company. The Board of Directors wishes to record their gratitude for the significant contribution of Incanto Limited to the success and growth of Salford City Football Club. Outstanding loans owed to Incanto Limited of £25,347,053 and £6,154,995 owed to other shareholders were written off during the year. As a result, the net profit for the financial year was £27,420,185 compared with a net loss of £5,344,768 in the previous year. During the financial year, Nicky Butt stepped down as the club’s Chief Executive and was replaced by Jonathan Jackson on an interim basis. The season proved to be one of steady progress in football performance as the club mounted a sustained challenge for promotion but ultimately an 8th position in League Two meant narrowly missing out on the play-offs by a single point. Across 46 matches, the club recorded 18 wins, 15 draws and 13 defeats, including a run of six consecutive wins in December that propelled the club to 2nd position in the first week of 2025. However, a number of injuries to key players impacted the team’s ability to sustain the same form in the second half of the season. An emphatic 4–1 victory over Colchester United late in the season briefly reignited hopes of a top-seven place but ultimately a final day draw at Carlisle United meant that the club would once again compete in League Two in the 2025/26 season. The first team squad was a blend of experienced professionals supplemented by younger academy-trained players. Schoolboy Marshall Heys created history when he became the youngest player to represent a professional club in the FA Cup when he made his debut against Shrewsbury Town at the age of 15 years, 2 months and 6 days. Victory in this second-round match against League One opponents paved the way for another notable first – reaching the third round for the first time in the club’s history. The reward for reaching the third round was a high-profile away tie against Premier League champions Manchester City in a match that was played in front of 52,586 supporters and broadcast live on BBC 1. While the eventual 8–0 defeat was sobering, the occasion itself represented a landmark moment for the club, offering valuable exposure and financial benefit, as well as an opportunity for players and supporters to experience elite-level opposition. On 8th May 2025, the directors announced that the company had been acquired by a new ownership group led by existing shareholders Gary Neville and David Beckham. The acquisition included a commitment by the new shareholders to invest significantly in the company to fund an ambitious growth strategy with an intention to combine football success with commercial growth enabling a financially sustainable business within five years.

The announcement marked the start of a new chapter for the club, building on the achievements under the ownership of the Class of 92 and providing strong financial foundations to create a successful football club leveraging the extensive commercial experience and value of a unique international ownership group. Principal risks and uncertainties
The directors are responsible for continually assessing the risk factors of the group. The key financial risk is cash flow sustainability ensuring that cash balances are sufficient to fund future obligations arising from liabilities as they fall due. The group seeks to reduce this risk by: Carefully managing its working capital inflows and outflows and ensuring, via regularly updated cash forecasts, that the necessary funding is available to finance the working capital position. Strategies to achieve football success in order to firstly retain its position as an EFL club and then achieve future promotions. Football success will provide the opportunity to maximise commercial revenues to achieve the objective of financial sustainability which will enable the group to become less reliant on shareholder funding within the constraints of football financial regulations. Continued investment in the first team squad and academy player recruitment. Football staff have access to modern analysis and performance measurement tools to assist the first team to recruit suitable players to achieve successful results. In addition, the group is exposed to foreign exchange risk as shareholder capital calls are scheduled to be received in US dollars whilst the cost base remains in sterling. The group seek to mitigate this risk by entering into future fixed-rate conversion agreements. The group is also exposed to interest rate fluctuations on existing bank borrowings. The company does not consider that the size of its interest rate exposure warrants the hedging of potential interest rate changes. Other risks include health and safety issues which are managed via regular internal management meetings, and the use of professional advisors and information received from the EFL and the Football Association.
Development and performance
The performance of the men’s first team was satisfactory in a season that focused on reducing operating losses with a long-term strategy of profitable player trading and investment in the women’s team and academy operations. Following the change in shareholders, promotion to League One remains the club’s immediate objective, aligned to a robust financially sustainable model. Investment in facilities and people funded by commercial revenue growth will provide the foundations for long-term success and stability. Commercial revenue growth will be achieved by utilising the value and connections of the unique shareholder group to create a successful sustainable small club model.
