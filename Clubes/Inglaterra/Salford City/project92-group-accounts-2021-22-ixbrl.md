STRATEGIC REPORT
FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2022
The directors present the strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2022.
Fair review of the business
Season 2021-2022 saw the return of spectators to sporting events, thanks to the development and rollout of a vaccine to combat Covid-19. An accompanying decline in the number of positive cases provided a basis for a degree of normality to return, although there would be significant challenges along the way. On 7th August 2021 Salford City’s opening game of the EFL League Two season -vs- Leyton Orient became the first fixture to be played in front of spectators at The Peninsula Stadium since March of the previous year. An attendance of 1,968, including 474 visiting supporters, may have been considered a little disappointing but it became clear that many fans did not yet have the confidence to return to crowded venues. Attendances would slowly recover as the season progressed but the emergence of the Omicron variant of Covid-19, along with uncertainty regarding its virulence, again raised the prospect of attendances being legally restricted. In December 2021 a law was passed obliging football clubs with a stadium capacity of 10,000 or more to perform mandatory vaccination checks on adult supporters seeking admission. With a stadium capacity of 5,032 for Salford City F.C. there was not the requirement to perform these admission checks, but the effect of what at the time threatened to be a resurgence of the pandemic carried consequences. The home game -vs- Stevenage on 18th December 2021 attracted just 1,375 spectators and potentially lucrative home fixtures over the Christmas/New Year period were postponed due to rates of Omicron infection amongst the visiting clubs’ first-team squads.
Fortunately, the Omicron variant proved to be less dangerous than had been feared, and with all restrictions lifted the club was able to complete a full EFL season in front of spectators, at the third attempt. Attendances would remain modest throughout the season, due mostly to Covid-19 concerns. Gary Bowyer had reinvigorated performances after taking the manager’s job on an initially temporary basis late in season 2020-2021. Hopes were high for 2021-2022, with a strong squad recruited. Despite continued investment in the recruitment of players, a poor first half of the season left the team in the bottom half of the table league positions. Results improved dramatically from February onwards and a run of 11 unbeaten games – 7 of them wins – encouraged real hopes of a play-off place. Unfortunately, the patchy form returned for the final seven games and Salford City finished in 10th position, 7 points outside of the play-off places. In the end season 2021-2022 did not deliver on its initial promise, but the club continued to enjoy plenty of media attention. As well as both FA Cup ties being televised, the final home league game of the season -vs- Mansfield Town was selected for live broadcast. The club’s documentary series Class of 92: Full Time was broadcast in December 2021 and its continued popularity ensured the commissioning of a further series. The ongoing high level of investment in the playing squad meant that failure to qualify for the play-offs was seen as a major disappointment and Gary Bowyer was relieved of his managerial duties at the end of the season.

Key Performance Indicators
The return of spectators following the global pandemic was very welcome indeed. Even so, the tentative recovery of attendances was affected by disappointing results and modest entertainment value on the playing side. Every professional football club was profoundly affected by the pandemic. In Salford City’s case, the curtailment of its first EFL season then playing without spectators for the whole of the second had a real effect on levels of support. The enthusiasm and momentum that accompanied four promotions in five seasons dissipated over the period of restrictions and has yet to be entirely reignited. The result of this being that the metrics were adversely affected for the season against what would be expected, however we are already seeing signs of positivity that things are heading towards a ‘more normal’ season for 2022-2023, in which we would hope KPI’s and forecasts can be reviewed against a more traditional set of circumstances.
Risks and Uncertainties
With supporters allowed to attend fixtures at the Peninsula Stadium throughout season 2021-2022, hopes are high that the restrictions of the pandemic may now be behind us. It seems though that World events may conspire to produce another set of very challenging economic conditions. The club would like to put on record thanks for our main sponsors, TalkTalk and Peninsula, who have continued to show us tremendous support and encouragement throughout this an extremely challenging season.
Development & Performance
The club’s primary focus since promotion to the EFL was secured in 2019 is to continue its on-field progress. Promotion to EFL League One is the immediate priority and once again significant investment has been made available for the construction of a squad capable of achieving this ambition. In June 2022 Neil Wood, former lead coach of Manchester United’s Under 23 side was appointed to the role of Salford City Head Coach. It is hoped that a fresh approach may stimulate a return to the momentum that drove the club up the non-league pyramid, as well as providing a style of football that will encourage supporters to attend. The development of young players is another priority, and the club is looking at operating a Level Three Academy in the 2023-2024 season. New intakes of scholars and EDS (Elite Development Squad) players have been recruited and it is hoped that a path of progression to the first team will be established over the coming seasons. Seven former Academy/EDS players made first-team appearances during the 2021-2022 season with striker Brandon Thomas-Asante the most prominent. His progress has been impressive since signing for the club and the attention of clubs in higher divisions has been attracted. Since the 2021/22 season Brandon has been sold to West Bromwich Albion F.C. for an undisclosed fee. Salford City Lionesses have continued to progress, reaching the Second Round of the Women’s FA Cup during the 2021-2022. Their historic run was eventually ended by Newcastle United in a tie played at the Peninsula Stadium. The aim of reaching the national level of the women’s game is a very achievable one. Despite the financial challenges of very recent years, the club continues to invest in infrastructure, technology and staffing levels. The work required to operate an efficient, progressive club at EFL level is considerable, but the structure to meet these challenges is taking shape.

Future Developments
Promotion is the priority on the playing side and the club continues to be backed financially by shareholders and directors. In addition, a continually evolving commercial strategy and increasingly sophisticated marketing are tailored specifically to generating revenue. As a club we continue to look at ways of establishing lasting relationships with Premier League and Championship clubs, with the intention of improving our utilization of the loan player market. During the 2021-2022 loan signings made in excess of 80 first-team appearances, with Corrie Ndaba (signed on loan from Ipswich Town) picking up the club’s Player of the Season award. Although Salford City as a football club has been around for over 80 years, it has only very recently risen to relative prominence. Not too long ago an attendance of three figures was considered to be very healthy, and the club perhaps lacks a significant traditional fan base. Considerable progress has been made to embed the club in the life of the city and a real focus on engaging with supporters is at the heart of our engagement strategy. Considerable efforts are being put into improving the matchday experience, expanding the reach of the club into the local community and raising our local profile. It is hoped and expected that these activities will encourage a sustainable increase in our ‘hard-core’ support. Since the club became fully professional the provision of suitable training facilities has been a challenge. A new venue is being sought for the future. In the short to medium term, a newly refurbished Partington Sports Village is being used.

| 1.9 | Impairment of fixed assets |  |

At each reporting period end date, the group reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the impairment loss (if any). Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. Recognised impairment losses are reversed if, and only if, the reasons for the impairment loss have ceased to apply. | 1.12 | Financial instruments |  |

The group has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12 ‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments. Financial instruments are recognised in the group's balance sheet when the group becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument. Financial assets and liabilities are offset and the net amounts presented in the financial statements when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.
Other financial assets
Other financial assets, including investments in equity instruments which are not subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures, are initially measured at fair value, which is normally the transaction price. Such assets are subsequently carried at fair value and the changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss, except that investments in equity instruments that are not publicly traded and whose fair values cannot be measured reliably are measured at cost less impairment.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting end date. Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are settled, or when the group transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the group after deducting all of its liabilities.
