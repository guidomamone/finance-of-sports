CHAIR’S
REPORT
Last year was a pivotal one for the Club and the
game of cricket.

England Men’s T20 team had a 23-run victory
over Pakistan with skipper Jos Buttler putting
in a match-winning 84 off 51 balls. A balanced
England bowling attack, featuring the return
of Jofra Archer and two wickets for Moeen Ali
proved too much for Pakistan. A clean sweep
of England wins at Edgbaston reinforced our
standing as a favoured venue for players and
fans alike.

In the fast-changing global dynamics of cricket,
the game here needs a world class short form
tournament, alongside our domestic Blast, to
develop playing talent, engage and develop
these new audiences, as well as secure England
& Wales’ status and influence in the world game.

As we enter 2025, the decisions taken over
the last 12 months will start to play through. I’m
pleased to report the Club has been at the centre
of helping shape these progressive changes, in
parallel with securing a core role on all fronts for
the Club and Edgbaston. More of that later.
A seventh place finish in the 2024 County
Championship, with only one win and nine draws,
represented a backward step on 2023 and left
us 37 points off third place which had been
our minimum target. Everyone at the Club was
understandably frustrated and none more so
than the players, coaches and support staff.

However, we must recognise we have built a
strong men’s Blast squad over the last five years
and we should remain confident the team has the
ability to achieve our goals.
2024 was the last season of the Central Sparks
as we move to a new structure for the women’s
professional game in 2025.

At the end of the season we initiated the most
comprehensive review of our set-up ever
undertaken by the Club, looking at every high
performance angle with help from independent
experts. We continue to implement the results
and, as you’ll have seen, we have strengthened
both our red and white ball squads, as well as our
support structures.

We were delighted to have secured one of the
eight Tier one teams in that new structure and
are looking forward to Bears Women’s inaugural
season. The growth in women’s professional
sport has been phenomenal and with both the
Bears Women and Birmingham Phoenix Women,
we’re now in pole position to energise this
progress in cricket.

The review led to some people changes. In
particular, by mutual consent, Mark Robinson
left after four seasons as men’s First Team
Coach. During that time, we won the County
Championship and Bob Willis Trophy in 2021. I’d
like to thank Mark and wish him every success
for the future.

In 2024, Phoenix Women made significant
progress and although eventually finishing
seventh, it was a very close competition and we
were only one win off a place in the Eliminator
match. No doubt they will look to build further on
that in 2025.

While the home quarter final loss in the
Vitality Blast campaign was again frustrating, the
team topped the North Group and matched the
previous season’s record 11 wins. The High
Performance Review also included our white ball
campaign and again there are learnings which
are being implemented.

In the coaching and back-office staff we have
more than 200 years of cricketing experience
and 75 years of combined experience as a Bear.
Mixing this with James’ experience in global sport
creates something special.
As we move into our next four-year strategic
cycle, we have to do things differently if we want
to get back to winning ways. We’ve done that, but
please be patient. It may take a season to settle
everything down, but we’re building something
that we hope will deliver consistent success up to
2028 and beyond.

As our Chair Mark McCafferty has said, the game
is going through massive change and we have
worked hard to make sure the Bears stay at the
forefront of new developments at Edgbaston and
further afield.
During times of change, it’s important to have a
plan and we’ve been well guided by our strategy
during its final year. I’ll touch on our successes
below.
This new coaching team is already having a
positive impact with players and there is a fresh
buzz around the dressing room.

But first and foremost we are a cricket club, here
to serve the Members, and we can’t hide from the
fact we didn’t achieve our goals in 2024 of being
in the Vitality County Championship’s top three
going in to the last block of games and reaching
Vitality Blast Finals Day. Last season was the third
in succession where we’ve not put ourselves in a
position to challenge for a title.

Of course, things are always good at this stage
– after all, we haven’t lost a game yet! But we
go into the new season with a new coaching
team, high quality overseas recruits and new
domestic recruits.
Following Gavin Larsen’s departure, James
Thomas will soon join us from Manchester City
FC as Performance Director. His decision to
leave one of the world’s most advanced high
performance environments to become a Bear
is testament to the power of Warwickshire as a
sporting entity and also the interest in cricket
as we move to new territory with women’s
professional cricket and The Hundred, as well
as our burning desire for the men’s team to
dominate county cricket.

To address this we launched a detailed review
of the last four years into all aspects of men’s
cricket. It’s led to considerable change – some
big, some small – designed to deliver better
results as well as help us successfully launch
Bears Women and integrate Birmingham
Phoenix.
Changes range from ‘small tweaks’ that could
make a big difference, such as the introduction
of two more hybrid pitches which allows us to
play the group and quarter-final Blast games
on the same style wickets, through to ‘material’
changes. Most notably that’s seen us introduce a
new men’s coaching structure with Ian Westwood
taking the reins as First Team Coach from
Mark Robinson; Simon Kerrigan coming in as
Second XI Coach; and Matt Walker joining as
Batting Coach. Stuart Barnes continues as
Bowling Coach.

DRONE SHOW LIGHTS UP
EDGBASTON STADIUM

The ‘Evolution’ drone show on 26 October
replaced the traditional fireworks night as a more
environmentally-friendly event.
Around 4,000 people attended what was the first
drone display of its kind in Birmingham.
The immersive event featured 620 drones, each
equipped with changeable, colourful LED lights,
which took spectators on “a journey through time
and space”.
It was a narrated story starting with the Big Bang,
the explosive birth of the universe, and plotted
the course of time through to modern day.

Edgbaston Events Manager Ella Hogan said:
“We know our fireworks event has been hugely
popular over the years but we felt it was time
for change and to look at an alternative, more
sustainable option – but one that still has the
wow factor.
“There was no noise pollution, no loud bangs,
or firework debris issues that we know had
concerned some of our near neighbours.
“The drones performed a dazzling animation
in the night sky with each one’s flight path
pre-programmed and mapped out. It really was
quite special.”
Edgbaston is working with Celestial – creators
of drone light shows for brands across the globe
– and local experiences company Yuup to bring
more shows to the stadium.

# CHIEF OPERATING OFFICER'S REPORT

In summary, revenue in 2024 was lower than in 2023, principally driven by the attractiveness of the Men's Ashes Test Match in 2023 compared to England versus West Indies hosted in July 2024. Costs were also substantially higher in 2023 primarily due to the larger Major Match Day (MMD) staging fees paid to The England and Wales Cricket Board (ECB) and the additional three months' salary costs (October 2023 to December 2023) paid in the previous 15 month financial period.

The sales outlook for 2025 is positive with good demand for the England vs India Men's Test Match, Men's One Day International, T20 Blast Finals Day and Women's IT20.

The Club is continuing to progress the stadium Masterplan with the next phase of development works having received planning approval from the City Council in February 2025. The rebuilding of the Raglan and Priory stands with an integrated hotel is due to start in September 2025 following the Club hosting the T20 Finals Day.

## PROFIT AND LOSS ACCOUNT

Income for the year ended 31 December 2024 at £25.1m was £7.7m (23%) lower than the 15 month period ended 31 December 2023, which was predominantly due to our 2024 Test Match attracting lower ticket and hospitality sales than the Ashes Test Match in 2023. Catering and hospitality revenues were also negatively impacted by the game finishing in three days in 2024 compared to the five day match in 2023.

**DEFERRED TAXATION**

Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences which are differences between taxable profits and total comprehensive income that arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from those in which they are recognised in the financial statements, except that unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that the directors consider that it is probable that they will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.

**PENSION COSTS**

**LEASING AND HIRE PURCHASE COMMITMENTS**

**INTEREST BEARING LOANS AND BORROWINGS**
