# Chief Operating Officer’s Report

Both revenue and EBITDA in 2021 were substantially higher than in 2020, as the effect of the COVID-19 pandemic reduced with restricted crowds returning to stadia. As mentioned in the Chairman’s report, both the England vs New Zealand Test Match in June and England vs Pakistan One Day International in July were included in the Government’s Event Research Programme (ERP), investigating the safe return of crowds to major events, which resulted in significantly increased retail and hospitality sales.

Whilst the Club continues to be diligent around the potential future impact of COVID-19 there are more encouraging signs for 2022 and beyond with strong early ticket sales for our 2022 Major Match Day programme and an upturn in non-match day event bookings, reflecting a strong desire from our fans and customers to return to live events.

**Profit and loss account**

Turnover at £17.6m was £6.0m (52%) higher compared to 2020, with increases in cricket income, catering, hospitality and sponsorship thanks to the return of crowds and the commencement of the new Hundred competition. ECB income was lower compared to the previous year primarily due to a £1.0m development fee received in 2020.

Costs of sales increased by £4.3m (67%) as a direct result of the return of crowds and increased activity in 2021 compared to the previous year. Cricket cost increases were mainly due to cricket staging fees and other match activation costs. Catering and hospitality costs increased in line with the respective revenue upturns.

Administration costs increased by £790,000 (16%) due to the increase in activity compared to 2020. Whilst staff costs remained stable, increased cricket and event activity increased costs such as utilities and match insurance premiums.

Our principal measure of profitability is operating EBITDA and this increased by £982,000 (54%) to £2.8m in 2021. After depreciation, the accounts show an operating profit of £1.2m compared to a £281,000 profit in 2020.

Below operating profit, the interest charge in 2021 is £1.3m (2020: £1.3m), primarily due to the interest payments to Birmingham City Council to service the secured loan.

There is a net tax charge of £195,000 (2020: £124,000 credit), which reflects a decrease in the Group deferred tax asset and return to profit for Edgbaston Experience Limited, our stadium events, catering and hospitality subsidiary. The minority interest of £247,000 (2020: £259,000 credit) reflects Compass’ minority shareholding in Edgbaston Experience Limited. The above items resulted in a retained loss of £583,000 for 2021 (2020: loss of £591,000).

**Balance sheet and cash flow**

The fixed asset base increased by £543,000 driven by £2.3m of additions for the year and a depreciation charge of £1.8m for 2021. The significant capital projects in the year include the ongoing development works being undertaken as part of phase 2 of the Edgbaston stadium Masterplan along with the ongoing development of the Edgbaston Community Sports Ground facility so that the Club can potentially use it for first team men’s cricket in 2022.

Both debtors and creditors increased as activity for both match and non-match days returned, with various cricket related income and expenditure relating to the season accrued at the year end.

Group cash reserves increased by £2.4m to £7.9m, primarily due to a significant amount of tickets for our 2022 Major Match day programme being sold in advance in September 2021.

The minority interest of £2m (2020: £1.8m) represents Compass’ minority share in the net assets of Edgbaston Experience Limited.
