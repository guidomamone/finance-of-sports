Bath Rugby Limited
Strategic Report for the Year Ended 30 June 2022
The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2022.
Principal activity
The principal activity of the company is operation of a professional rugby union club.
The club is also focussed on developing the Bath Rugby brand and in fostering a strong sense of pride and belonging
from our stakeholders. These include our staff and players, supporters, corporate partners, suppliers, the local
community including local residents, the business community and other community organisations along with other
stakeholders in the club and in the City where the club plays its matches at The Rec.
It is our goal to operate sustainably as a club and as a business in terms of financial sustainability, in terms of
sustainable performance success, and in terms of the impact we have across our communities.
Fair review of the business
Rugby review
The 21/22 season was an incredibly challenging season for everyone involved with the club: staff, players,
supporters, corporate partners and wider stakeholders. The club finished the season bottom of the Gallagher
Premiership.
It was clear early in the 21/22 season that changes needed to be made to enable a reset and to provide the
foundations for a revival of the club over the coming seasons.
We were delighted to appoint Johann van Graan as Head of Rugby, assuming full responsibility for rugby
matters, in good time to lead our 22/23 pre-season heading into the 22/23 season campaign. Johann brings
huge experience from his time coaching at the Bulls, the Springboks, and latterly for five seasons at Munster.
We bolstered Johann’s coaching staff with the recruitment of JP Ferreira as Defence Coach and Joe Maddock
as Attack Coach. Other notable changes included new Heads of Medical (Rory Murry) and of Athletic
Performance (Alex Martin), together with a dedicated Nutritionist (Sarah Jenner).
In addition, investment was made in specific enhancements to the gym and other team facilities at the club’s
High Performance Centre at Farleigh ahead of the 22/23 pre-season commencing.
These investments have borne fruit with improved performances in the early rounds of the 22/23 season, with
the club’s revival now underway.
The focus of Johann and his team has been implementing and embedding their high performance model.
Specific areas of strategic focus in high performance, recruitment and retention to support the long-term
squad strategy, and player pathways are under review and further KPIs are being developed.
The club continues in its commitment to developing outstanding home grown talent as a core pillar of our
squad strategy. During the 21/22 season, 11 players who have been developed through our academy, played
senior team rugby for the club, and nine players who were developed in our Academy debuted in the senior
team. We were also delighted that seven players represented England during the season, two of those being
injury replacements.
The club now has strong foundations in place to drive rugby performance in the 22/23 season and beyond.

Business review
The impact of the Covid-19 pandemic on sport, on rugby, and on the club was heavy. Whilst this impact was felt most
significantly in the 20/21 season and financial year, it continued to cast a long shadow over 21/22 as we emerged
from many of the government restrictions which had been in place.
This review considers the key areas of business performance, and also the wider areas of the club’s impact.

Central income
Income distributed by Premiership Rugby (PRL) and the Rugby Football Union (RFU) to clubs suffered due to the
impact of the Covid pandemic. With fans being allowed back into stadiums in 21/22 for club and international fixtures
including the Premiership Final, the Six Nations and Autumn Internationals, revenue streams have improved for both
PRL and the RFU. However, revenues have not recovered fully to pre-pandemic levels, with PRL recovering the
income it overdistributed in the 2019/20 and 2020/21 seasons to aid the clubs’ financial difficulties during the Covid
crisis.
Sadly, the 22/23 season has seen Worcester and Wasps entering administration due to significant financial
challenges and both clubs have been suspended from the Premiership. This has been tragic for the clubs, their staff,
players and supporters. Their demise also impacts PRL and the other Premiership clubs, not least in terms of
cancelled fixtures.
Gate Receipts
The 2021/22 season saw a return to games played in front of home crowds and although Covid restrictions still led to
two cancelled home games, the club experienced a strong 95% attendance rate across its home Premiership fixtures
in the 21/22 season. The loyal and passionate support the club has received from its fans has continued to be
outstanding.
The club was extremely grateful for the strong season ticket renewals for the 22/23 season in a difficult economic
climate. The 22/23 season started with a run of home games at The Rec and again the support from the fans has
been unwavering. With both economic uncertainty and changes to fixtures due to suspension of Worcester and
Wasps from the league, gate sales at the start of the 22/23 season have not been as high as those experienced in the
21/22 season. However, with recent world-class signings the Club is targeting strong gate receipts over the remainder
of the 22/23 season.

Commercial Activities
The Clubs’ sponsorship, merchandising, match day (excluding gate receipts) and grassroots revenue totalled £6.6m in
the 21/22 financial year. This was an increase of £3.9m on the prior year, with the 20/21 season being materially
impacted by the requirement for games to take place behind closed doors.
Sponsorship revenue totalled £2.9m, an increase of £1.1m on the previous season . The 21/22 season was the first
year with new partners Avon Protection, Your Red Car, Lombard Odier, Huboo and 1st Office.
The club's official partnership portfolio also includes Dyson as the title sponsor, Thatchers, Grant UK, Macron, St
Austell and Novia. The club's focus for the 22/23 season and beyond is to expand its sponsorship portfolio, and further
opportunities will be developed through the planned stadium development.
Other drivers of the increase in revenue from commercial activities in 21/22 were Corporate Hospitality revenue of
£1.7m vs £63k in 20/21, and Matchday Food & Beverage revenue of £1.1m vs £63k in 20/21.
The club is investing resource in building its fanbase and following as a core pillar of its commercial strategy. The club
engages with current and new supporters through the matchday experience, other experiences and events including
the extensive community engagement programme run by the club’s Grassroots team in clubs and schools across our
region, and through digital channels.
As at 30 June 2022 the club's fanbase and following included:

* Marketable database records of 83.5k (30 June 2021: 82.6k).

, social media following of 385k (30 June 2021: 216K) including followings across Twitter (135K), Instagram

(116K) and Facebook (108K), and to a lesser extent YouTube, LinkedIn and TikTok.
Operating costs
The activities of the club are dependent on the hard work and commitment of its staff and players, who represent the
largest area of investment by the club comprising 61% (2021: 64%) of total operating costs.
The club restructured during the pandemic which included a number of roles being made redundant. In addition, staff
and players accepted a percentage reduction to their wages to support the club in managing the impact of the
pandemic.
The year-on-year increase in staff costs of £0.3m to £12.2m for the 21/22 financial year is, in part, due to the return of
salaries to ‘normal’.
The club continues to invest in its people, and this includes a commitment to make full use of the Salary Cap -
including various allowances for injury dispensation, international players, home grown and academy players - to
support Johann van Graan in his recruitment and squad strategy. In the 22/23 season notable signings for the club
have been Ollie Lawrence, Ted Hill and Fergus Lee-Warner.
Despite the significant year-on-year increase in revenue in 21/22, operating costs have remained relatively static. This
demonstrates the relatively high proportion of fixed costs in the clubs’ operations, including people and property costs,
such as rent and rates.

Balance sheet and Funding
During the pandemic the club secured a loan totalling £5.3m from the Department of Culture and Sport (DCMS). The
loan carries an interest rate of 2% and includes a repayment holiday.
Bruce Craig, the club’s owner, continues to support the club financially and has indicated that he will continue to do so
for the foreseeable future. During the year, Bruce Craig increased his loan to the club by £1.7m. The balance on the
loan at the year-end is £17.5m, this loan is interest free and repayable upon 12 months’ notice. The loan is adjusted
for net present value in the accounts.
At the time of signing the accounts the club have no payments due to HMRC beyond the standard monthly and
quarterly balances which are paid as they fall due.
The club holds its investment in PRL at a value of £20.5m. The valuation is based upon management's review of the
valuation provided by PRL and the weighted average cost of capital of the club. The review by management of the
valuation for the current financial year has resulted in no change.
Bath Rugby's wider impact:
In the City:
The club has implemented a number of programmes to bring Bath Rugby into the City of Bath, in particular with
players working more closely with the local community. This includes reading and mentoring programmes in local
schools and working closely with senior residents of local almshouses. These initiatives are being expanded upon in
the coming season, to support the contribution and positive impact the club can deliver across the city.
A new stadium:
On the 18th October 2022, the Supreme Court formally refused an application seeking to appeal the ruling of the
Court of Appeal of 4th February 2022 in relation to covenants pertaining to the Recreation Ground, Bath.
This brings to an end the long-running legal case relating to the 1922 covenants and paves the way for Bath Rugby to
bring forward proposals for a new stadium in the heart of the city.
Bath Rugby has played its rugby at the Recreation Ground, its spiritual home, since 1894. The club is also
tremendously proud of the significant economic, sporting and cultural contribution it makes to Bath. Stadium
development will not only underpin the long-term financial sustainability of the club, it will also enable the club to
continue contributing to the city and wider community over the long-term.

Grassroots
The club's Community team has been renamed “Grassroots”. The Grassroots team manage a significant community
engagement programme across local clubs, schools and the wider community across our region of Dorset, Somerset
and Wiltshire.
The Grassroots team are focussed on engaging and inspiring the next generation of rugby fans, at a time when rugby
participation has struggled even before the impact of the Covid-19 pandemic accelerated decreasing participation
rates.
The Grassroots team have expanded their reach in the year, now partnering with 45 schools across Dorset, Somerset
and Wiltshire, along with 30 clubs through the Club Affiliate programme which provides coaching support and CPD,
along with other support to local rugby clubs across our region. Other programmes such as ‘Move With Bath’ and ‘On
your feet’, a walking rugby initiative, have engaged an older audience with the club.
The Grassroots team delivered the Land Rover Cup, working with 32 grassroots teams, totalling 640 players and led
three Dyson 7s events across North Dorset RFC, Warminster RFC and a Finals Day at The Rec.
The year has also seen a focus on increasing participation of girls in grassroots rugby with specific participation hubs
and camps targeted for girls - see further comments below.
Women and Girls’ Rugby:
The club publicly committed to investing in women’s and girls’ rugby in May 2021, and set out its roadmap through
four core pillars: Participation, Pathway, Promote and Professional Team.
Participation was launched in the 21/22 season with the employment of Maria Crowfoot as the Women and Girls’
Rugby Development Lead with the aim of doubling participation across our region over three years. The club's first
girls-only participation hubs were set up across the region. In 21/22 we operated six hubs across Somerset, Dorset
and Wiltshire engaging with over 500 U13-U18 girls.
Pathway was launched in 22/23 and included the establishment of a partnership with Bath College to provide places
for students with elite rugby aspirations, similarly to the men’s pathway partnership with Beechen Cliff School, with five
female participants in this first year. In addition, the first Bath Rugby women’s 7s team was launched in late summer
2022 to provide a player pathway to competitive rugby.
Promote includes the commitment to staging two double headers each season (Bath Rugby Premiership match,
followed by Bath Rugby Ladies match). Three double headers have been staged at The Rec: vs Supermarine in
October 2021 (3,000 supporters), vs Buckingham Swans in March 2022 (2,500 spectators) and OA Saints in October
2022.
The club is currently undertaking the RFU’s tender process to participate in the next cycle of the Premiership 15s
professional women's competition.
A core pillar of the club’s women and girls’ programme is its partnership with Bath Rugby Ladies, in place since May
2021. Bath Rugby Ladies is the women’s division of the amateur club Bath RFC. Bath Rugby Ladies compete in the
second tier of the women’s game, Championship South 1, which is currently the second tier of women’s rugby and the
top community level. The partnership with Bath Rugby Ladies includes the double header games noted above, along
with knowledge sharing and the provision of coaching resource.

Sustainability
The club is undertaking a sustainability review as part of the work to bring forward proposals for new world-class
stadium facilities at The Rec.
The club has implemented a number of sustainability measures, including a commitment to zero single-use plastics at
The Rec. Water refill fountains are situated around the ground and refillable bottles and cans of water are sold at all
the bars.
Bath Rugby Foundation
The club works very closely with its charitable arm, Bath Rugby Foundation (BRF), a standalone registered charity.
BRF works primarily with children and young adults who often face significant challenges in their lives, empowering
them with confidence, life skills and pathways to independence. The club provides support for BRF in a number of
ways including marketing, communications and fundraising, and works closely with BRF, including staff and players
engaging on delivery programmes.
Bath is one of the most unequal cities in England and our Local Education Authority (LEA) is ranked 146 out of 150 for
their ‘attainment gap’. BRF works with around 4,000 vulnerable young people across the club’s region annually. This
work includes:
, Supporting the social and learning needs of vulnerable children in the most underprivileged primary and
secondary schools in the area;
, Helping marginalised and homeless 14-19 years olds (and up to age 24 with Special Educational Needs) to
gain vital qualifications, work experience and life skills at BRF’s Alternative Learning Hub;
. Running community hubs and holiday camps across Bath and North East Somerset supporting struggling
families;
. Working with children with Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) giving them opportunities to
access sport and social event in a fun, safe environment with specialist coaches.
Principal risks and uncertainty
Team performance and Premiership league standing
The performance of the team impacts revenues including gate receipts, sponsorship and hospitality income, and retail
sales. The club manages this risk through investment in a world-class squad, coaching and support staff; and through
the execution of its strategic rugby plan.
PRL and RFU delivering on their growth plans
PRL have an ambitious growth plan over the next five years and this forms part of the Club’s plan to become
financially sustainable. Should PRL fail to deliver the planned growth, all clubs in the league would be negatively
impacted. This risk is mitigated by the club supporting PRL in executing its strategic plan, whilst not placing sole
reliance on PRL’s growth. The club is developing innovative new revenue streams, and is also pushing forward with
plans to build a new stadium in the heart of Bath which would provide significant incremental revenue.

Compliance with Salary Cap and wider PRL and RFU and regulations
Failure to comply could lead to fines and other potentially material penalties. The club mitigates this risk, in particular
with regard to the Salary Cap, by operating an internal working group who meet regularly and are responsible for
managing the Salary Cap and overseeing squad recruitment decisions. Additionally, all documentation on regulatory
requirements is kept up to date and the club carefully manages adherence to matters such as minimum standards
required by central governing bodies.
Compliance with other financial and legal regulatory matters
The club has appropriate policies in place to manage its obligations regarding employment law and other relevant
areas of law and compliance, although it does not consider these are areas of significant strategic risk to its
operations.
Liquidity and funding
The continued availability of funding from key stakeholders is important to the on-going operations and outlook for the
club. The club undertakes rigorous annual budgeting and longer-term financial planning processes. The club
continues to have the committed support of its sole shareholder and owner, and separately manages on-going
communication with DCMS to ensure compliance with covenants and other requirements in relation to its borrowings.
New stadium build
The club’s plans to redevelop its facilities at The Rec and provide world-class facilities which generate significant
incremental revenues. There is a risk that planning permission for the new stadium is not granted, or is delayed. This
risk is mitigated through the engagement of an excellent design team and wider team of consultants, with both
sporting and heritage expertise, to create a design which is sensitive to its city centre location. At the same time, the
club continues to manage strong relationships with the local planning authority officers to address key matters ahead
of submitting a planning application.
Going Concern
The directors have considered the working capital requirements of the club for the foreseeable future taking account
of planned investment in people, operating costs, capital expenditure and other potential investments. The directors
have also considered the funding in place and the commitment to support future funding from the owner, together with
the loan in place with DCMS.
These considerations have been made in the context of a challenging economic environment. The UK economy
officially entered recession in the summer of 2022, and inflation is forecast by The Bank of England to hit 11% by the
end of 2022. Further to this, the IMF are warning of a high risk of global recession. The impact of this in real terms is
that families will experience a dramatic increase in their living costs and disposable income will be reduced. Many
businesses will be reviewing their costs and identifying where they generate value.

have reviewed the club’s current and future business activities, together with the factors likely to impact those
activities and the financial performance and position of the club. A number of factors were considered by the directors
in making this assessment of going concern, including:

. Implications of the current economic climate, in particular the impact of rising inflation and interest rates and

the wider impact of the war in Ukraine;
* The availability of future funding from the club’s shareholder and other potential stakeholders;
Consideration of the lamentable circumstances surrounding two Premiership clubs which recently entered
° administration, and what the implications on other clubs might be;

Business outlook
The financial landscape for the club remains challenging, as is the case for all Premiership clubs. Rising inflation and
pressure across cost lines, together with the broader economic climate mean that the club needs to continue to
manage its costs carefully, whilst also investing in rugby success and being innovative in generating new sources of
revenue.
The club has a long-term strategic plan for delivering rugby success and a financially sustainable business. Despite
the challenging outlook, the club is lucky to have committed and supportive stakeholders, in particular supporters and
corporate partners. The club is also committed to supporting PRL in achieving its strategic plan which will deliver
increased central income for all of the clubs.
The recent Supreme Court ruling enables the club to bring forward proposals for the development of a world-class
stadium at The Rec, which will generate significant incremental revenue for the club.
There are opportunities for the club, and for Premiership Rugby. The club's goal is to execute its long-term strategic
plan, to reduce its reliance on its committed owner and sole shareholder, and to secure a long-term sustainable future
for the club and its stakeholders.

Revenue recognition
Turnover is recognised to the extent that it is probable that the economic benefits will flow to the Company and the
revenue can be reliably measured. Turnover is measured as the fair value of the consideration received or receivable,
excluding discounts, rebates, value added tax and other sales taxes. The following criteria must also be met before
revenue is recognised:
Sale of goods
Turnover from the sale of goods is recognised when all of the following conditions are satisfied:
* the Company has transferred the significant risks and rewards of ownership to the buyer;
* the Company retains neither continuing managerial involvement to the degree usually
associated with ownership nor effective control over the goods sold;
* the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
* it is probable that the Company will receive the consideration due under the transaction; and
* the costs incurred or to be incurred in respect of the transaction can be measured reliably.
Rendering of services
Turnover from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are provided in
accordance with the stage of completion of the contract when all the following conditions are satisfied:
* the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
* it is probable that the Company will receive the consideration due under the contract;
* the stage of completion of the contract at the end of the reporting period can be measured reliably;
* the costs incurred and the costs to complete the contract can be measured reliably.
Other turnover
Other turnover such as transfer fees and central funding is recognised when all the following conditions are satisfied:
* the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;
* it is probable that the Company will receive the consideration; and
* the costs incurred and costs to complete can be measured reliably.
