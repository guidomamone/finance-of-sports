Bath Rugby Limited

Strategic Report for the Year Ended 30 June 2024

The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2024.

Principal activity

The principal activity of the Club is the operation of a professional rugby union club.

Bath Rugby’s goal is to deliver sustained success on the pitch, to drive growth in the fanbase and wider rugby
participation, and to ensure the long-term financial sustainability of the club, whilst making a significant positive impact
on the local and wider community.

With a strong focus on growing the Bath Rugby brand, the club is committed to building a deep sense of pride and
belonging among all its stakeholders-staff, players, supporters, local clubs, schools, corporate partners, suppliers, and
the entire Bath business community.

The club has submitted plans to create world-class stadium facilities by redeveloping its iconic riverside site in the
heart of Bath, elevating the fan experience and strengthening the club’s connection to the city. It is anticipated that the
stadium plans will be determined at planning committee in 2025. The stadium redevelopment will regenerate the
riverside in Bath, whilst also enabling the long term financial sustainability of the club.

The club’s vision is to be a powerful force for good in Bath, driving positive change and inspiring future generations.
By pursuing success on the pitch and making a profound, lasting impact in the community, Bath Rugby aims to be a
source of pride for the city, focussed on building excellence both on and off the field.

Fair review of the business

Rugby review

The 2023/24 season saw the club take significant strides forward, building on the momentum from the latter
stages of 2022/23 campaign.

Under the leadership of Johann van Graan, the coaching team was further strengthened with the additions of
Lee Blackett as Attack Coach, Richard Blaze as Forwards Coach, and Stevie Scott as Scrum Coach. Club
legend, former player and coach Andy Robinson joined the Academy leading the development of the coaches
and transition players. Rob Burgess was appointed as Head of Recruitment, reinforcing the Club’s long-term
commitment to building an outstanding squad of players comprising world-class talent and home-grown
stars.

Key signings ahead of the season included Scottish international Finn Russell and South African World Cup
winner Thomas du Toit. The return of important players such as Charlie Ewels, Alfie Barbeary, and Will Muir
from long-term injuries further bolstered the squad, providing depth and experience.

The season began with a series of impressive performances, including a landmark away victory over
Saracens in Round 2. The squad’s ability to deliver consistent results saw them top the Premiership table at
Christmas, a testament to the team’s growing confidence and cohesion. In Europe, Bath secured a place in
Round of 16 of the Investec Champions Cup, narrowly missing out on topping their qualifying pool but
demonstrating their ability to compete with the best clubs in the tournament.

The club's commitment to developing outstanding young homegrown talent bore fruit as the U18s academy team
clinched the 2023/24 U18s Premiership title in February, overcoming Northampton in a hard-fought final at Kingsholm.
This historic achievement underscored the success of the club’s investment in its academy and pathway system, a
core pillar in the club’s rugby plan, which will ensure a pipeline of talent for the senior team in the years to come.

As the season progressed beyond the Six Nations window, the team maintained their competitive edge showcasing
their power game and attacking flair. The squad’s depth and consistent performances, honed under Johann and his
coaching staff, ensured they remained in contention for silverware. The team advanced to the Premiership Final after
defeating Sale Sharks in a phenomenal Semi-Final held at The Rec in front of a passionate sell-out home crowd, but
narrowly lost to Northampton Saints, 25-21, in a closely contested final at Twickenham.

Looking ahead, the club is committed to building on the successes of the 2023/24 season and taking its performance
to new heights. With a team building in confidence and belief, and a fiercely dedicated fanbase driving them forward,
the club is well-positioned for continued success in the seasons to come. The club has also focussed on securing
continuity of key personnel to underpin current and future success. The club confirmed in May 2024 the long-term
extension of Johann van Graan’s contract to June 2030, and has subsequently secured extensions to the contracts of
key coaching, support and playing staff.

In the 2024/25 season, the club has emerged as a dominant force in the Gallagher Premiership, leading the league
with dynamic play and impressive try-scoring. The club went into the Six Nations break at the top of the table with a
six-point lead over the second-placed team, and continues to top the Premiership following the resumption of the
league post-Six Nations.

Central income

Central income is distributed to the Premiership clubs by Premiership Rugby Limited (PRL) in relation to revenue
generated from broadcast and other commercial income streams managed by PRL, and by the Rugby Football Union
(RFU) in relation to the role of the clubs in managing the professional club and player development system in
England.

Central income continues to fall short of pre-pandemic levels. Income from PRL was £3.0m (2023: £5.7m) and from
the RFU was £2.3m (2023: £1.7m).

Gate Receipts

The club built on the success of the 2022/23 season's ticket sales, with the majority of Premiership games selling out
in the 2023/24 season and increased attendances experienced in the European games. Season ticket income
increased to £3m, up from £2.8m the previous year. Junior season ticket prices were frozen again as part of the club’s
initiative to encourage young fans to The Rec.

Matchday income saw impressive growth, rising by £2.2m to £3.6m, boosted by increased attendance across all
games and a home semi-final which generated £0.6m in ticket revenue.

The Club is dedicated to creating an unforgettable matchday experience for all our supporters. In the 2023/24 season
the tunnel of noise was further enhanced and became a regular fixture popular with fans and players. The Fan Village
has been expanded to include more entertainment, an increased variety of food offerings {including some from our
own players), and more activities for kids. With exciting rugby being played on the pitch and a festival atmosphere in
the ground the club’s ambition is to fill the stadium for every game, creating fans and generating the revenue needed
to enable the club to achieve financial sustainability.

Commercial Activities

In the 2023/24 season, the Club saw positive growth in several areas of commercial activity. Matchday hospitality
increased by £0.4m, reaching £2.0m, while stadium food and beverage revenue rose by £0.2m to £1.4m. Sponsorship
and advertising also saw a £0.5m boost, totalling £3.3m. Other hospitality and events experienced a significant
increase of £0.7m due to the hosting of two U20’s games and additional friendlies at The Rec.

A new revenue stream saw the club hosting weddings and corporate events at the training ground in Farleigh
Hungerford. This generated £0.1m, further contributing to the overall growth.

Merchandise revenue declined by £0.5m to £0.1m, due to the club transitioning to an outsourced model with Castore.
Under this new arrangement, Bath Rugby receives a commission on sales, rather than selling products directly to the
customer. This has led to an improved bottom line albeit there were operational challenges during the first year of the
deal.

Overall total commercial income was £8.2m, a £1.3m increase from £6.9m in the 2022/23 season. Removing the
merchandise variance gives the true picture of an increase in commercial revenues of £1.9m in comparison to the
2022/23 season.

The club plans to further increase commercial revenues, delivering value to our partners and customers while building
the Bath Rugby brand to create a larger, more diverse and more engaged fanbase.

Operating costs

The Club is committed to controlling costs and making savings where possible while not sacrificing the quality of the
performance on the pitch or the matchday experience. The club remains committed to making full use of the Salary
Cap to support Johann in his recruitment and squad strategy. The quality of the club’s squad supports the primary
objective to deliver sustained rugby success, whilst also underpinning the club’s ambition to grow its brand and
following in England and overseas.

After a significant investment in the Rugby Coaching Staff and players in the 2022/23 season the investment in people
has levelled out with staff costs Increasing by £498K, 3.3% on the previous year, this can be attributed to annual pay
increases.

The club has a large, fixed cost base which is monitored closely for opportunities to make savings or reduce the
impact on the liquidity of the Club through partnerships with key suppliers.

Balance sheet and Funding

During the pandemic the club secured a loan totalling £5.3 million from The Department for Media Sport and Culture
(DCMS). The loan carries an interest rate of 2% and included a repayment holiday until September 2023. The club
has consistently made the twice-yearly repayments when they fall due and is committed to continue to do so, the next
payment falling due in March 2025.

During the year, the club’s owner, Bruce Craig, increased his loan to the club by £5.9m (22/23: £6.725m), the balance
on the loan due at the year-end was £30.1m. The loan is interest free and repayable upon 12 months’ notice. The loan
is adjusted for net present value in the accounts.

The Club continues to hold its investment in PRL at a value of £20.5m. Management has tested a range of possible
valuations and has concluded that the midpoint of these is the appropriate value to hold.

Bath Rugby's wider impact

A new Stadium:

The club submitted updated designs and related technical documents to the council in December 2024 relating to its
planning application for a new stadium at the Rec. The enhanced designs responded to feedback from statutory
stakeholders on the planning application originally submitted in September 2023. The club anticipates the application
being determined at a planning committee in the coming months, another historic milestone on the long journey to
redevelop the club’s historic home at the Rec.

The design proposals aim to modernise and expand facilities to create a state-of-the-art venue that can accommodate
larger crowds and enhance the matchday experience. The design includes a curved roof, ensuring it blends
seamlessly with the city’s heritage while revitalising the riverside area. The development promises economic benefits
by attracting more visitors and events, while preserving the club’s long-standing connection to the city.

For over 125 years, Bath Rugby has called The Recreation Ground (The Rec) home, but its limitations have made it
difficult to meet the demands of modern rugby. The new stadium will address these challenges, offering better
amenities, improved access, and increased community engagement whilst also securing the long-term financial
sustainability of the club. It will also become a vibrant hub for sport, leisure, and culture, benefiting both locals and
visitors, while ensuring the club remains a key part of Bath for generations to come.

Grassroots:

Bath Rugby’s Schools Programme introduces rugby to young people through delivery in local schools. The club
delivers coaching, workshops, and rugby festivals, helping students develop rugby and life skills like teamwork and
resilience.

Residential camps provide young players with an immersive rugby experience, focusing on skill development,
fitness, and teamwork. The camps allow players to train with top coaches in a professional environment, helping them
improve both on and off the pitch while building a strong sense of community.

To boost girls’ participation, Bath Rugby runs dedicated female hubs that offer a safe space for girls to develop
rugby skills and build confidence. These hubs help challenge stereotypes and create more opportunities for girls in
rugby, contributing to the wider push for gender equality within the sport.

Women’s and Girls’ Rugby:

Bath Rugby Women’s & Girls’ Rugby: Strategic Growth and Ambitious Future

Bath Rugby is fully committed to advancing its women’s and girls’ rugby programme, focused on four key pillars:
Participation, Pathway, Performance, and Promotion. The club continues to make important strides in growing and
nurturing talent at all levels, as part of its longer term plans for investment in the women’s game.

Participation: Expanding the Rugby Landscape

Bath Rugby’s women’s and girls’ programme is built on fostering widespread participation. Through outreach efforts
like girls-only events, residentials, skill series, and camps, the club inspires and engages the next generation of
female players. Strong relationships with local clubs and education are expanding opportunities for women’s rugby,
while the excitement around the 2025 Rugby World Cup provides an unprecedented opportunity for growth both
nationally and locally.

Pathway: Building a Clear Route to Elite Rugby

The partnership with the University of Bath strengthens Bath Rugby’s women’s pathway. With the team’s promotion to
Premier South and a top four finish in 2024/25, the club is cementing its commitment to being a key cog in our
pathway. Their Breakthrough programme provides crucial support for over 10 women’s rugby players, offering
world-class facilities and services to help them advance to the highest levels of the game.

Since its partnership with Bath Rugby Ladies (BRL) was launched in 2021, the club has continued its investment and
support for the top-level amateur team. BRL, currently competing in Championship South 1, benefits from athletic
development, physiotherapy. coaching, and team management support provided by the club under the leadership of
Bath Rugby’s women’s performance lead Maria Crowfoot, who has played a pivotal role in guiding the team.

Performance: Ambition for Elite Women’s Rugby

Bath Rugby’s ambition is to compete at the highest level of women’s rugby, with ongoing discussions about entering
the Premiership Women’s Rugby league (PWR). Meanwhile, the club’s 7s team continues to excel, winning multiple
tournaments and securing top finishes in 2024.

Promotion: Raising the Profile of Women’s Rugby

Bath Rugby is focused on raising the profile of women’s rugby, hosting two double headers per season and its
first-ever standalone fixture in 2023/24, attracting over 1,000 attendees. The next double header on March 29th,
featuring BRL against London Irish, will continue this momentum, highlighting the talent of Bath Rugby Ladies and
inspiring the next generation of players.

Sustainability Working Group

At the start of the year, the club established a Sustainability Working Group to develop key focus areas and a
long-term plan to improve the club’s environmental impact. Staff and players completed a sustainability survey,
sharing their perspectives and suggesting future areas for focus. These insights will guide our action plan, balancing
sustainability goals with our commercial objectives.

Farleigh House and Agricultural Initiatives

The club continues to improve the ecological use of the Farleigh House High Performance Centre by enhancing
grazing land and developing bee hives for natural pollination. The Vegetable Garden supports player nutrition, and we
plan to increase the garden’s output to further benefit players and the local environment.

Sustainable Procurement and Waste Management

We are focusing on sustainable procurement by prioritising local suppliers and selecting electric-powered machinery
over engine-fuelled alternatives to reduce environmental impact. Our partnership with MJ Church has improved
waste-streaming practices and reduced costs. The operational team is working on further initiatives to decrease waste
transported from our sites.

Carbon Footprint and PRL Collaboration

Bath Rugby participated in PRL’s carbon footprint initiative and now has a record of its base carbon footprint for the
24/25 season at both The Rec and Farleigh House. This data will help track progress and highlight areas for future
improvement.

Promoting Sustainable Travel

We have worked with B&NES to promote the use of Park & Ride services for matchday travel, encouraging fans to
reduce car use. This has resulted in increased use of Park & Ride services, helping to reduce traffic congestion and
pollution on matchdays.

New Stadium Design and Environmental Opportunities

The next stages of the new stadium design offer the chance to incorporate new technologies and improved operating
practices. We aim to ensure the stadium development benefits the environment and the city of Bath, incorporating
sustainable solutions into the design.

Future Sustainability Focus

The Sustainability Working Group will continue to consolidate actions taken and structure our future sustainability
focus. We are committed to advancing our efforts and making environmental responsibility central to Bath Rugby’s
operations.

Bath Rugby Foundation:

In the 2023-2024 season, the Bath Rugby Foundation continued to make a profound impact on the lives of young
people in Bath and North East Somerset (B&NES), Wiltshire, and Somerset. The Foundation, which has been
dedicated to transforming the lives of vulnerable young people since 2003, focuses on empowering children and
young people who face challenges such as poverty, disability, and social exclusion.

Our Mission: Why We Do What We Do

The Foundation exists to help close the gap between young people in disadvantaged areas and those with better
access to opportunities. In B&NES alone, where 10,000 children live in poverty, the Foundation's work is critical in
offering pathways out of disadvantage. There are pockets of significant inequality in B&NES with areas such as
Twerton and Whiteway falling within the most deprived 10% nationally. The educational attainment gap in the region is
stark, with a 31% gap at Key Stage 3, compared to a national average of 20%. Through sport, education, and
community engagement, the Foundation strives to bridge this gap and offer every young person the chance to
succeed.

Key Achievements in 2023-2024

In the past year, the Foundation supported 5,594 young people, providing essential services to improve physical
health, mental well-being, and educational outcomes. The Foundation’s initiatives included 2,573 hours of physical
activity, fostering an active and healthy lifestyle for vulnerable youth. In addition, 1,947 hot nutritious meals were
provided, ensuring that children had access to proper nutrition, which is essential for both physical health and
cognitive function. The Foundation also delivered 1,516 hours of classroom teaching, helping to bridge educational
gaps and support academic success.

Programmes for Social Inclusion and Support

The Foundation’s Sport For All programme, which includes Project Rugby, Mixed Ability Rugby, Hi5! Clubs, and
BreakOut Camps, reached children and young people facing challenges such as special educational needs and
disabilities (SEND), social, emotional, and mental health difficulties (SEMH). These programmes are designed to
combat the decline in physical activity, exacerbated by the Covid-19 pandemic, and promote inclusivity, teamwork,
resilience, and self-confidence through sport.

Hi5! Clubs specifically address the issues of social isolation and physical inactivity faced by young people with SEND.
These after-school clubs provide a safe, supportive environment where young people can engage in physical activity,
develop new skills, and form meaningful social connections.

The Alternative Learning Hub (ALH)

A cornerstone of the Foundation’s educational work, the Alternative Learning Hub (ALH), provides an inclusive and
flexible educational setting for children who struggle with mainstream education. The ALH focuses on academic
achievement and personal development, offering a tailored approach that adapts timetables and sessions to the
needs of each learner. The success of the ALH is evident, with 95% of students passing exams-an impressive
achievement for students who had often been excluded from traditional educational paths.

Community and Collaborative Partnerships

In the 2023-2024 season, the Foundation forged strong collaborations with 53 schools, 19 businesses, 8 local service
providers, 7 local authorities, and 2 colleges, ensuring that their programs reach a wide audience. These partnerships
are critical to the Foundation’s work, enabling it to provide tailored services that address the specific needs of different
communities. A highlight of these partnerships was the Summer Scrum event, which brought together students and
staff from participating schools for a day of multi-sport activities, team-building exercises, and celebration of their
achievements.

Impact on Mental Health

The Foundation also delivered 180 hours of mental health workshops, helping young people navigate the pressures
they face. These workshops focus on improving emotional well-being, fostering resilience, and teaching coping
strategies to support mental health in an increasingly challenging world.

Core Values and Long-Term Vision

At the heart of the Foundation's work are its core values: Collaboration, Trust, Hope, Belief, and Compassion. These
values guide the Foundation in delivering its mission to ensure every child, regardless of background, has access to
opportunities for success. As the Foundation continues its work in B&NES and beyond, it remains committed to
closing the gaps in educational achievement, health, and well-being, creating a future where no child is left behind.
Through its comprehensive programmes, the Bath Rugby Foundation empowers young people to unlock their
potential, build resilience, and create independent pathways for the future. The Foundation's work has a lasting
impact, and its continued dedication ensures that vulnerable children and young people have the tools they need to
thrive.

1. Team performance and Premiership league standing

Risk: Poor team performance can affect Bath Rugby’s league standing, which impacts matchday attendance,
sponsorship deals, and merchandise sales.

Mitigation:

Player Recruitment and Development: Bath Rugby invests in both domestic and international scouting to
recruit top talent. The club also has a robust youth academy to develop home-grown players.

Coaching and Support Staff: The club have recruited world-class coaching staff to optimize player
* performance. Continuous professional development for coaches helps the team adapt to evolving game
strategies.

Injury Prevention and management: A dedicated team consisting of Head of Medical Rory Murray, Head of
* Physical Performance Alex Martin and Head of Nutrition Sarah Jenner focuses on injury prevention, fitness, and
rehabilitation to ensure players remain healthy and at peak performance levels.

2. PRL Delivering on Their Growth Plans

Risk: Failure of the Premiership Rugby League (PRL) to meet its growth projections, impacting financial stability
through lower broadcasting and sponsorship revenues.
Mitigation:

Active Participation: Bath Rugby actively engages in PRL discussions and initiatives to ensure that the league’s
growth strategies align with the club's objectives.

Diversified Revenue Streams: The club works to diversify revenue sources, including sponsorship deals,

merchandise, nonmatchday events and experiences and international competitions, to reduce dependency on
PRL-specific income.

3. Compliance with Salary Cap and Wider PRL and RFU and Regulations

Risk: Non-compliance with salary cap regulations or other PRL and RFU rules could lead to sanctions, including fines
or point deductions.

Mitigation:

Internal Monitoring and Audits: Bath Rugby has robust procedures and processes in place in addition to a
dedicated team that approves recruitment decisions, contracts, and financial transactions relevant to the salary
cap to ensure adherence to the salary cap regulations. The internal team consists of Johann VanGraan (Head of
Rugby), Tarquin McDonald (CEO), the Clubs Head of Recruitment, Finance Director, and Financial Controller.
Regular internal audits are conducted to ensure the accuracy of the internal records.

Engagement with the Salary Cap Director: The club maintains open and honest communication with Andrew
Rogers, the Salary Cap Director, and is transparent in these communications.

Record Keeping: The club maintains detailed records for each player and for all the credits claimed under the
salary cap regulations. These are made available tqpBOeduring the annual salary cap audit.

Risk: Insufficient liquidity and funding could lead to cash flow problems, affecting the club’s ability to meet its financial

Regulatory Compliance: Bath Rugby is working closely with legal and planning experts to ensure that all
* aspects of the stadium’s design and construction comply with planning, environmental, and safety regulations,

6. Injury and Player Availability
Risk: Injuries to key players can impact team performance and require additional costs for rehabilitation and
replacements.
Mitigation:
Preventative Health Programs: Bath Rugby invests in an elite team of Medical, Physical Performance and

Depth of Squad: The club maintains a strong, deep squad, ensuring there are capable replacements for injured
players without drastically impacting team performance.

Mental Health and Wellbeing: |In addition to physical care, the club supports players’ mental health, which helps
them recover mentally and physically from injuries.

subsequently carried at amortised cost, with the difference between the proceeds, net of transaction costs, and the
amount due on redemption being recognised as a charge to the profit and loss account over the period of the relevant
borrowing.

Interest expense is recognised on the basis of the effective interest method and is included in interest payable and
similar charges.

Borrowings are classified as current liabilities unless the company has an unconditional right to defer settlement of the
liability for at least twelve months after the reporting date.

Leases

Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to the Statement of Profit and Loss on a straight line basis over the
lease term.

Benefits received and receivable as an incentive to sign an operating lease are recognised on a straight line basis
over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is representative of the time pattern of the lessee’s benefit from
the use of the leased asset.

Share capital

Ordinary shares are classified as equity. Equity instruments are measured at the fair value of the cash or other
resources received or receivable, net of the direct costs of issuing the equity instruments. If payment is deferred and
the time value of money is material, the initial measurement is on a present value basis.

Defined contribution pension obligation

A defined contribution plan is a pension plan under which fixed contributions are paid into a pension fund and the
company has no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions even if the fund does not hold sufficient
assets to pay all employees the benefits relating to employee service in the current and prior periods.

Contributions to defined contribution plans are recognised as employee benefit expense when they are due. If
contribution payments exceed the contribution due for service, the excess is recognised as a prepayment.
