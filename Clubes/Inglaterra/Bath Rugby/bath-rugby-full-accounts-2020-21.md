# Bath Rugby Limited

# Strategic Report for the Year Ended 30 June 2021

The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2021.

# Principal activity

The principal activity of the company is operation of a professional rugby union club.

# Fair review of the business

The financial and rugby years were dominated by the Covid-19 pandemic. The 2020/21 season was condensed into a 32-week season with a later than usual November start to accommodate the late finish of the interrupted 2019/20 season.

The majority of the Club's revenues are generated from matchdays, however, the season was largely played behind closed doors. Only two home games in the season were played in front of a socially distanced crowd, with crowd numbers restricted to less than 3,500 for each game.

The Club continued to provide digital products and events for supporters including a new flexible membership, and received generous support from season ticket holders, members, supporters and partners. The Club also continued to provide broader support in terms of digital teaching content to support parents home-schooling, the delivery of nutrition and cooking classes to the general public, the provision of virtual fitness classes, and the support of key workers through digital campaigns.

The 2020/21 Gallagher Premiership season was challenging and at times severely disrupted by Covid-19 whether that be through cancelled fixtures or test results - including materially incorrect test results - impacting the squad's preparation through isolation requirements. The staff and players were outstanding throughout the season in terms of managing those challenges. Overall it was a disappointing campaign, although an excellent bonus point win at the Rec in front of over 3,000 supporters on the final day of the season resulted in a 7th place finish and qualification for the Heineken Champions Cup.

The 2020/21 European campaign started with a narrow home defeat to Scarlets, followed by three cancelled games which meant the Club dropped into the Challenge Cup. The Club secured an away win against Italian side Zebre setting up a home quarter-final against Premiership rivals London Irish, which was won with a strong performance. The home semi-final against French giants Montpellier was hard fought, but ultimately won by the visitors meaning the Club narrowly missed out on the opportunity to play in the final and compete for silverware.

The year saw a positive EBITDA of £47,542 (2020: £2,748,676 EBITDA loss), resulting in a £2,796,218 decrease in EBITDA loss year on year. This does not accurately reflect the impact of the pandemic on the club's results as, within the EBITDA 'gain' is a fair value adjustment on the club's investment held in PRL of £6,664,853. Removing this results in an EBITDA loss for the year of £6,617,311, an increase in the EBITDA loss on last year of £3,868,635.

The increase in EBITDA loss is directly related to the playing of all but two games behind closed doors in the season, and the impact on Premiership Rugby distributions to the Club. With more than half of the Club's revenue generated from match income this had a substantial impact which was mitigated to some degree by cost-saving measures and the utilisation of government support where available during the Covid-19 pandemic. It is noted that the Club remains in the unique position of incurring operating costs in the region of c.£1 million per annum to erect a temporary stadium and ancillary facilities each season.

# Future Developments

Results in the first half of the 2021/22 season were consistently poor and, looking ahead to the 2022/23 season and beyond, the Club confirmed in December 2021 that it had secured Johann van Graan as Head Coach from the start of the 2022/23 season.

Johann is hugely experienced and will join after five years as Head Coach at Munster, with previous coaching experience at the Springboks and the Bulls. Johann brings with him JP Ferreira as Defence Coach, and they are joined by Joe Maddock as Attack Coach. The Club also announced Neal Hatley and Luke Charteris continuing as Forwards Coach and Line-Out Coach respectively.

The Club's continued investment in the Academy and Player Pathway has resulted in breakthrough seasons for a number of players including Orlando Bailey, Max Ojomoh and Ewan Richards whilst the likes of Miles Reid and Josh Bayliss have fully established themselves in the senior team.

Despite the results in 2021/22, support for the Club has been outstanding with most home games having been sold out and an incredible atmosphere at the Rec.

Financially, the strong support of the fans at the Stadium has been partially offset by the impact of two cancelled games in the season as a result of Covid-19.

The Club separated the roles of Chairman and Owner with effect from 1st January 2022. Bruce Craig continues to provide full financial and emotional support for the Club as its sole owner and shareholder. Edward Griffiths has been appointed as Chairman, bringing expertise and experience across rugby and commercial operations.

The Club remains committed to redevelopment and the creation of a permanent home in the heart of Bath, not only for the Club but also for the positive contribution this can make to the City and its economy, and the wider community.

In December 2021 it was confirmed that the Club, together with its landlord Bath Recreation Limited, had won their appeal of a first instance judgement in respect of a covenant in relation to development of the Recreation Ground. The Club are now waiting for one further legal window to close, and look forward to bringing forward design proposals in due course.

# Principal risks and uncertainties

The principal risks and uncertainties facing the Club are considered to be:

- Competition from other leisure activities impacting on attendance figures and matchday revenues;
- Suboptimal rugby performance impacting on matchday and sponsorship revenues;
Recruitment and retention of playing and support staff across the Club;
- Playing and coaching staff wage inflation;
- The maintenance of the salary cap framework at a level that allows well-managed Clubs to invest without undermining their financial viability;
- The ability for Premiership Rugby and the RFU to grow revenue distributions to the Premiership Rugby shareholder Clubs over the short, medium and long term; and
The impact of the Covid 19 pandemic on the Club.

The impact of Brexit on the Club continues to be uncertain.

However, given that nearly 100% of turnover is generated in the UK and only a very small proportion of the Club's supply chain is based within the EU, the Directors do not consider the uncertainty over Brexit to be a material risk to the Club.

The directors remain confident about the Club's future.

After making enquiries, the directors have reason to expect that the company has adequate resources to continue operations for the foreseeable future. For this reason, they continue to adopt the going concern basis in preparing the accounts.

Where they relate to timing differences in respect of interests in subsidiaries, associates, branches and joint ventures and the Company can control the reversal of the timing differences and such reversal is not considered probable in the foreseeable future.

Deferred tax balances are not recognised in respect of permanent differences except in respect of business combinations, when deferred tax is recognised on the differences between the fair values of assets acquired and the future tax deductions available for them and the differences between the fair values of liabilities acquired and the amount that will be assessed for tax. Deferred tax is determined using tax rates and laws that have been enacted or substantively enacted by the reporting date.

# Trade debtors

Trade debtors are amounts due from customers for merchandise sold or services performed in the ordinary course of business.

Trade debtors are recognised initially at the transaction price. They are subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method, less provision for impairment. A provision for the impairment of trade debtors is established when there is objective evidence that the company will not be able to collect all amounts due according to the original terms of the receivables.

# Cash and cash equivalents

Cash is represented by cash in hand and deposits with financial institutions repayable without penalty on notice of not more than 24 hours. Cash equivalents are highly liquid investments that mature in no more than three months from the date of acquisition and that are readily convertible to known amounts of cash with insignificant risk of change in value.

In the Statement of Cash Flows, cash and cash equivalents are shown net of bank overdrafts that are repayable on demand and form an integral part of the Company's cash management.

# Trade creditors

Trade creditors are obligations to pay for goods or services that have been acquired in the ordinary course of business from suppliers. Accounts payable are classified as current liabilities if the company does not have an unconditional right, at the end of the reporting period, to defer settlement of the creditor for at least twelve months after the reporting date. If there is an unconditional right to defer settlement for at least twelve months after the reporting date, they are presented as non-current liabilities.

Trade creditors are recognised initially at the transaction price and subsequently measured at amortised cost using the effective interest method.

# Borrowings

Interest-bearing borrowings are initially recorded at fair value, net of transaction costs. Interest-bearing borrowings are subsequently carried at amortised cost, with the difference between the proceeds, net of transaction costs, and the amount due on redemption being recognised as a charge to the Profit and Loss Account over the period of the relevant borrowing.

Interest expense is recognised on the basis of the effective interest method and is included in interest payable and similar charges.

Borrowings are classified as current liabilities unless the company has an unconditional right to defer settlement of the liability for at least twelve months after the reporting date.

# Leases

Rentals paid under operating leases are charged to the Statement of Comprehensive Income on a straight line basis over the lease term.

Benefits received and receivable as an incentive to sign an operating lease are recognised on a straight line basis over the lease term, unless another systematic basis is representative of the time pattern of the lessee's benefit from the use of the leased asset.

# Defined contribution pension obligation

A defined contribution plan is a pension plan under which fixed contributions are paid into a pension fund and the company has no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions even if the fund does not hold sufficient assets to pay all employees the benefits relating to employee service in the current and prior periods.
