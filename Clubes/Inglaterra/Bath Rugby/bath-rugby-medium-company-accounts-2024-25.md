The directors present their strategic report for the year ended 30 June 2025.

Principal activity
The principal activity of the Club is the operation of a professional rugby union club.

Bath Rugby's goal is to deliver sustained success on the pitch, to drive growth in the fanbase and wider rugby
participation, and to ensure the long-term financial sustainability of the club, whilst making a significant positive impact on
the local and wider community.

With a strong focus on growing the Bath Rugby brand, the club is committed to building a deep sense of pride and
belonging among all its stakeholders: staff, players, supporters, local clubs, schools, corporate partners, suppliers, and
the wider community.

An historic milestone was achieved in September 2025 when Bath & North East Somerset Council's Planning Committee
voted overwhelmingly in favour of the club's planning application for a new stadium at The Rec, with the Secretary of
State subsequently reviewing the decision before confirming the application would not be called in. The new 18,000 seat
stadium will create world-class facilities, regenerate the riverside in Bath, elevate the fan experience and strengthen the
club's connection to the city, whilst also enabling the long-term financial sustainability of the club.

The club's vision is to be a powerful force for good in Bath, driving positive change and inspiring future generations. By
pursuing success on the pitch and making a profound, lasting impact in the community, Bath Rugby aims to be a source
of pride for the city, focussed on building excellence both on and off the field.

REVIEW OF BUSINESS

Rugby review

The 2024/25 season will be remembered as one of the greatest in Bath Rugby's history. The club achieved an
extraordinary treble, winning the Gallagher Premiership, the European Challenge Cup, and the Premiership Cup, a feat
never achieved by any club and one that underlines the remarkable progress made under the leadership of Head of
Rugby Johann van Graan, his coaches and staff.

Building on the foundations laid in the 2023/24 season, which saw the club reach the Premiership Final, the squad
entered the campaign with renewed belief and hunger. Key additions to the squad, combined with the return of players
from injury and the continued development of homegrown talent through the academy, provided the club with a deep and
competitive squad capable of challenging on multiple fronts.

The season began with a statement of intent, with the club recording a series of impressive victories in the early rounds
of the Premiership. A landmark 68-10 home victory over Saracens in December 2024 - inflicting on them their worst
defeat in Premiership history - demonstrated the squad's attacking potency and depth. The club went into the Six Nations
break at the top of the Gallagher Premiership table with a commanding six-point lead over the second-placed team.

During the Six Nations window the club’s Premiership Cup squad, led by captain Ewan Richards, delivered a series of
impressive pool game wins achieving a home quarter-final draw against Harlequins at the Rec, which thy won 39-28. The
matchday squads were selected in the spirit of the competition, giving many of Bath's emerging academy players the
opportunity to shine and show their potential. Through the course of the competition a total of 49 players were selected.

The team played an experienced Newcastle side away in the semi-final, winning 41-29 to set up a final against Exeter
Chiefs at Sandy Park which they won 48-14 with a dominant display securing the club's first domestic trophy in 29 years.
Importantly the competition marked the breakthrough of a number of academy players including Ciaran Donoghue and
Arthur Green.

In Europe, after a couple of narrow defeats in the Champions Cup pool stages the Club qualified for the Challenge Cup,
progressing through the knockout stages with dominant victories over Pau, Gloucester, and Edinburgh before defeating
Lyon 37-12 in the final to lift the trophy for the second time in the club's history.

The team continued to deliver consistent results in the Premiership. In April 2025, a comprehensive 55-19 victory over
Newcastle Falcons secured top spot in the regular season for the first time since 2003/04, confirming Bath's position as
the dominant force in English rugby. A thrilling 34-20 semi-final victory over Bristol Bears at The Rec, played in front of a
passionate sell-out crowd, set up a Premiership Final for the second successive year.

At Twickenham, Bath defeated Leicester Tigers 23-21 in a tense and hard-fought final to claim the club's first Premiership
title in 29 years - a momentous achievement which, together with the Premiership Cup and European Challenge Cup
successes, was celebrated by thousands of supporters who lined the streets of Bath on Sunday 15th June the day after
the Premiership final for a victory parade over Pulteney Bridge, up Milsom Street and back to The Rec.

This historic treble success places the 2024/25 Bath squad among the finest in the club's distinguished history.

The successes of the team are underpinned by a thriving academy, with the U18s Academy team reaching the Prem
U18s final for the second year running. The pathway from academy to first team remains a core pillar of the club's rugby
strategy, ensuring a pipeline of talent for years to come. The club's investment in securing the long-term contracts of
Johann van Graan (to June 2030) and key coaching, support and playing staff continues to provide stability and
continuity.

heartland, across England and internationally.

Balance sheet and Funding

During the pandemic the club secured a loan totalling £5.3 million from The Department for Media Sport and Culture
(DCMS). The loan carries an interest rate of 2% and included a repayment holiday until September 2023. The club has
consistently made the twice-yearly repayments when they fall due, with the carrying amount reduced to £4.0m at the
year-end (2024: £4.6m). The next payment falls due in March 2026.

During the year, the club's owner, Bruce Craig, increased his loan to the club by £2.2m (2023/24: £5.9m), bringing the
undiscounted balance to £32.3m. The loan is interest free and repayable upon 12 months’ notice. The loan is adjusted for
net present value in the accounts, with a discount of £4.4m applied, giving a carrying amount of £28.0m. The reduced
level of additional funding required from the shareholder reflects the improved financial performance of the club during
the year.

The Club continues to hold its investment in PRL at a value of £20.5m. Management has tested a range of possible
valuations and has concluded that the midpoint of these is the appropriate value to hold.

Bath Rugby's wider impact

A new Stadium:

In September 2025, Bath & North East Somerset Council's Planning Committee voted eight to one in favour of the club's
planning application for a new stadium at The Rec. The Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local
Government subsequently confirmed that the application would not be called in for a central government decision,
marking a historic milestone in the club's long journey to redevelop its home.

The approved plans feature a state of the art 18,000 seat stadium. The scheme will regenerate and rejuvenate the
riverside in Bath whilst delivering a new West grandstand, new East and North stands, together with the refurbishment
and extension of the existing South Stand. The design includes a curved roof that blends with the city’s heritage, ensuring
it sits sensitively within Bath's World Heritage setting while revitalising the riverside area of the city.

The strength of public support for the project was evident, with 5,086 individuals writing to the council in favour of the
plans compared to 368 objections. No statutory consultees raised an objection, including Historic England, Natural
England, Sport England, the Environment Agency, and highways authorities, nor did UNESCO regarding World Heritage.
Historic England specifically welcomed the replacement of the existing ground, describing the latest plans as a
successful outcome of many years of negotiation.

The club's ambition remains to continue playing at The Rec through the construction programme, with a target of
commencing work in 2027. The new stadium will secure Bath Rugby's place in the heart of the city and will generate
long-term incremental revenue for the club whilst continuing to contribute significantly to the economy of Bath.

PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES
1. Team performance and Premiership league standing

Risk: Poor team performance can affect Bath Rugby's league standing, which impacts matchday attendance, spons
orship deals, and merchandise sales.

Mitigation:

- Player Recruitment and Development: Bath Rugby invests in both domestic and international scouting to recruit top
talent. The club also has a robust youth academy to develop home-grown players.

- Coaching and Support Staff: The club have recruited world-class coaching staff to optimize player performance.
Continuous professional development for coaches helps the team adapt to evolving game strategies.

- Injury Prevention and Management: A dedicated medical, physical performance and nutrition team focuses on injury
prevention, fitness, and rehabilitation to ensure players remain healthy and at peak performance levels.

2. PRL Delivering on Their Growth Plans

Risk: Failure of Premiership Rugby Limited (PRL) to meet its growth projections, impacting financial stability through
lower broadcasting and sponsorship revenues.

Mitigation:

- Active Participation: Bath Rugby actively engages in PRL discussions and initiatives to ensure that the league's
growth strategies align with the club's objectives.

- Diversified Revenue Streams: The club works to diversify revenue sources, including sponsorship deals,
merchandise, non-matchday events and experiences and hosting international match events, to reduce dependency on
PRL-specific income.

3. Compliance with Salary Cap and Wider PRL and RFU Regulations

Risk: Non-compliance with salary cap regulations or other PRL and RFU rules could lead to sanctions, including fines or
point deductions.

Mitigation:

- Internal Monitoring and Audits: Bath Rugby has robust procedures and processes in place in addition to a dedicated
team that approves recruitment decisions, contracts, and financial transactions relevant to the salary cap to ensure
adherence to the salary cap regulations. Regular internal audits are conducted to ensure the accuracy of internal records.
- Engagement with the Salary Cap Director: The club maintains open and honest communication with the PRL Salary
Cap Director, and is transparent in these communications.

- Record Keeping: The club maintains detailed records for each player and for all the credits claimed under the salary
cap regulations. These are made available to BDO during the annual salary cap audit.

4. Liquidity and Funding

Risk: Insufficient liquidity and funding could lead to cash flow problems, affecting the club's ability to meet its financial
obligations.

Mitigation:

- Strategic Financial Planning: Bath Rugby has a long-term financial plan to manage its revenue and expenditure. The
club ensures that funds from key stakeholders are available for operational needs, player salaries, and facility maintena
nce.

- Investment Diversification: The club seeks external investment and partnerships, diversifying its funding sources
beyond matchday revenues, including sponsorship deals, non-matchday events and experiences, and merchandise.

- Shareholder Support: Bath Rugby has continued and committed support from its sole shareholder and owner. The
cashflow requirements of the club are presented at the Board meeting before the start of the season with any challenges
arising within the season being highlighted in advance.

5. New Stadium Build

Risk: Although planning permission has now been granted, there remain risks associated with the detailed design,
funding and construction phases of the new stadium project. Delays or cost overruns could strain the club's finances and

- Stakeholder Engagement: The club maintains strong relationships with local stakeholders, including the council,

6. Injury and Player Availability

Risk: Injuries to key players can impact team performance and require additional costs for rehabilitation and repla
cements.

- Preventative Health Programs: Bath Rugby invests in an elite team of Medical, Physical Performance and Nutrition

- Depth of Squad: The club maintains a strong, deep squad, ensuring there are capable replacements for injured players

- Mental Health and Wellbeing: In addition to physical care, the club supports players' mental health, which helps them

7. Fan Engagement and Attendance
Risk: Decline in fan interest or attendance could impact matchday revenues, sponsorships, and overall club profitability.

Mitigation:

- Fan-Centric Initiatives: Bath Rugby consistently engages with its fanbase through social media, events, and special

supporter views help inform the club's strategy.

- Improved Matchday Experience: The club continually enhances the matchday experience by improving stadium
facilities, fan services, and community engagement programs. The new stadium will provide a significant step-change in
matchday experience.

- Loyalty and Membership Programs: The club has fan loyalty schemes and season ticket packages to encourage
repeat attendance and strengthen the fanbase.

8. Regulatory and Governance Changes

Risk: Changes in rugby governance, regulations, or league structures could affect the club’s operations and long-term
planning.

Mitigation:

- Continuous Monitoring of Regulatory Landscape: Bath Rugby actively monitors changes in regulations and
governance, ensuring compliance with all new rules and requirements.

- Engagement in Governance Bodies: The club is actively involved in discussions within PRL and RFU governance to
ensure it has a voice in any regulatory changes.

These mitigation strategies help Bath Rugby navigate the risks associated with operating at a high level in the
competitive and regulated world of professional rugby.

Going Concern

The directors have reviewed Bath Rugby'’s financial position and working capital requirements for the period from July
2025, focusing on the potential risks and opportunities that will impact the Club's ability to continue as a going concern.
This review includes the ongoing operating costs, planned investment in people, and other capital expenditure, as well as
the support committed by the Club's owner. The directors have also considered the existing financial arrangements,
including the loan from DCMS, and the external economic factors expected to influence the Club's operations.

In assessing the going concern assumption, the directors have considered the following factors:

- Global Instability and Trade Risks: Global trade tensions, particularly between the US and other countries, including
potential changes in tariff structures, may increase the cost of imported goods and services. Although Bath Rugby is not
directly involved in international trade, this is likely to impact the UK economy. The club, being in the leisure and e
ntertainment business, is more vulnerable to fluctuations in the disposable income of individuals. The vulnerability to
these fluctuations is further impacted by Bath Rugby’'s high-cost base. Geopolitical instability, including the ongoing
situations in Ukraine and other global conflicts, also contributes to economic uncertainty. The directors are actively
monitoring these risks and are taking steps to mitigate their impact on the Club's operations.

- Funding and Liquidity Needs: The Club will require additional funding from its shareholder to address liquidity
demands throughout the coming year. The directors are confident in the ongoing commitment of the shareholder to
provide the necessary financial support, ensuring the Club's liquidity is maintained and its financial obligations can be m
et.

- Business Plans and Cash Flow Forecasts: The directors have prepared detailed business plans and cash flow
forecasts extending into 2026 and beyond, incorporating potential downside scenarios, including continued economic
stagnation or a prolonged reduction in consumer spending. These forecasts account for the Club's operational costs and
include mitigating actions, such as cost containment measures, revenue diversification strategies, and capital planning,
ensuring that the Club can continue operating without significant financial strain.

Based on this review, the directors are confident that the Club has the necessary support and strategies in place to
continue as a going concern. The financial statements for the period ending June 2025 have been prepared on a going
concern basis, with ongoing monitoring of economic conditions and business performance.

Turnover from the sale of goods is recognised when all of the following conditions are satisfied:

- the Company has transferred the significant risks and rewards of the ownership to the buyer;

- the Company retains neither continuing managerial involvement to the degree usually associated wit ownership

- the amount of revenue can be measured reliably;

- itis probable that the Company will receive the consideration due under the transaction; and

Turnover from a contract to provide services is recognised in the period in which the services are provided in

Financial instruments

impairment. If an asset is impaired the impairment loss is the difference between carrying amount and present
vale of estimated cash flows discounted at the asset's original effective interest rate, this loss is recognised in the
Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Other financial assets, including investments, are initially measured at fair value and revalued at each reporting
date to fair value, with the movement recognised in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Financial assets are derecognised when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are
settled or substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership of the asset are transferred to another party.

Current tax is recognised at the amount of tax payable using the tax rates and laws that have been enacted or

Deferred tax
Deferred tax is recognised in respect of all timing differences that have originated but not reversed at the balance
sheet date.

Timing differences arise from the inclusion of income and expenses in tax assessments in periods different from
those in which they are recognised in financial statements. Deferred tax is measured using tax rates and laws that
have been enacted or substantively enacted by the year end and that are expected to apply to the reversal of the
timing difference.

Unrelieved tax losses and other deferred tax assets are recognised only to the extent that it is probable that they
will be recovered against the reversal of deferred tax liabilities or other future taxable profits.

Defined contribution pension obligation

A defined contribution plan is a pension plan under which fixed contributions are paid into a pension fund and the
company has no legal or constructive obligation to pay further contributions even if the fund does not hold
sufficient assets to pay all employees the benefits relating to employee service in the current and prior periods.

Contributions to defined contribution plans are recognised as employee benefit expense when they are due. If
contribution payments exceed the contribution due for service, the excess is recognised as a prepayment.
