150th Anniversary ForewordThe 2025/26 Season is a special and important season in the Club’s history given the Club has reached the significant milestone of 150 years. It is a testament to all those who have supported the Club, played for the Club and served the Club that we have reached this milestone with a strong sense of momentum following a hugely successful 2024/25 season. Although ultimately we did not progress from the UEFA Conference League qualification rounds at the start of the 2025/26 season, we faced high-quality opposition and acquitted ourselves well. This is a significant moment for the Club and a milestone in which we all take pride and celebrate together.The Directors present their Strategic Review along with the Directors’ Report and audited financial statements for the year ended 30 June 2025.The Club is a leading professional football club in Scotland. Established in 1875, the Club owns the Easter Road Stadium which is its registered home ground for senior competitions. The Club also has its own training centre near Ormiston, in East Lothian. The Club is a full member of the Scottish FA and the Scottish Professional Football League Limited.

FOR THE YEAR ENDED 30 JUNE 2025
The 2024/25 season can only be described as a season of two halves. After enduring a challenging start to the domestic league campaign, the home fixture against Aberdeen FC on a dark Tuesday in November marked the significant turning point for the Men’s first team. Going into this fixture, the team were in 12th place and a significant number of points from the closest placed team. After being behind at the 90 minute mark 1-2, the following 6 minutes of extra time were the most dramatic and impactful of the whole season. A magnificent Nicky Cadden goal from a free kick drew things level just before Aberdeen FC went ahead again at 90+5 minutes. Rocky Bushiri equalised in the dying seconds of the game to level things at 3-3. From this pivotal moment, the team - led by David Gray in his first full season as Head Coach -advanced up the table with unstoppable progress. The unbeaten run of 17 games matched the post- war record set in 1948 by the team that included Gordon Smith, Lawrie Reilly, Eddie Turnbull and Willie Ormond – four fifths of the Famous Five. The men’s team finished the 2024/25 season in 3rd place, earning David Gray three Scottish Premiership Manager of the Month awards. Importantly, this also earned the team entry into the UEFA Europa League Qualifying rounds. In the League Cup campaign, Hibs were handed a challenging draw against Celtic FC, at Celtic Park, in the second round. Celtic won this 3-1 and went on to win the League Cup, and Hibs were eliminated. In the Scottish Cup campaign, Hibs progressed to the quarter finals where they were again drawn against Celtic at Celtic Park. Celtic won this 2-0, eliminating Hibs. Celtic also progressed to the final in the Scottish Cup.The 2024/25 was a hugely successful season for the Women’s team too. The team was in 1st place in the SWPL going into the final round of games but, due to the top three clubs being close on points, all three clubs including Hibs were in a position to be crowned champions. This was the third successive season in which the SWPL winner was confirmed on the last match day, adding to the excitement of the league. On the day, Grant Scott’s Hibs team won 1-0 against Rangers at Ibrox to secure Hibs’ first SWPL title since 2006/07 and sealing entry into the UEFA Women’s Champions League.The Women’s team also reached the SWPL Cup Final but were, unfortunately, defeated by Rangers. In the Scottish Cup, Hibs Women exited in the Semi-final round following a defeat to Glasgow City.In addition to the successful seasons for both the Men and the Women, Hibs Under-18 team won the Club Academy Scotland Elite League title. In doing so they also qualified for the Champions Pathway of the UEFA Youth League.Off the pitch, the stadium improvement work, that was commenced in May 2024, was completed prior to the start of the 2024/25 season ensuring the Stadium was ready for the first home William Hill Scottish Premier League game of the season. This significant capital investment work, predominantly to the North Stand, therefore straddled two financial years. This work included significant upgrades to the hospitality areas with the establishments of the “Behind the Goals” suite, “The Terrace Experience” and the “Famous Five” suite, as well as giving the Hibernian Community Foundation a purpose-built space in furtherance of their community objectives. The Behind the Goals suite, which is open every match day, has provided an excellent hospitality experience for fans and has proved to be hugely popular. The re-purposed spaces also enhanced the experience of our youngest supporters.In addition, the match day experience has been enhanced by the capital investment in the creation of the 1,238 safe standing rail seating spaces in the lower tier of the North Stand and the relocation of some fans to this area. The club has also installed additional dedicated viewing platforms for supporters with disabled or accessibility seating requirements, to ensure that Hibs remains a community club accessible to all. Overall, the Club invested more than £3m in upgrading the stadium infrastructure.Towards the end of the 2024/25 season, and with the potential for qualification for the League phase of a UEFA competition becoming a real possibility, the Club also had to take the decision to make some specific upgrades to the stadium facilities which would ensure compliance with UEFA regulations. These included an upgrade to the PA system and specific works to the technical areas to meet the February 2025 UEFA infrastructure requirements, such as: (a) new dug outs with enough space to allow seats for all technical staff including all medical staff; (b) upgrades to dressing rooms; (c) increasing the size of the referees’ room; (d) provision of additional media facilities in the tunnel and main stand; and (e) compliant medical and doping control areas.

Key performance indicators
Financially, turnover increased to £16.8m, an increase of 6%. The 2023/24 season included additional revenue attributable to our participation in the UEFA Conference League qualification - boosting earnings predominantly across the prize money, ticketing, and hospitality income streams. This was not, of course, directly replicated in 2024/25. However, the run of form in the second half of the season, resulting in a 3rd place finish significantly increased SPFL prize money from 2023/24. The excitement around the Men’s first team performance also ensured that the match-by-match ticketing sales were extremely strong in the second part of the season. Both Cup matches at Celtic Park also provided a ticketing boost. Hospitality sales increased from 2023/24 as a direct result of the feel-good factor surrounding the Club. Even though the Club did not benefit from any European Prize money, the Club benefitted from an increase in the UEFA Solidarity distributions which are payable to SPFL Clubs that do not participate in European Group stage football. UEFA Solidarity is calculated with reference to the National team co-efficient and the distribution in the 2024/25 season was the highest to date. The Club also benefitted from strong support from its majority shareholder, the Gordon Family, who again donated a significant figure during the season to strategically support Player Recruitment and David Gray in his first full season in charge. Additionally, the Gordon family -along with other major benefactors - donated additional funds to support the strategic goals of the Women’s Team.Operating costs during the season remained high, although a slight decrease was achieved. Overall employee costs very marginally decreased, noting also that the Club remains committed to being a living wage employer. Match day costs increased slightly due to supplier increases, with the changes to the National Minimum wage and Employers National Insurance starting to take effect in supplier contracts. A major area of expenditure continues to be rates and utilities and every effort is taken to ensure the best tariffs are achieved for energy. Given global uncertainty, it is likely that these costs will continue to remain significant. Due to continuing investment in the Stadium, depreciation has increased slightly by £93k per annum, however, amortization has reduced by £97k with a few players leaving the Club during the season or having no remaining book value.The financial gains from Player Trading during the year were predominantly derived from the transfers of Dylan Vente, Ewan Henderson and Academy Player Josh Landers. The release of the book value of some players who left the Club for no value have reduced this figure.The Club had cash balances £3.3m, up from £2.9m at 30 June 2024. Net assets at 30 June 2025 were £27.7m, a slight decrease from £28.7m. This decrease recognises the losses but also the on-going financial investments made by the Gordon Family, Black Knight Football Club UK Limited, Hibernian Supporters Limited and Leslie Robb.It is imperative to the Board of Directors that the Club sets high standards both on and off the pitch, with an expectation that the Club positively impacts all stakeholders. Financially, the current climate is very challenging for football clubs of a similar size to Hibs. This season has been one where key strategic decisions have been taken to position the Club for the future. Of crucial importance is the recruitment strategy - and this has been a key focus for management this season. Balancing spend and ongoing financial commitments with the needs of squad performance and future player value has to be the aim of any successful football Club and one that has to be carefully managed. We believe that we have taken important steps in this area. These steps should prove to be crucial in the short and medium term of the Club for both performance and financial reasons which are intrinsically linked. Many players left the Club in the 2024/25 season and we aim to protect investment in players where possible through development loans where game time can be guaranteed. Management will continue to monitor expenditure of key areas such as staff and will drive major contracts forward ensuring revenue is maximised and costs are controlled. As a Club we are fortunate to have supportive shareholders and planned loans made by the Gordon family to support the cashflow of the Club throughout the season were converted to equity prior to the year-end. In addition, the three other main shareholders participated in the equity issue. It is to the immense credit of all shareholders that they have remained committed to continue to financially support the Club in this way to ensure the Club remains free of any significant shareholder loans and whilst the profit and loss account reflects further annual losses, the balance sheet is strong and the future of the club is stable. The Board is very much aware that the general cost of living has risen in recent years and has strived to maintain ticket prices at an accessible level. Given this season is a massive milestone in the Club’s history, being its 150th year, and coming from a very successful 2023/24, the aim of the Board was to ensure a regularly full stadium and encourage and support the Club’s fan base as much as possible by ensuring general access Season Ticket prices were not increased. The Club continues to offer flexible ways to purchase season tickets and tries to operate in an open and transparent manner with supporters. This Season has also seen the introduction of the Fan Forum, and the Club continues to maintain and enhance relations with its supporters wherever possible.

| 1.6 | Impairment of fixed assets |

At each reporting period end date, the company reviews the carrying amounts of its tangible and intangible assets to determine whether there is any indication that those assets have suffered an impairment loss. If any such indication exists, the recoverable amount of the asset is estimated in order to determine the extent of the impairment loss (if any). Where it is not possible to estimate the recoverable amount of an individual asset, the company estimates the recoverable amount of the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.
Recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use. In assessing value in use, the estimated future cash flows are discounted to their present value using a pre-tax discount rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the asset for which the estimates of future cash flows have not been adjusted. If the recoverable amount of an asset (or cash-generating unit) is estimated to be less than its carrying amount, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is reduced to its recoverable amount. An impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation decrease.
Recognised impairment losses are reversed if, and only if, the reasons for the impairment loss have ceased to apply. Where an impairment loss subsequently reverses, the carrying amount of the asset (or cash-generating unit) is increased to the revised estimate of its recoverable amount, but so that the increased carrying amount does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined had no impairment loss been recognised for the asset (or cash-generating unit) in prior years. A reversal of an impairment loss is recognised immediately in profit or loss, unless the relevant asset is carried at a revalued amount, in which case the reversal of the impairment loss is treated as a revaluation increase.

| 1.8 | Financial instruments |  |

The company has elected to apply the provisions of Section 11 ‘Basic Financial Instruments’ and Section 12 ‘Other Financial Instruments Issues’ of FRS 102 to all of its financial instruments. Financial instruments are recognised in the company's balance sheet when the company becomes party to the contractual provisions of the instrument. Financial assets and liabilities are offset, with the net amounts presented in the financial statements, when there is a legally enforceable right to set off the recognised amounts and there is an intention to settle on a net basis or to realise the asset and settle the liability simultaneously.
Basic financial assets
Basic financial assets, which include debtors and cash and bank balances, are initially measured at transaction price including transaction costs and are subsequently carried at amortised cost using the effective interest method unless the arrangement constitutes a financing transaction, where the transaction is measured at the present value of the future receipts discounted at a market rate of interest. Financial assets classified as receivable within one year are not amortised.
Other financial assets
Other financial assets, including investments in equity instruments which are not subsidiaries, associates or joint ventures, are initially measured at fair value, which is normally the transaction price. Such assets are subsequently carried at fair value and the changes in fair value are recognised in profit or loss, except that investments in equity instruments that are not publicly traded and whose fair values cannot be measured reliably are measured at cost less impairment.
Impairment of financial assets
Financial assets, other than those held at fair value through profit and loss, are assessed for indicators of impairment at each reporting end date. Financial assets are impaired where there is objective evidence that, as a result of one or more events that occurred after the initial recognition of the financial asset, the estimated future cash flows have been affected. If an asset is impaired, the impairment loss is the difference between the carrying amount and the present value of the estimated cash flows discounted at the asset’s original effective interest rate. The impairment loss is recognised in profit or loss. If there is a decrease in the impairment loss arising from an event occurring after the impairment was recognised, the impairment is reversed. The reversal is such that the current carrying amount does not exceed what the carrying amount would have been, had the impairment not previously been recognised. The impairment reversal is recognised in profit or loss.
Derecognition of financial assets
Financial assets are derecognised only when the contractual rights to the cash flows from the asset expire or are settled, or when the company transfers the financial asset and substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to another entity, or if some significant risks and rewards of ownership are retained but control of the asset has transferred to another party that is able to sell the asset in its entirety to an unrelated third party.
Classification of financial liabilities
Financial liabilities and equity instruments are classified according to the substance of the contractual arrangements entered into. An equity instrument is any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting all of its liabilities.
