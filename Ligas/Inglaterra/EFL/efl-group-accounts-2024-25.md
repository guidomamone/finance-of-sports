Review of the business
: The EFL (English Football League) is the world's original league football competition and the largest single body
of professional Clubs in European football. A vital part of the UK’s sporting life, it operates some of the world’s
most respected, popular and successful football competitions, whilst positively impacting communities across
England and Wales. :
Responsible for administering and regulating the Sky Bet EFL, Carabao Cup and Vertu Trophy, as well as
reserve and youth football, the EFL is home to 72 Clubs from England and Wales across three divisions. With
1,891 matches every season across EFL competition, it is one of the highest attended League bodies in Europe
and attracts millions of supporters both domestically and across the globe.
We were again extremely proud that more than 23 million fans attended games across EFL competitions in
2024/25. More than 12.1 million people attended a Sky Bet Championship game this season, making it the
second best attended division in Europe, ahead of the Bundesliga, La Liga and Serie A. Sky Bet League One
recorded cumulative attendances of 5.6m and Sky Bet League Two's cumulative attendances of 3.4m make it .
the best-attended fourth tier in Europe. Cumulative attendances across our 2025 EFL Play-off matches of
431,818 were the second highest ever. Attendances across the Carabao Cup and Vertu Trophy were also on the
up, with cumulative Carabao Cup attendances of 1.8m being 19% up on 2023/24 and Vertu Trophy cumulative
attendances of 332,000 being 13% up on 2023/24.
As part of its commercial operation, the EFL sells and manages commercial rights on behalf of Clubs.
Sky Sports continues to be the EFL’s main domestic broadcast partner with 2024/25 being the first season of the
new ground-breaking agreement. The new rights agreement between the EFL and Sky Sports saw over 1,000
EFL fixtures aired live via Sky and NOW, with the update making Sky Sports the home of the EFL in a five-year
deal. A new platform, Sky Sports+, was also introduced, having the capacity to show up to 100 live events via
concurrent streams. Worth £935m, the landmark deal for EFL Clubs established the League as a ‘premium
partner’ with the world-renowned broadcaster.
, Production values and camera numbers were enhanced as Sky Sports and the EFL, alongside its production
partner IMG, invested significantly in production infrastructure to enhance the viewing experience.
Sky Sports and ITV also reached an agreement to give all UK football fans the chance to watch free-to-air
coverage of select Carabao Cup and Championship fixtures.
In relation to overseas broadcasting, 2024/25 was the first year of the EFL’s latest overseas broadcasting cycle.
For the first time, a broadcasting agreement was secured with Relevent Sport LLC separately covering the
distribution through North, Central and South America. As part of this, Relevent secured a multi-year, multi-
platform deal with CBS Sports to offer the widest distribution of EFL matches ever in the United States, with over
400 EFL matches being aired throughout the Season. A record deal for the rights to broadcast to the rest of the
world was also agreed with Pitch International up to the end of 2027/28, enhancing a long-standing relationship
that has been in place for 15 years.

season, up £6.9m on the £27.6m achieved in 2023/24,

Financial review
Profit and Loss Account . :
Group turnover for the year is £282.0m (2024: £207.5m), £74.5m (35.9%) higher than the previous period,
substantially driven by the increased domestic broadcast deal with Sky Sports, in addition to increased overseas
Broadcast income from the new deals with Relevent covering the Americas and Pitch International covering the
rest of the world. There was also an increase in Sponsorship revenue, specifically due to uplifted deals with Sky
Bet and EA Sports. This increased revenue was offset-by a reduction in revenue from the EFL Digital streaming
product, iFollow, due to loss of domestic match pass sales and reduced overseas streaming subscribers. Other
operating income decreased by £0.6m in the year to £0.9m with the costs and interest incurred on the MetLife
loan no longer being recharged to the Premier League and recognised as income. .
Overall, the Group's total costs including payments made to member Clubs increased by £74.5m (35.3%) to
£285.3m in 2024 (2024: £210.8m). Distributions payable to member Clubs of the EFL (excluding iFollow) have
increased by £43.0m to £219.1m mainly reflecting the increases to EFL Basic Award. Gate levies received by
Clubs, which are netted off against external charges, increased by £0.7m to £12.1m (2024: £11.4m), driven by
the increase in average attendances across both the League's Play offs and Carabao Cup in the 2024/25
season.
" The Group continued to recognise loans to member Clubs, which were provided in prior financial periods. Under
. Section 11 of FRS 102, these loans have been discounted using a market rate of interest. The effective interest
will unwind over the loan period, with £0.2m (2024: £0.5m) recognised as interest receivable in the current year.
At the year end, £2.2m (2024: £4.8m) of discounted interest free loans to member clubs remained as
outstanding. .
The Group’s profit for the year of £2.9m (2024: £3.1m) was driven by underlying pre tax profit of £1.4m (2024:
£2.7m) plus non-underlying pre tax profit of £2.3m (2024: £1.4m), all net off by tax of £0.8m (2024: £1.0m).
Underlying pre tax profits of £1.4m are driven by the £1m pension scheme contribution and £0.3m of profit from
associated companies which are excluded from the Company’s results prior to making final distributions to
member Clubs. Non-underlying pre tax profits of £2.3m are driven by a £2.3m gain on the derivative financial
instrument and interest receivable in the year due to discounting in 2021 of £0.2m (2024: £0.5m). This was offset
by loan interest of £0.1m (2024: £0.9m) payable to Metlife and other interest payable of £Nil (2024: £0.3m),
which represents transactions costs released over the loan period. Further details for non-underlying
performance can be found in note 32 (page 61).
Balance Sheet
Net assets at 31 July 2025 amounted to £5.4m compared to £2.7m at 31 July 2024. The movement is due to the

Cash Flow
The Group generated a cash outflow from operating activities in the year of £2.1m (2024: £28.6m outflow), which
was largely contributed by a consistent debtor balance, as the Group completed its first season of the new
sponsorship & broadcasting agreements. After capital expenditure of £0.3m (2024: £1.1m), interest received of
£6.2m (2024: £6.5m), net loan repayments received from member clubs of £2.7m (2024: £19.8m) and £2.3m
(2024: £26.7m) of net cash outflow from financing activities (being external loan repayments), the Group’s cash
balances increased by £4.1m to £91.2m at 31 July 2025.

Financial risk management objectives and policies
The Group's activities expose it to a number of financial risks including credit risk, cash flow risk and liquidity
risk. The use of financial derivatives is governed by the board of directors. The Group does not use derivative
financial instruments for speculative purposes. ,
Cash flow risk
A key focus for the Group is clearly the availability of cash. The most material cash flow risk still faced by the
Group would be the inability to complete fixtures and therefore fulfil the rights committed to as part of our
broadcast and sponsorship contracts, due to another pandemic or global event. Due to the support provided by
its broadcast partners, upfront payments included within contracts, and the distribution model, which would react
to any significant reduction in income, the Group is able to mitigate the risks being faced.
The Group’s overseas broadcasting agreements expose it to the financial risks of changes in foreign currency
exchange rates. The Group uses foreign exchange forward contracts to hedge these exposures.

: Interest bearing assets are held at a fixed and variable rate. Interest bearing liabilities are held at a floating rate,
with the risk mitigated due to the interest costs being recharged to the relevant Clubs. The Group's activities do
not expose it to material financial risks of changes in interest rates.

7 Credit risk ;

The Group's principal financial assets are cash and trade and other receivables.
The Group's credit risk is primarily attributable to its trade receivables. The amounts presented in the Balance
Sheet are net of allowances for doubtful receivables. An allowance for impairment is made where there is an
identified loss event which, based on previous experience, is evidence of a reduction in the recoverability of the
cash flows.
The credit risk on loans to member Championship Clubs is managed through the assignment of Premier League
solidarity payable to those Clubs. Any further recovery on these loans or other Club loans can be deducted from _
EFL central distributions if required. Loans provided to previous members, where they were members of the EFL
but are now members of another league, are assessed for recovery based on Club financial projections.
The credit risk on liquid funds is limited because the counterparties are banks with high credit-ratings assigned
by international credit-rating agencies.
The Group has no significant concentration of credit risk, with exposure spread over a number of counterparties
and customers. Credit risk is also managed through a thorough due diligence process prior to completion of all
contract agreements in addition to a strict credit control policy.
Liquidity risk
To ensure that sufficient funds are available for ongoing operations and future developments, the company
maintains a strong invoicing and credit control policy. A decline in the EFL's Club distribution due to changes in
revenue, or failure to repay on agreed repayment dates could result in a breach of lending covenants. A breach :
of such covenants would result in the remaining facility being repaid immediately. The covenant headroom is
monitored each quarter and reported to the Board.

Going concern
The directors have a reasonable expectation that the Group and Company have adequate resources ‘to continue
in operational existence for at least the next 12 months to the end of December 2026.

with substantial broadcasting and sponsorship agreements secured for a number of seasons. The Group
maintains a strong liquidity position with net current assets of £26.2m (2024: £38.7m) and cash held of £91.2m
(2024: £87.1m) at the Balance Sheet date.
This assessment has been made by the Directors taking into account stress tests‘on the Group’s forecasts to
ensure it has the ability to cover cash requirements in the event of the loss of a large broadcast or sponsorship
partner and a partial Season due to events outside of the Group's control. This scenario is regarded as fairly
remote.

‘ The Group monitors its funding position and liquidity risk throughout the year to ensure it has access ta sufficient
funds to meet forecasted cash requirements. Historically and as evidenced in the primary statements, due to
upfront payments received from central broadcast providers and sponsors, the Group has always had sufficient
cash to meet its needs.

The Group continues to hold a debt facility agreed with MetLife on the 29th March 2021, which at the time,
supported the majority of Championship Clubs with PAYE liabilities. The external debt facility includes two
financial debt service ratio covenants measuring the debt service against both assigned Premier League and
: EFL distributions of participating Clubs and EFL distributable revenues. Both covenants have been assessed
based on the facility terms and forecasts over the 12 month going concern period and indicate that the Group will
remain comfortably in compliance with the covenants even after sensitivities. The outstanding balance of the
debt facility at the year-end was £964,743 (2024: £3,312,667) and at the point of signing the accounts is
£723,557 (2024: £1,443,147). Forecasted repayments for the next 12 months from the signing of these accounts
will result in a remaining balance of £241,185 (2024: £723,557), and the Group has forecasted cash reserves to
cover this balance if required. The Group has also considered several adverse financial scenarios on its own
cash flows. These reviews have included a stress test assumption on the loss of the majority of Sponsorship
income with the Group maintaining its committed Basic Award distributions to Clubs in full. In this scenario, the ;
Group was able to maintain a positive cash position for at least a 12-month period.
As a consequence, the directors believe that the Group is well placed to manage its business risks successfully.

Measures taken to improve energy efficiency -
: The EFL has continued to put in place measures to reduce its energy use.

To address transport-related energy use, the company has implemented a sustainable travel policy (and

: associate travel directory) to encourage public transport use, and continues to promote an employee salary
sacrifice scheme for electric vehicles. Video conferencing facilities in the company’s Preston and London offices
have been further integrated to minimise meeting travel.
To address energy use in the company's offices, the EFL has continued employee engagement activities to
promote energy saving behaviour and broader awareness of environmental sustainability themes.
Additional measures to support our network to consider its environmental impact
The EFL also works closely with its 72 Clubs to support energy efficiency and environmental action across the
League.
This financial year the EFL Green Clubs scheme was updated to introduce a new assessment cycle that awards
Clubs with a status of “Participant”, “Bronze”, “Silver” or “Gold”. There are 34 Clubs currently signed up to the ,
scheme, with 29 having moved to full assessment with scheme partners Green Code during the 2024/25 football
season. Of those 29, the current ratings include: 5 Participant, 11 Bronze, 11 Silver and 2 Gold clubs. Clubs who
received Gold status also achieved the full Green Codé Mark accreditation. ,
The scheme will move into a development season over the next financial year and no further assessments will
take place until the 2026/27 season. Instead, the EFL will work with participating EFL Green Clubs to identify key
priority areas for improvement and to begin to implement environmental sustainability measures and protocols,
such as having a published environmental policy and a baseline carbon footprint. The League will also deliver a
series of training programmes open to all 72 members clubs and reward best practice work through its annual
Awards ceremony.
The EFL continues to include environmental sustainability as a category in the annual EFL Awards. This year’s
EFL Green Club Award recognised Club initiatives to encourage fans and local communities to take
environmentally friendly actions. It was awarded to Northampton Town and their Community Trust for their
“Cobblers Tackle Food Waste project”, which aims to improve the community's understanding of how they can
play a part in reducing food waste across Northamptonshire.

2.18 Impairment of fixed assets and goodwill ;
Assets that are subject to depreciation or amortisation are assessed at each Balance Sheet date to
determine whether there is any indication that the assets are impaired. Where there is any indication
that an asset may be impaired, the carrying value of the asset (or cash-generating unit to which the
asset has been allocated) is tested for impairment. An impairment loss is recognised for the amount

. by which the asset's-carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. The recoverable amount is the

higher of an asset's (or CGU's) fair value less costs to sell and value in use. For the purposes of
assessing impairment, assets are grouped at the lowest levels for which there are separately
identifiable cash flows (CGUs). Non-financial assets that have been previously impaired are reviewed
at each Balance Sheet date to assess whether there is any indication that the impairment losses
recognised in prior periods may no longer exist or may have decreased.

value cannot be reliably determined, such investments are stated at historic cost less impairment.
